## Part XIV. Extension of sc⁺-multisections (HWZ Chapter 14, §§14.1–14.6)

**Decisions that shape the chapter.**

- *The boundary.* $\partial X$ is the set of objects with degeneracy index $d_X \ge 1$. It is closed, and saturated because the index is invariant along morphisms; sets open in $\partial X$ are written $O \cap \partial X$ with $O$ open in $X$. Good neighbourhoods are neighbourhoods of the object space, with the natural action of Part XIII, and $U(x) = V(x) \cap \partial X$.
- *Sc⁺ on faces through the charts of $X$.* "$s|\bar\theta_j$ is sc⁺" in Definition 14.1.3 (1) is read as: in every tame strong bundle chart of $W$, at every point of $U(x)$, the local representative of $s$ is sc⁺ on every local face germ $\{t_i = 0\}$ of the model, the condition of Part I's Definition 5.4.1 with $\partial X$ replaced by $U(x)$. In a tame chart the germs of the $\bar\theta_j$ at a point are exactly these local faces, so no sub-M-polyfold structure on the $\bar\theta_j$ is needed. §14.6 reads sc⁺ on a face $F$, and on a corner $F \cap F'$, in the same way.
- *The standing assumptions.* "A strong bundle over a tame ep-groupoid" supplies tame strong bundle charts; every strong bundle over a tame ep-groupoid has a tame strong bundle chart at every object (Chapter 10), so the results that name this hypothesis are applied with it discharged. "$X$ admits sc-smooth partitions of unity" is Definition 5.5.1 for the object M-polyfold. Tameness of $X$ and paracompactness of $|X|$ are assumed where a proof uses them; each result says which it uses.
- *Index sets.* Definition 14.1.3 asks $I_x \ne \emptyset$, which its factor $|I_x|^{-1}$ presumes. Hence every sc⁺-multisection over $\partial X$ charges every boundary fibre, the zero function is not one when $\partial X \ne \emptyset$, and Theorem 14.1.9 needs no charging hypothesis; charging is necessary for its conclusion.
- *Abstract steps, concrete instance.* Each step of §§14.3–14.5 is proved over a structure that names the data and the facts of the previous steps it uses (the z-loop setting, the boundary sections, the limit setting, the local extension data, the transport input, the assembly input, the criterion data), and then instantiated by the objects the construction produces from a structured representative. The final theorem takes no hypothesis on these structures.
- *z-loops with their arcs.* A z-loop and a z-arc along it are one inductive predicate, built by appending one morphism and one index step at a time. Concatenation, reversal, the action of $G_a$, the deformation of Proposition 14.4.6 and the transport of Proposition 14.5.4 are inductions on it.
- *Averaging.* Equivariance is obtained by averaging over the whole isotropy group, $\bar s_j(y) = |G_x|^{-1}\sum_g\mu(\Gamma(g^{-1}, g * y), s_{g(j)}(g * y))$, in Proposition 14.1.4 and in Proposition 14.4.14, instead of the text's averaging over a stabilizer and transport along an orbit.
- *Faces in §14.6* are Part XI's ep-faces $X_\theta$ (Definition 11.1.3). Theorem 14.6.3 reads the equality of classes with the equality of sections that p. 682 states (§XIV.28).

### XIV.1 Proposition 14.1.1, Definition 14.1.2 and the remark after it (2017: the discussion before Definition 14.1, Definition 14.1): good neighbourhoods of boundary points

**Faces at a point.** For $x \in \partial X$ and an open set $V \ni x$, *faces of $V$ at $x$* are sets $\theta_1, \dots, \theta_{d_X(x)}$ ⟦Chapter14/GoodBoundary · EpGroupoid.HasBoundaryFaces⟧ that are pairwise distinct connected components of $\{y \in V : d_X(y) = 1\}$, whose closures $\bar\theta_i = V \cap \operatorname{cl}\theta_i$ in $V$ contain $x$, and with the counting formula $d_X(y) = \#\{i : y \in \bar\theta_i\}$ for $y \in V$. The formula gives (4)(c) of the proposition, $V \cap \partial X = \bigcup_i \bar\theta_i$ ⟦Chapter14/GoodBoundary · EpGroupoid.HasBoundaryFaces.inter_boundary_eq⟧: a point of $V$ lies in $\partial X$ iff its count is positive.

**Proposition 14.1.1** (same) ⟦Chapter14/GoodBoundary · EpGroupoid.proposition_14_1_1⟧, p. 638. *Statement*: let $X$ be tame, $x \in \partial X$ and $W \ni x$ open. There is an open $V(x) \subset W$ with the natural $G_x$-action such that (1) $V(x)$ and $V(x) \cap \partial X$ are connected, (2) the natural action is defined on $V(x)$, (3) $t : s^{-1}(\operatorname{cl}_X V(x)) \to X$ is proper, and (4) there are faces $\theta_1, \dots, \theta_{d_X(x)}$ of $V(x)$ at $x$, with $V(x) \cap \partial X = \bigcup_i \bar\theta_i$. "Arbitrarily small" is read as "inside every open $W \ni x$". The 2017 text states this inside the discussion before Definition 14.1; the 2021 edition gives a proof in a tame chart.

*Proof.* The Lean proof is the relative form ⟦Chapter14/GoodBoundary · EpGroupoid.exists_isGoodBoundaryNbhd_le⟧: given a neighbourhood $N$ of $x$ with the natural action and $t$ proper on $s^{-1}(\operatorname{cl}N)$, and an open $W \ni x$, there is a good $V \subset N \cap W$ carrying the action of $N$. The proposition follows by taking $N$ from Part XIII (Theorem 7.1.13 with the properness of Lemma 13.1.2), and the relative form is what refinements of good systems need (§§XIV.4, XIV.8). It does not follow the text's chart computation; it uses Part I's abstract face calculus, which works for any function $d$ satisfying the corner axioms, and the degeneracy index of a tame M-polyfold does ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundary · MPolyfold.isCornerIndex_degIndex⟧.

1. *A neighbourhood all of whose open subsets are face-structured.* Part I provides an open $D \ni x$ such that every open subset of $D$ is a face-structured open set in the sense of Definition 2.7.6 ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundary · TameBoundary.exists_nbhd_forall_isFaceStructuredOpen⟧.
2. *Shrink inside $W \cap D$.* Restrict $N$ to an invariant neighbourhood $N' \subset N \cap W \cap D$ with the same action ⟦Chapter13/GoodSystem · EpGroupoid.NatNbhd.exists_restrict_subset⟧; the properness passes to subsets.
3. *The centred part.* Let $V_1 = \operatorname{reach}(N', x)$, the set of $y \in N'$ such that every face of $N'$ through $y$ contains $x$ ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundary · TameBoundary.reach⟧. It is open ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundary · TameBoundary.isOpen_reach⟧ and centred at $x$, every face of $V_1$ containing $x$ ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundary · TameBoundary.isCenteredAt_reach⟧. It is invariant under the natural action: each $\Phi_g$ is a homeomorphism of $N'$ preserving $d_X$ and fixing $x$, so it maps the faces through $y$ onto the faces through $g * y$ ⟦Chapter7/TameBoundaryEp · TameBoundary.IsDHomeoOn.facesThrough_image⟧, and the condition "every face through $y$ contains $x$" is carried to $g * y$. That the natural action preserves $d_X$ is ⟦Chapter14/GoodBoundary · EpGroupoid.NatNbhd.degIndex_Φ⟧.
4. *The component.* Let $V$ be the connected component of $x$ in $V_1$. It is open (components of open sets in the locally connected object space are open) and invariant: $\Phi_g(V)$ is connected, contains $\Phi_g(x) = x$, and lies in $V_1$, hence in $V$.
5. *$V$ is centred and face-structured.* The component of a centred set is centred ⟦Chapter14/GoodBoundary · TameBoundary.isCenteredAt_connectedComponentIn⟧, and the faces of $V$ are the traces of those of $V_1$: for $p \in V$, the component of $p$ in $V \cap \{d = 1\}$ is its component in $V_1 \cap \{d = 1\}$ ⟦Chapter14/GoodBoundary · TameBoundary.connectedComponentIn_inter_deg_of_mem_cc⟧. $V$ is face-structured by step 1.
6. *The faces $\theta_i$.* Since $V$ is centred and face-structured, the faces of $V$ through $x$ are $d_X(x)$ in number; enumerate them through an equivalence with $\{1, \dots, d_X(x)\}$ and let $\theta_i$ be the component of $V \cap \{d = 1\}$ at a chosen point of the $i$-th face. The counting formula is the face-structured property of $V$, and $x \in \bar\theta_i$ is centredness.
7. *Connectedness of $V \cap \partial X$.* By (4)(c), $V \cap \partial X = \bigcup_i\bar\theta_i$ (together with $x$); each face is preconnected ⟦Chapter14/GoodBoundary · TameBoundary.isPreconnected_faceAt⟧ and all contain $x$, so the union is preconnected.
8. *Properness.* $t$ is proper on $s^{-1}(\operatorname{cl}V)$ because $\operatorname{cl}V \subset \operatorname{cl}N$ is closed and $t$ is proper on $s^{-1}(\operatorname{cl}N)$ ⟦Chapter7/ProperMaps · isProperMap_domRestrict_of_subset_of_isClosed⟧.

The text's argument builds the set $Q$ in a tame chart with $\partial C \cap Q = \Theta$; the face calculus replaces it and gives the same four properties.

**Definition 14.1.2** (restated) ⟦Chapter14/GoodBoundary · EpGroupoid.IsGoodBoundaryNbhd⟧, p. 641. A neighbourhood $V(x)$ with the natural $G_x$-action is a *good open neighbourhood of $x$ in $X$* if (1) $V(x)$ and $V(x) \cap \partial X$ are connected, (3) $t$ is proper on $s^{-1}(\operatorname{cl}_X V(x))$, and (4) faces at $x$ exist; the *good open neighbourhood in $\partial X$* is $U(x) = V(x) \cap \partial X$ ⟦Chapter14/GoodBoundary · EpGroupoid.NatNbhd.bdry⟧. *Restated*: the text phrases the definition through $U(x)$; the formal one is on $V(x)$, of which $U(x)$ is the trace, because every later use (Definition 14.1.5, Proposition 14.1.4, §14.3) needs $V(x)$. "Admits the natural $G_x$-action" is read as carrying one, which is how Part XIII reads the same words. The remarks after the definition are proved: $U(x) = \bigcup_i\bar\theta_i$ ⟦Chapter14/GoodBoundary · EpGroupoid.IsGoodBoundaryNbhd.bdry_eq⟧; $U(x)$ is invariant under the natural action ⟦Chapter14/GoodBoundary · EpGroupoid.NatNbhd.mapsTo_Φ_bdry⟧, since the action preserves $d_X$; and only boundary points have good neighbourhoods ⟦Chapter14/GoodBoundary · EpGroupoid.IsGoodBoundaryNbhd.mem_boundary⟧: for $d_X(x) = 0$ the counting formula forces $V \cap \partial X = \emptyset$, which is not connected in the sense that requires non-emptiness. **The remark after Definition 14.1.2** (same) ⟦Chapter14/FaceTame · EpGroupoid.HasBoundaryFaces.face_isTame⟧, p. 641: for $X$ tame, the $\bar\theta_j$ are tame M-polyfolds. *Proof.* Fix $V$ open with faces $\theta_1, \dots, \theta_{d_X(x)}$ at $x$.

### XIV.3 Proposition 14.1.4 (2017: Remark 14.2): the local extension

**Proposition 14.1.4** (same) ⟦Chapter14/OpenExtension · SBLocalization.proposition_14_1_4_full⟧, p. 642. *Statement as formalized*: suppose every object lies in a tame strong bundle chart and the object M-polyfold admits sc-smooth partitions of unity. Let $(s_i)_{i \in I_x}$ be a symmetric sc⁺-section structure for some $\Lambda$ on $U(x) = V(x) \cap \partial X$. Then there are sc⁺-sections $\bar s_i$ on $V(x)$ with $\bar s_i = s_i$ on $U(x)$ and $\bar s_{g(i)}(g * y) = \mu(\Gamma(g, y), \bar s_i(y))$ for all $y \in V(x)$ (Definition 13.2.5 (1)). The two hypotheses are the text's standing assumptions. "$X$ admits sc-smooth partitions of unity" is Definition 5.5.1 for the object M-polyfold. The tame strong bundle charts hold for every strong bundle over a tame ep-groupoid: a strong bundle over a tame M-polyfold has a tame strong bundle chart at every point ⟦Chapter10/TameBundleCharts · StrongBundle.exists_isTameChart⟧, and so the bundle has one at every object ⟦Chapter10/TameBundleCharts · SEP.exists_isTameChart⟧. The paracompactness of $|X|$ and the goodness of $V(x)$ are not used. The text calls the $\bar s_i$ "sc⁺-multisections"; sc⁺-sections are meant, as its proof shows. *Witness of the hypotheses*: the trivial bundle $\mathbb R^n \times \mathbb R^k \to \mathbb R^n$ over the trivial ep-groupoid of $\mathbb R^n$ ⟦Chapter14/OpenExtension · SBLocalization.proposition_14_1_4_hypotheses_rn⟧, which has $\partial X = \emptyset$; the half-space bundle of §XIV.26 witnesses them with boundary.

*Proof.* The text extends each $s_i$ to an sc⁺-section on $V(x)$ "using an sc-smooth partition of unity argument" (Proposition 12.3.7 locally), averages over the stabilizer $H$ of an index, and transports the result along the orbit of the index. The Lean proof has the same two stages, with a different gluing and a different averaging.

*Stage 1: the non-equivariant extension.* For each $i$, find an sc⁺-section on $V(x)$ equal to $s_i$ on $V(x) \cap \partial X$ ⟦Chapter14/OpenExtension · SBLocalization.exists_scPlusSectionOn_extension_of_face⟧ (for any open $V$ and any face-wise sc⁺ $s$ on $V \cap \partial X$).

1. *The local step* ⟦Chapter14/LocalExtension · StrongBundle.exists_local_extension_of_face⟧. Let $y$ lie in a tame strong bundle chart $c$, let $G \ni y$ be open, and let $s$ be a section with values in $W_{0,1}$ on $G \cap \partial X$ that is sc⁺ on every local face germ at $y$ (for $y \notin \partial X$ there are none). Then there are an open $U$ with $y \in U \subset G$ and an sc⁺-section $\sigma$ of $W$ with $\sigma = s$ on $U \cap \partial X$. This is Part I's local step of the ∂-extension (Proposition 12.3.7 in a tame chart, Part XII) with the hypothesis made local: the face condition only at $y$, the section property only on $G \cap \partial X$. The proof applies the inclusion–exclusion construction at the faces through $\varphi(y)$ ⟦Chapter12/LocalBoundaryExtension · proposition_12_3_7_of_faces_at⟧, which gives an sc⁺ extension $e$ of the local representative on a neighbourhood $V'$ of $\varphi(y)$; restricts the chart to $S = \operatorname{dom} \cap \varphi^{-1}(V')$; takes an sc-smooth bump function $\beta$ at $y$ supported in $S \cap G$ ⟦Chapter5/BumpFunctions · proposition_5_5_3⟧; and sets $\sigma$ = the section cut off by $\beta$, $\beta \cdot e$ in the chart and $0$ outside, which is sc⁺ ⟦Chapter5/IndexAlongPathsLocalBump · isScPlusSection_cutoffSection_of_isChart⟧ and equals $s$ on the interior of $\{\beta = 1\}$ intersected with $S \cap G \cap \partial X$.
2. *Gluing over a set with closure inside $V$* ⟦Chapter14/LocalExtension · SBLocalization.exists_scPlusSection_extension_of_face⟧. Let $G$ be open with $\operatorname{cl}G \subset V$. Every object $y$ has a local piece: for $y \in \partial X \cap V$ the local extension of step 1 with $G' = V$; for $y \in \partial X \setminus V$, hence $y \notin \operatorname{cl}G$, the zero section on $X \setminus \operatorname{cl}G$; for $y \notin \partial X$ the zero section on $X \setminus \partial X$. These open sets cover $X$; an sc-smooth partition of unity subordinate to them glues the pieces to a global section ⟦Chapter12/ScPlusExtension · glueSection⟧, which is sc⁺ ⟦Chapter12/ScPlusExtension · isScPlusSection_glueSection⟧, and at a point of $G \cap \partial X$ every piece whose weight is non-zero there equals $s$, so the glued section does. A single partition of unity cannot give the extension on all of $V$: it would have to cover the frontier of $V$, where the face data may be unbounded and no local extension exists.
3. *Exhaustion.* The object space is metrizable. Let $G_n = \{y : \operatorname{dist}(y, X \setminus V) > 1/(n+1)\}$; then $\operatorname{cl}G_n \subset G_{n+1}$ and $\bigcup_n G_n = V$. By step 2, $\sigma_n$ is a global sc⁺-section equal to $s$ on $G_{n+1} \cap \partial X$.
4. *Cut-offs.* For each $n$ an sc-smooth $\chi_n : X \to [0, 1]$ equals $1$ on $\operatorname{cl}G_n$ and has support in $G_{n+1}$ ⟦Chapter14/OpenExtension · SBLocalization.exists_scSmooth_cutoff⟧ (a partition of unity for the two-set cover $\{G_{n+1}, X \setminus \operatorname{cl}G_n\}$). With $c_0 = 0$ and $c_{n+1} = \chi_n$, the weights $w_n = c_{n+1} - c_n$ vanish off $G_{n+1}$, are locally finite on $V$ (on $G_m$, $c_n = 1$ for $n > m$, so only $w_0, \dots, w_m$ are non-zero there), and sum to $1$ on $V$.
5. *The extension* is $\sum_n w_n\sigma_n$. On each $G_m$ it is a finite sum of sc⁺-sections times sc-smooth functions, hence sc⁺ there; being sc⁺ on an open set is local ⟦Chapter13/Commensurable · SBLocalization.IsScPlusSectionOn.of_locally⟧, so it is sc⁺ on $V$. At $y \in V \cap \partial X$, every $\sigma_n$ with $w_n(y) \ne 0$ has $y \in G_{n+1}$ and so $\sigma_n(y) = s(y)$; the weights sum to $1$.

*Stage 2: averaging.* Given sc⁺-sections $\sigma_k$ on $V$ (indexed by $I_x$), the average is ⟦Chapter14/BoundaryMultisection · SBLocalization.avgSection⟧
$$\bar s_j(y) = |G_x|^{-1}\sum_{g \in G_x}\mu\big(\Gamma(g^{-1}, g * y),\ \sigma_{g(j)}(g * y)\big),$$
the sum of the translates ⟦Chapter14/BoundaryMultisection · SBLocalization.avgTerm⟧ taken in the fibre over $y$. The morphism $\Gamma(g^{-1}, g * y)$ goes from $g * y$ to $y$ ⟦Chapter14/BoundaryMultisection · SBLocalization.src_Γ_inv_Φ⟧ ⟦Chapter14/BoundaryMultisection · SBLocalization.tgt_Γ_inv_Φ⟧, so each translate is a section over $V$ ⟦Chapter14/BoundaryMultisection · SBLocalization.P_avgTerm⟧. Each translate is sc⁺ on $V$ ⟦Chapter14/BoundaryMultisection · SBLocalization.isScPlusSectionOn_avgTerm⟧: $y \mapsto g * y$ is an sc-diffeomorphism of $V$ and $y \mapsto \Gamma(g^{-1}, g * y)$ an sc-smooth family of morphisms, along which the fibre action preserves sc⁺-sections (Part XIII). 1. *The key identity* ⟦Chapter14/BoundaryMultisection · SBLocalization.avgTerm_equivariant⟧: $\mu(\Gamma(h, y), \mathrm{term}_{gh, j}(y)) = \mathrm{term}_{g, h(j)}(h * y)$. Both sides are $\mu$ of a composite morphism applied to $\sigma_{gh(j)}(gh * y)$, and the two composites, $\Gamma(h, y) \circ \Gamma((gh)^{-1}, gh * y)$ and $\Gamma(g^{-1}, g * (h * y))$, are equal by the multiplicativity of the natural representation; the fibre action is functorial.

### XIV.11 Lemmas 14.3.6 and 14.3.7 (2017: Lemmas 14.4, 14.5): a locally finite cover of the boundary

**Closed saturated sets.** Four small facts about saturated sets are used throughout §§14.3–14.5. **Lemma 14.3.6** (explicit) ⟦Chapter14/BoundaryCover · EpGroupoid.lemma_14_3_6⟧, p. 654. *Statement*: let $|X|$ be paracompact, $B$ a set of objects with $|B|$ closed in $|X|$, and $\tilde U(x)$, for every object $x$, an open neighbourhood with the natural $G_x$-action. *Proof*, the text's.

1. *The cover.* The sets $|\tilde U(x)|$, $x \in B$, and $|X| \setminus |B|$ form an open cover of $|X|$, indexed by $B$ plus one extra index: $|\tilde U(x)| = \pi(\tilde U(x))$ is open by Proposition 7.1.8 (Part VII), and $|X| \setminus |B|$ is open because $|B|$ is closed. It covers: an orbit meeting $B$ at $x$ lies in $|\tilde U(x)|$.
2. *Refinement.* $|X|$ is paracompact, so the cover has a locally finite open refinement $(v_i)$ indexed by the same set, each $v_i$ inside the $i$-th member (precise refinement). A paracompact Hausdorff space is normal, and a point-finite open cover of a normal space can be shrunk to an open cover $(w_i)$ with $\operatorname{cl}w_i \subset v_i$; $(w_i)$ is again locally finite.
3. *The sets $\tilde V_a$.* For $a \in B$ put $\tilde V_a = \tilde U(a) \cap \pi^{-1}(w_a)$, and let $A$ be the set of $a \in B$ with $\tilde V_a \ne \emptyset$. Then $\tilde V_a$ is open, contained in $\tilde U(a)$, and invariant under the natural $G_a$-action, because $\tilde U(a)$ is and $\pi^{-1}(w_a)$ is saturated. Its image is $|\tilde V_a| = w_a$: $w_a \subset v_a \subset |\tilde U(a)|$, so every orbit in $w_a$ meets $\tilde U(a)$. So $|\tilde V_a|$ is open and $\operatorname{cl}|\tilde V_a| = \operatorname{cl}w_a \subset v_a \subset |\tilde U(a)|$.
4. *Local finiteness and cover.* $(|\tilde V_a|)_{a \in A}$ is a subfamily of $(w_i)$, re-indexed injectively, hence locally finite. A point $\pi(x)$ with $x \in B$ lies in some $w_i$; not in the extra one, since $\operatorname{cl}w_{\text{extra}} \subset |X| \setminus |B|$; so $\pi(x) \in w_a$ for some $a \in B$, and then $\tilde V_a \ne \emptyset$ and $a \in A$.

2. *Properness* ⟦Chapter14/BoundaryCover · EpGroupoid.exists_nhds_of_isProperSubset⟧. For $b \in o(y)$: every morphism $\varphi$ with $s(\varphi) = y$ and $t(\varphi) \in \operatorname{cl}_X\tilde U(b)$ lies in $\hat O_{a,b}$, because $y \in \tilde U'(a) \cap B$, $t(\varphi) \in \operatorname{cl}\tilde U(b) \subset \tilde U'(b)$, and $t(\varphi) \in B$ by saturation ⟦Chapter14/BoundaryCover · EpGroupoid.IsSaturated.tgt_mem⟧. The general lemma: if $Q$ is proper, $S$ open, and every $\varphi$ with $s(\varphi) = y$, $t(\varphi) \in Q$ lies in $S$, then the same holds for all $\varphi$ with $s(\varphi)$ in an open $W \ni y$. Its proof: the inverses of the bad morphisms, $\{\psi \in s^{-1}(Q) : \psi^{-1} \notin S\}$, form a closed subset $T$ of $s^{-1}(Q)$; by properness of $t$ on $s^{-1}(Q)$, $t(T)$ is closed; $y \notin t(T)$ by hypothesis; $W$ is the complement of $t(T)$. So each $b \in o(y)$ gives an open $W_b(y) \ni y$.
3. *$O_0(y)$.* $O_0(y) = \tilde U'(a) \cap \pi^{-1}(W(|y|)) \cap \bigcap_{b \in o(y)} W_b(y)$, a finite intersection of open sets containing $y$. It has (1), as $|O_0(y)| \subset W(|y|)$, and (3), by step 2 and $\tilde U(b) \subset \operatorname{cl}\tilde U(b)$.
4. *Symmetrization.* $\tilde O(y) = \bigcap_{g \in G_a}\{z \in \tilde U'(a) : g * z \in O_0(g * y)\}$. It is open (finite intersection, the actions continuous), contains $y$ (as $g * y \in O_0(g * y)$), lies in $O_0(y)$ (take $g = 1$), so keeps (1) and (3). For (2): $z \in \tilde O(y)$ iff $hz \in O_0(hy)$ for all $h$; then for $g * z$ and $g * y$, $h * (g * z) = (hg) * z \in O_0((hg) * y)$, so $g * \tilde O(y) \subset \tilde O(g * y)$, and the reverse inclusion is the same argument for $g^{-1}$, since $g^{-1} * (g * z) = z$ for the natural action.

**Definitions 14.4.2 and 14.4.3** (restated) ⟦Chapter14/ZLoops · EpGroupoid.ZSetup.Arc⟧, pp. 659–660. A z-loop with a z-arc from $i$ to $i'$ is $\mathrm{Arc}(a, a, i, i', 1_z)$: the composite $1_z$ fixes the start and the end at $z$. Every arc has $a, b \in A$, $s(\varphi) \in \hat V_a$ and $t(\varphi) \in \hat V_b$, by induction. *Restated*: the text's loop is a sequence and the arc a further sequence along it; the predicate records only what the later proofs use, the composite and the end indices.

*Concatenation* ⟦Chapter14/ZLoops · EpGroupoid.ZSetup.Arc.trans⟧: from $\mathrm{Arc}(a, b, i, j, \varphi)$ and $\mathrm{Arc}(b, c, j, k, \psi)$ with $s(\psi) = t(\varphi)$, $\mathrm{Arc}(a, c, i, k, \psi \circ \varphi)$. *Proof*: induction on the second arc; its start $1_{t(\varphi)}$ composes to $\varphi$, and each appended step is appended to the concatenation, by associativity. *Reversal* ⟦Chapter14/ZLoops · EpGroupoid.ZSetup.Arc.symm⟧: from $\mathrm{Arc}(a, b, i, j, \varphi)$, $\mathrm{Arc}(b, a, j, i, \varphi^{-1})$. *Proof*: induction; for an appended step $\psi$ with pair $(j, k)$ of $\hat\tau_{b,c}(\psi)$, the reversed chain starts with $\psi^{-1}$ from $\hat V_c$ to $\hat V_b$, whose correspondence is $\hat\tau_{c,b}(\psi^{-1}) = \hat\tau_{b,c}(\psi)^\sharp$ by *swap*, with the reversed pair $(k, j)$; it is followed by the reversal of the rest, and $(\psi \circ \varphi)^{-1} = \varphi^{-1} \circ \psi^{-1}$.

**Lemma 14.4.5** (explicit) ⟦Chapter14/ZLoops · EpGroupoid.ZSetup.lemma_14_4_5⟧, p. 660. *Statement*: for $a \in A$, $z \in \hat V_a$, $i, i' \in I_a$ and $g \in G_a$, $i \sim_z i'$ iff $g(i) \sim_{g*z} g(i')$; hence $[g(i)]_{\sim_{g*z}} = g([i]_{\sim_z})$ ⟦Chapter14/ZLoops · EpGroupoid.ZSetup.lemma_14_4_5_class⟧. *Explicit*: *diag* is a hypothesis of the setting. *Proof*, the text's ⟦Chapter14/ZLoops · EpGroupoid.ZSetup.zRel_smul⟧. Let $w = g * z$, which lies in $\hat V_a$ by invariance.

1. *The first step.* $\Gamma(g^{-1}, w)$ is a morphism from $w$ to $g^{-1} * w = z$, between points of $\hat V_a$, with correspondence $[\mathrm{id}, g^{-1}]$ by *diag*; its pairs are $(k, g^{-1}(k))$, so $(g(i), i)$ is one. Start at $w$ with index $g(i)$ and append this step: $\mathrm{Arc}(a, a, g(i), i, \Gamma(g^{-1}, w))$.
2. *The loop.* Concatenate the given $\mathrm{Arc}(a, a, i, i', 1_z)$: the composite is $\Gamma(g^{-1}, w)$ again.
3. *The last step.* Append $\Gamma(g, z)$ from $z$ to $w$, correspondence $[\mathrm{id}, g]$, pair $(i', g(i'))$. The composite is $\Gamma(g, z) \circ \Gamma(g^{-1}, w) = 1_w$, by the functoriality of the natural representation ($\Gamma(g, g^{-1} * w) \circ \Gamma(g^{-1}, w) = \Gamma(gg^{-1}, w)$). So $g(i) \sim_w g(i')$.
4. *The converse* applies this to $g^{-1}$ and $w$: $g^{-1}(g(i)) = i$, $g^{-1} * w = z$. The class identity follows from the equivalence at each $i'$.

2. *The good set.* Put $h = N(\Lambda'')$ and $K = \operatorname{dom-supp}(\Lambda'') = \operatorname{cl}\{h > 0\}$ (Definition 13.2.14 through the norm); $h = 0$ off $K$. $G = \{h < f\} \cup (X \setminus K)$ is open and saturated ($h$, $f$ functors; $K$ the closure of a saturated set).
3. *$\partial X \subset G$.* Let $x \in \partial X \cap K$. Over $x$, $\Lambda''$ and $\Lambda$ have the same support. If $x$ is in the boundary domain support, $h(x) = N(\Lambda)(x) < f(x)$. 8. *Over $\partial X$*, $\sigma = 1$, so $\Lambda' = \Lambda'' = \Lambda$.

### XIV.27 §14.6 and Definitions 14.6.1, 14.6.2 (2017: §14.6, Definitions 14.10, 14.11): structured sc⁺-multisections on the faces

§14.6, pp. 680–682, assumes $X$ tame and face-structured. Each face $F$ is saturated and a sub-M-polyfold, hence an ep-groupoid with the morphisms between its points. One is given a structured sc⁺-multisection on each $W|F$, compatible on the intersections of faces, and Theorem 14.6.3 glues them into one over $\partial X$, which then extends to $W$ by Theorem 14.1.9. This subsection treats the preamble of p. 680 and the two definitions; the next one treats the theorem.

**Faces.** The faces of §14.6 are Part XI's ep-faces $X_\theta$ (Definition 11.1.3, Part XI): for a face $\theta$ of the orbit space, the set of objects whose orbit lies in $\theta$. They are closed and saturated, and tame ep-groupoids with the morphisms between their points (Proposition 11.1.6, Part XI). The proof of Theorem 14.6.3 uses five properties of them near a boundary point, each proved separately.

1. *Faces consist of boundary points* ⟦Chapter14/FaceGerm · EpGroupoid.IsEpFace.subset_boundary⟧. *Proof*: $X_\theta$ is the preimage of the closure of a component of $\{d = 1\}$ in $|X|$, and since the orbit map is open (Proposition 7.1.8, Part VII), the preimage of the closure is the closure of the preimage. Let $y$ be in that closure and suppose $d(y) = 0$. Near $y$ the degeneracy index is at most $d(y)$ (Lemma 2.4.3, Part II, upper semicontinuity), so a point $y'$ of the component close to $y$ has $1 = d(y') \le d(y) = 0$, which is impossible. Hence $d(y) \ge 1$.
2. *Every boundary point lies on a face* ⟦Chapter14/FaceGerm · EpGroupoid.exists_isEpFace_mem⟧. *Proof*: face-structured means that $|x|$ lies on exactly $d_X(x)$ faces of $|X|$; for $x \in \partial X$ this number is at least $1$, so the set of faces through $|x|$ is non-empty, and the ep-face of any of them contains $x$. *And on finitely many* ⟦Chapter14/FaceGerm · EpGroupoid.finite_isEpFace_mem⟧: the faces of $|X|$ through $|x|$ number $d_X(x)$, and every ep-face through $x$ is the image of one of them.
3. *Near $x$, every face through a point contains $x$* ⟦Chapter14/FaceGerm · EpGroupoid.eventually_mem_of_isEpFace⟧. *Proof*: the degeneracy index of a tame M-polyfold is a corner index (Part II), so at $x$ there are local pieces: an open $B \ni x$ such that each face of $X$ through a point of $B$, read in $B$, is one of the $d(x)$ local pieces of $\{d = 1\}$ at $x$, each of which has $x$ in its closure (Proposition 2.7.5 (3), Part II). For $y \in B$ and $X_\theta \ni y$, the unique face of the object M-polyfold through $y$ inside $X_\theta$ is such a piece, so it contains $x$, and so does $X_\theta$.
4. *Faces are saturated* ⟦Chapter14/FaceAssembly · EpGroupoid.IsEpFace.tgt_mem_iff⟧: $s(\varphi) \in F$ iff $t(\varphi) \in F$, because $s(\varphi)$ and $t(\varphi)$ have the same orbit. Hence a face is invariant under every natural action at its points ⟦Chapter14/FaceAssembly · EpGroupoid.IsEpFace.Φ_mem⟧: $\Phi(g)(y) = t(\Gamma(g, y))$ and $s(\Gamma(g, y)) = y$. This is the text's $g * (F \cap U(x)) = F \cap U(x)$.
5. *A local face of a tame chart lies in one face* ⟦Chapter14/FaceGerm · EpGroupoid.exists_isEpFace_germ⟧: in a tame chart $\varphi$, if $t_i(\varphi(y)) = 0$, there are $\varepsilon > 0$ and a face $X_\theta$ such that every point $e'$ of the retract within $\varepsilon$ of $\varphi(y)$ with $t_i(e') = 0$ has $\varphi^{-1}(e') \in X_\theta$. *Proof*: by Part I's local face structure, there are $\varepsilon$ and a point $p$ of $\{d_O = 1\}$ such that, within $\varepsilon$, the face $\{t_i = 0\}$ of the retract lies in the closure of the component $S$ of $\{d_O = 1\}$ through $p$. In a tame chart the degeneracy index of $X$ is that of the model, so $\varphi^{-1}(S) \subset \{d_X = 1\}$; it is preconnected, as a continuous image of a preconnected set, and therefore lies in the component of $\{d_X = 1\}$ through $q = \varphi^{-1}(p)$. Let $\theta$ be the face of $|X|$ through $|q|$. A point $e'$ as above lies in $\operatorname{cl}S$, so $\varphi^{-1}(e')$ lies in the closure of $\varphi^{-1}(S)$, by continuity of $\varphi^{-1}$ within $S$, hence in the face of the object M-polyfold through $q$, whose image in $|X|$ lies in $\theta$.

A sixth fact concerns natural actions, not faces: *two natural representations of $G_x$ agree near $x$* ⟦Chapter14/FaceGerm · EpGroupoid.NaturalRep.eventually_Γ_eq⟧. For each $g$, $\Gamma(g, \cdot)$ and $\Gamma'(g, \cdot)$ are continuous with value $g$ at $x$, so near $x$ both lie in an open set around $g$ on which $s$ is injective; there $s(\Gamma(g, y)) = y = s(\Gamma'(g, y))$ gives $\Gamma(g, y) = \Gamma'(g, y)$. The natural actions agree as well ⟦Chapter14/FaceGerm · EpGroupoid.NaturalRep.eventually_Φ_eq⟧. This is why the text can speak of "the" natural action on $F \cap U(x)$: it does not depend on $U(x)$ near $x$.

**Sc⁺ relative to a face** (restated) ⟦Chapter14/FaceGerm · SBLocalization.IsFaceRelScPlusSectionOn⟧, p. 680. The text's sections of $W|F$ are sc⁺ for the induced M-polyfold structure of $F$. *As formalized*: $s$ is a section over $U \subset F$ with values in $W_{0,1}$, and in every tame strong bundle chart, at every $y \in U$, on every local face germ $\{t_i = 0\}$ at $\varphi(y)$ that lies in $F$ ⟦Chapter14/FaceGerm · SBLocalization.FaceGermIn⟧ (for some $\varepsilon > 0$, the points of the retract within $\varepsilon$ of $\varphi(y)$ with $t_i = 0$ map into $F$), the local representative is sc⁺ on that germ in the sense of Part I. *Reading*: the local face germs lying in $F$ are the local models of the sub-M-polyfold $F$ at its points (Theorem 2.5.17, Part II; Proposition 11.1.6, Part XI), so this is "$s|F$ is an sc⁺-section of $W|F$" read in the charts of $X$, as Definition 14.1.3 (1) is read (§XIV.2); only the values of $s$ on $F$ near $U$ enter. ### XIV.30 What the formalization taught us

**One statement of Chapter 14 does not hold as we read it with Definition 13.3.8 as printed**: Theorem 14.6.3, since that equivalence does not compare sections; with the equality of sections that its proof states on p. 682 it is proved (§XIV.28). Theorem 14.1.9 is proved as printed, and one statement has a printed range wider than its proof covers (Lemma 14.4.13 (2)). The rest is proved as printed, or in a stronger form.

**Two kinds of formal content.** Chapter 14 is the longest construction of the book, and what it needs splits in two.

The first kind is *local topology of the morphism space*: shrinking good neighbourhoods so that closures stay inside (Lemma 14.2.1, §XIV.8), separating the values of a locally constant correspondence by open sets (Lemma 14.3.1, §XIV.9), extending it by components (Proposition 14.3.4, §XIV.10), and choosing neighbourhoods compatible with the action and with local finiteness (Lemmas 14.3.6, 14.3.7, Proposition 14.4.10, §§XIV.11, XIV.18). All of it rests on three facts used again and again. First, $t$ is proper on $s^{-1}$ of the closure of a good neighbourhood, so a property of the morphisms from a point persists near it. Second, $s$ and $t$ are local homeomorphisms on sheets, so the sheet through a morphism is locally constant. Third, a locally constant function on an open set is constant on its components. The diagonal property of Proposition 14.3.4, for which the text gives no argument, is the second fact applied through sheet labels: the component of $\Gamma(g, y)$ stays in the sheet of $g$ and reaches the boundary there (§XIV.10). The inversion symmetry of the extended correspondences, which the text does not state, costs one more intersection in Lemma 14.3.1 and is what the z-loops of §14.4 need to be reversible (§§XIV.9, XIV.14).

The second kind is *combinatorics of index sets along chains of morphisms*. Encoding a z-loop together with its arc as one inductive predicate, built by appending one morphism and one index step at a time, made the equivalence-relation properties of $\sim_z$, which the text asserts, and Lemma 14.4.5 short (§XIV.14). It made Proposition 14.4.6 (1) a deformation of the chain along sheets, one morphism at a time, without Lemma 14.4.7 (§XIV.15). It carried the limit argument of Proposition 14.4.6 (2): with Lemma 14.4.7 around the finitely many isomorphs of the limit point, each step of a nearby loop moves along its own sheet to the boundary, so the text's limit loop need not be built, and the result holds for loops at points of $\hat V_a$, the form Lemma 14.4.9 (3) uses (§XIV.16). It also carried Proposition 14.5.4: defining the transported section through chains rather than single morphisms gives its equivariance and its compatibility with $\hat\tau$ directly, and its well-definedness is the text's closing of two chains into a loop (§XIV.23).

**Steps that needed less than printed.**

- *Theorem 14.5.5* needs neither its properness nor its closure condition: local transports into the sets $V_a$ and the conjugation homomorphisms of Theorem 13.5.3 (Part XIII) carry the structure (§XIV.24).
- *Lemma 14.5.1* does not use tameness, and (14.53) holds for the cover of §14.3 itself, without the shrinking the text performs: a morphism from an invariant subset of a natural neighbourhood to a point of its closure ends in that subset, because the isotropy group is finite and the morphism space Hausdorff (§XIV.21).
- *Lemma 14.5.2* enters the proof of Theorem 14.1.9 only through its identity for the summed sections; its multisection form is proved on its own (§XIV.22).
- *Proposition 14.1.8* holds along generalized strong bundle isomorphisms and their classes without the paracompactness of the orbit spaces (§XIV.6).
- *Definition 14.4.11 (2)* is not needed once Proposition 14.4.14 is proved by averaging (§§XIV.18, XIV.20).
- *Proposition 14.4.6 (1)* does not need Lemma 14.4.7 (§XIV.15).

**Steps that needed more, or a different argument.**

- *Proposition 14.1.4.* The local extension cannot be glued with one partition of unity of $X$, because the boundary data on an open $V(x)$ may be unbounded near its frontier. An exhaustion of $V(x)$ by the sets at distance $> 1/n$ from its complement, glued by sc-smooth cut-offs, does it (§XIV.3).
- *Averaging.* Averaging over the whole isotropy group, $\bar s_j(y) = |G_x|^{-1}\sum_g \mu(\Gamma(g^{-1}, g * y), s_{g(j)}(g * y))$, replaces the text's average over a stabilizer together with transport along an orbit, both in Proposition 14.1.4 and in Proposition 14.4.14. In Proposition 14.4.14 it also replaces the class-by-class, orbit-by-orbit construction with an invariant partition of unity (§§XIV.3, XIV.20).
- *Theorem 14.1.6* is proved from tameness of $X$ and paracompactness of $|X|$, which its proof uses, in place of the paracompactness of $|\partial X|$ printed in the statement (§XIV.4).
- *The size control of Theorem 14.1.9* hides one choice: the extension must be taken with support in $\{f > 0\}$, not merely in $\tilde U$, or the cut-off cannot reach the boundary points where $f = 0$; and the inequality $N(\Lambda) < f$ is needed, and assumed, on the boundary domain support rather than on all of $\partial X$ (§§XIV.7, XIV.25).
- *Proposition 14.5.4* needs one property of the cut-offs that its statement does not list, that $\beta_a$ vanishes off the saturation of $\hat V_a$; Lemma 14.5.1 (1) gives it (§XIV.23).
- *Index sets on two domains.* The index sets of §14.1 live on boundary points and those of §14.4 on all objects; a map to boundary points that is the identity on $\partial X$, with the identity on morphisms as a homomorphism of isotropy groups, joins them (§XIV.13).
- *Proposition 14.1.8 over $\partial X$* carries everything along except Definition 14.1.3 (1), which is read in every tame strong bundle chart; its transport needs that tame charts compose with local strong bundle isomorphisms, a fact Part I proved for Theorem 3.6.8 (§XIV.6).
- *Sc⁺ on corners.* The restriction of Definition 14.6.1 is sc⁺ on the corner $F \cap F'$ because, in a tame model, the corner germ $\{t_i = t_j = 0\}$ is itself an sc-retract by Part I's corner retraction, and an sc⁺ map on a face germ restricts to it (§XIV.27).

**Index sets and charging.** Definition 14.1.3 (3) writes $\Lambda(w) = |I_x|^{-1}\cdot\#\{i : s^x_i(P(w)) = w\}$, which presumes $I_x \ne \emptyset$, and the formal definition asks for it. Without it the zero function, with empty index sets, would count as structurable over $\partial X$, while no structurable extension vanishes over a boundary fibre; witnesses on spaces without boundary cannot show this, since every condition on $\partial X$ is then vacuous. With it, every sc⁺-multisection over $\partial X$ charges the boundary, and charging, which the conclusion of Theorem 14.1.9 requires, holds automatically (§§XIV.2, XIV.7). Two lessons for formalization follow: a statement on $\partial X$ needs a witness with boundary, which the half-space bundle provides (§XIV.26); and a statement about structurable functors should be checked against the zero function.

**§14.6.** The step from compatible classes to agreeing representatives is where the text is shortest. Definition 13.3.8 compares $\Lambda$, the index sets and the correspondences, not the sections, and with it alone Theorem 14.6.3 does not hold as we read it: on the quadrant $[0,\infty)^2 \times \mathbb R$, two pairs of sections with equivalent restrictions to the corner, but differing index by index, form a compatible assignment that no sc⁺-multisection over $\partial X$ glues. With the equality of sections that p. 682 states built into the equivalence, the theorem holds with the text's hypotheses, also for index families that differ from face to face (§XIV.28). Everything else in §14.6 is local: faces are closed and saturated, every boundary point lies on finitely many, near a point every face through a nearby point contains it, and a local face of a tame chart lies in one face (§XIV.27). These four facts, with the gluing criterion for sc⁺ on faces, are the whole proof of the gluing.

**How the chapter is used later.** Theorem 14.1.9 is the tool by which the transversality results of Chapter 15 and the cobordism results of Chapter 16 control a multisection near the boundary. Theorem 15.3.9 (Part XV) uses it; the cut-off step of its proof uses the extension of face-wise sc⁺ sections of Proposition 14.1.4 (§XIV.3) and Definition 14.1.3 (1) read through the charts (§XIV.2). Theorem 16.5.7 (Part XVI) uses it in one representative of a strong polyfold bundle, and the transport between representatives is Proposition 14.1.8 over $\partial X$ (§XIV.6). What these later chapters need from Chapter 14 is proved here: the theorem, the transport over $\partial X$, and the half-space witness with boundary.

**A note on how the proof is organized.** The text proves Theorem 14.1.9 in one pass, introducing the objects of each section from those of the previous one. The formalization splits each step into an abstract statement over a structure that names its inputs, and an instance built from the objects of the construction (§§XIV.13, XIV.25). The split made three things visible. First, which properties of the previous objects each step actually uses: Theorem 14.5.5 uses neither properness nor closures, Lemma 14.5.1 not tameness, Proposition 14.4.14 not Definition 14.4.11 (2). Second, where a step uses a property its statement does not list: Proposition 14.5.4 and the support of the cut-offs. Third, where each standing hypothesis enters: paracompactness of $|X|$ through the good systems of Theorem 14.1.6 and Part XIII and through the size control; tameness through Proposition 14.1.1 and the faces; the sc-smooth partitions of unity and the tame strong bundle charts through the local extensions of Propositions 14.1.4 and 14.4.14, the charts also through Definition 14.1.3 (1) and its transport (§XIV.6). Each of these is named in the subsection where it is used.
