## Part IV. Manifolds with boundary with corners (HWZ Chapter 4, §§4.1–4.4)

Chapter 4 asks when an M-polyfold — a space glued from images of sc-smooth retractions — is secretly an ordinary manifold with boundary with corners, and why the fibres of a family of such manifolds are good for almost every parameter. The pivot is the *sc⁺ condition*: a retraction that gains a level. At a smooth point its derivative is compact, so its retract has finite-dimensional tangent spaces (Lemma 4.1.7 (preprint 4.7)), and near a point where the tangent space is in good position to the partial quadrant the retract is the graph of a map over that tangent space (Proposition 4.1.9 (preprint 4.9)). Theorem 4.1.2 (preprint 4.2) characterizes the manifolds with corners among M-polyfolds by these two properties; Theorem 4.2.7 (preprint 4.18) applies the characterization to submanifolds in good position; Theorem 4.2.11 (preprint 4.22) describes a submanifold near a point of general position; Theorem 4.3.1 (preprint 4.23) combines that description with Sard's theorem on the strata of the corners. This Part retells: finite-dimensional sc-spaces and corner charts (§IV.1); sc⁺-maps (§IV.2); sc⁺-retractions, Lemmas 4.1.5 and 4.1.7 (preprint 4.5 and 4.7) (§IV.3); Proposition 4.1.8 (preprint 4.8) with its chart form and Remark 4.1.3 (preprint 4.3) (§IV.4); charts centred at a smooth point, which the text uses and never constructs (§IV.5); Proposition 4.1.9 (preprint 4.9) with Lemmas 4.1.12 and 4.1.13 (preprint 4.11 and 4.12), by a direct proof (§§IV.6–IV.9); Theorem 4.1.2 (preprint 4.2) (§IV.10); smooth finite-dimensional submanifolds and Remark 4.2.2 (preprint 4.14) (§IV.11); Proposition 4.2.3 (preprint 4.15) (§IV.12); good and general position in charts and their chart independence (§IV.13); Lemma 4.2.8 (preprint 4.19) and Theorem 4.2.7 (preprint 4.18) (§IV.14); Remark 4.2.9 (preprint 4.20) (§IV.15); the linear algebra at the corner of a centred chart (§IV.16); Theorem 4.2.11 (preprint 4.22) (§§IV.17–IV.18) and Remark 4.3.2 (preprint 4.24) (§IV.19); Sard's theorem (§IV.20); the finite-dimensional core of Theorem 4.3.1 (preprint 4.23) (§§IV.21–IV.22); the fibre over a regular value (§IV.23); Theorem 4.3.1 (preprint 4.23) (§IV.24); what is not formalized (§IV.25); and what the formalization taught us (§IV.26).

**Five structural choices.** (1) *sc⁺ is a property of maps.* The text defines it for retractions and operators and uses it for maps; here a map is sc⁺ when its lift into $F^1$ is sc-smooth, and the calculus of such maps is proved first (§IV.2). (2) *Tangent spaces of $X$ are read in charts.* $T_xX$ carries no linear structure in the formalization (gap G.7 of Chapter 2), so $T_aA$, good position, general position and stratified regularity are stated in a chart, and — except for stratified regularity — proved independent of the chart (§IV.13). (3) *Centred charts are re-centred charts.* A chart with $\psi(x) = 0$ is the chart $\varphi - \varphi(x)$ on the set where the partial quadrant $C$ agrees with $C_{\varphi(x)}$, which exists only at smooth points (§IV.5). (4) *Proposition 4.1.9 (preprint 4.9) on fixed sets.* The sc-diffeomorphism $p : O \cap W \to V$ is proved with $\Gamma = p^{-1}$ sc-smooth on a *fixed* relatively open $V$, by contraction arguments on level $1$, a frame formula for $D\Gamma$ and a $C^k$-bootstrap along the finite-dimensional tangent space. (5) *Stratified Sard.* Sard's theorem is applied stratum by stratum in the chart of Theorem 4.2.11 (preprint 4.22), and the null sets of critical values of countably many charts are collected.

### IV.1 Finite-dimensional sc-spaces and corner charts (Definition 4.1.1 (preprint 4.1))

**Finite-dimensional sc-spaces.** Definition 4.1.1 (preprint 4.1) models its charts on finite-dimensional spaces "with the constant sc-structure"; the text remarks after Definition 1.1.1 (preprint 1.1) that a finite-dimensional space has only that one. This is proved. If $E_0$ is finite-dimensional, every level $E_m$ is, since it injects into $E_0$ ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · ScBanach.finiteDimensional_lvl⟧. The range of $E_m \to E_0$ is then a finite-dimensional, hence closed, subspace; it contains the smooth points, which are dense (Definition 1.1.1 (2) (preprint 1.1)); so it is all of $E_0$ ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · ScBanach.toBase_surjective⟧. The inclusions are therefore bijective, and they form an sc-isomorphism of $E$ onto $E_0$ with the constant structure ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · ScBanach.scIsoConst⟧; in particular every point of $E_0$ is smooth ⟦Chapter4/Characterization · ScBanach.mem_smooth_of_finiteDimensional⟧. The density axiom is what makes this work: $E_0 = \mathbb R$, $E_m = 0$ for $m \ge 1$ has compact injective inclusions and levels that differ (Findings, Chapter 4, item 6).

**Definition 4.1.1 (preprint 4.1).** A chart of $X$ is a *corner chart* if its local model is $(U, C, V)$ with $V$ a finite-dimensional real normed space carrying the constant sc-structure, $C$ a partial quadrant in $V$, $U$ relatively open in $C$, and the identity retraction ⟦Chapter4/Characterization · MChart.IsCornerChart⟧. Its model is then relatively open in a partial quadrant ⟦Chapter4/Characterization · MChart.IsCornerChart.isRelOpenInQuadrant⟧, finite-dimensional ⟦Chapter4/Characterization · MChart.IsCornerChart.finiteDimensional⟧, a tame retract — the identity retraction is tame ⟦Chapter2/Tame · ScRetraction.id_isTame⟧ ⟦Chapter4/Characterization · MChart.IsCornerChart.isTameRetract⟧ — and an sc⁺-retract by Remark 4.1.3 (preprint 4.3) (§IV.4) ⟦Chapter4/Characterization · MChart.IsCornerChart.isScPlusRetract⟧; every point of its model space is smooth ⟦Chapter4/Characterization · MChart.IsCornerChart.mem_smooth⟧. $X$ *has a corner structure* if it has an atlas of corner charts equivalent to its own atlas ⟦Chapter4/Characterization · MPolyfold.HasCornerStructure⟧. The dimension of $V$ may vary from chart to chart, as in the text. The text's "sc-smoothly compatible charts", "$\varphi$ an sc-diffeomorphism", is read as compatibility with the atlas of $X$, as for tameness (Definition 2.5.7 (preprint 2.37)).

*The definition is local* ⟦Chapter4/Characterization · MPolyfold.hasCornerStructure_iff⟧: $X$ has a corner structure iff every point lies in a corner chart that is a chart of the M-polyfold structure. One direction takes a chart of the equivalent atlas through the point. For the other, the corner charts that are charts of $X$ form an atlas: they cover $X$ by hypothesis, any two charts of $X$ are compatible ⟦Chapter2/MPolyfold · MPolyfold.IsChart.compatible⟧, and the atlas is equivalent to that of $X$ because each of its members is a chart of $X$. *A corner structure makes $X$ tame* ⟦Chapter4/Characterization · MPolyfold.HasCornerStructure.isTame⟧: the same atlas is an atlas of tame charts.

**The transition maps are $C^\infty$.** For a chart whose model is relatively open in a partial quadrant, every overlap $\chi(V \cap V')$ is relatively open ⟦Chapter4/Characterization · MChart.isRelOpenInQuadrant_overlap⟧. For compatible corner charts, the transition map is sc-smooth between the overlap retracts; composed with the identity retraction of the overlap it is sc-smooth on a relatively open subset of a partial quadrant in a finite-dimensional space with the constant structure, and such maps are exactly the $C^\infty$ maps with derivatives within the set ⟦Chapter1/ExpositionLemmas · isScSmooth_iff_contDiffOn_const⟧ ⟦Chapter4/Characterization · MChart.IsCornerChart.contDiffOn_transition⟧. That is the text's "classically smooth"; "the atlas defines the structure of a smooth manifold with boundary with corners" is not formalized beyond it, because Mathlib's manifolds with corners have a single model space and here the partial quadrants vary from chart to chart.

**Arbitrary finite-dimensional models.** The constant structure is no restriction. A partial quadrant of a finite-dimensional $E$ is one of $E_0$ with the constant structure ⟦Chapter4/Characterization · IsPartialQuadrant.toConst⟧ ⟦Chapter4/Characterization · IsRelOpenInQuadrant.toConst⟧, and the identity of $E_0$ is an sc-diffeomorphism between the two models ⟦Chapter4/Characterization · IsRelOpenInQuadrant.isScDiffeoQuadrant_toConst⟧. Composing a chart with it (the construction $\mathtt{compDiffeo}$ of Chapter 3) ⟦Chapter3/GlobalIFTCenter · MChart.compDiffeo⟧ gives, with the same map and domain, a corner chart of the same dimension ⟦Chapter4/Characterization · MChart.exists_isCornerChart_of_finiteDimensional⟧. Hence $X$ has a corner structure iff it has an equivalent atlas of charts onto relatively open subsets of partial quadrants in sc-Banach spaces with finite-dimensional $E_0$ ⟦Chapter4/Characterization · MPolyfold.hasCornerStructure_iff_finiteDimensional⟧ — verbatim the definition of the manifolds with corners of locally constant dimension of Theorem 3.2.3 (preprint 3.27) (§III.36) — and the manifolds with corners of dimension $n$ of Theorem 3.2.3 (preprint 3.27) are the corner structures all of whose charts have dimension $n$ ⟦Chapter4/Characterization · MPolyfold.isManifoldWithCorners_iff⟧ ⟦Chapter4/Characterization · MPolyfold.IsManifoldWithCorners.hasCornerStructure⟧.

### IV.2 Sc⁺-maps

The text uses "sc⁺-map" in the proof of Lemma 4.1.7 (preprint 4.7) and composes sc⁺-retractions with sc-smooth maps in Lemma 4.1.5 (preprint 4.5), but defines sc⁺ only for retractions (Definition 4.1.4 (preprint 4.4)) and operators (Definition 1.1.5 (preprint 1.4)). The formalization defines it for maps on any set, as the neighbouring notions of the project are (sc⁺-operators are sc-operators into $F^1$, sc⁺-sections sc-smooth maps into $K[1]$).

**The lift.** For $x \in E_1 \subseteq E_0$, $1_0^1 x$ is the point of $E_1$ over $x$, with a junk value off $E_1$ ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · ScBanach.liftOne⟧ ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · ScBanach.toBase_liftOne⟧ ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · ScBanach.liftOne_toBase⟧.

**Definition.** $f$ is *sc⁺ on $U$* if $f(U) \subseteq F_1$ and the lift $x \mapsto 1_0^1 f(x)$, a map $U \to F^1$, is sc-smooth ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · IsScPlus⟧. Then $f(U_m) \subseteq F_{m+1}$: the lift is sc⁰, so it maps $U_m$ into $(F^1)_m = F_{m+1}$ ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · IsScPlus.mem_range_succ⟧. On an sc-domain the canonical lift can be replaced by any sc-smooth $g : U \to F^1$ with $\iota \circ g = f$, which is how the text phrases it ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · IsScPlus.iff_exists⟧. The calculus: an sc⁺-map is sc-smooth, being $\iota \circ$ (lift) with the sc-operator $\iota : F^1 \to F$ ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · IsScPlus.isScSmooth⟧; the property depends only on the values on $U$ ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · IsScPlus.congr⟧ and passes to smaller sc-domains ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · IsScPlus.mono⟧; *sc⁺ ∘ sc-smooth is sc⁺*, the lift of $g \circ f$ being (lift of $g$) $\circ f$, sc-smooth by the chain rule ⟦Chapter1/ChainRule · IsScSmooth.comp⟧ ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · IsScPlus.comp_isScSmooth⟧; *sc-smooth ∘ sc⁺ is sc⁺*, the lift of $g \circ f$ being $g^1 \circ$ (lift of $f$) with $g^1 : V^1 \to G^1$ the map $g$ induces on level $1$, sc-smooth by Proposition 1.2.2 (preprint 1.17) ⟦Chapter1/Levels · IsScSmooth.shift⟧ ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · IsScSmooth.comp_isScPlus⟧; and an sc⁺-operator is an sc⁺-map ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · ScPlus.isScPlus⟧.

**The derivative at a smooth point** ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · IsScPlus.exists_scPlus_deriv⟧. Let $f$ be sc⁺ on an sc-domain $U$ and $x \in U_1$ smooth. The lift $g$ is sc-smooth into $F^1$, so its derivative at the smooth point $x$ is an sc-operator $E \to F^1$ (the remark after Proposition 1.2.1 (preprint 1.16)), that is an sc⁺-operator $S : E \to F$. Since $f = \iota \circ g$ on $U$, the chain rule and the uniqueness of the derivative on an sc-domain give $Df(x) = \iota \circ Dg(x)$, the level-$0$ operator of $S$. This is the text's step "$Dr(o)$ is a sc⁺-operator, since $r$ is a sc⁺-map". Consequently $Df(x)$ is compact, and so is every level operator of $S$ ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · IsScPlus.isCompactOperator_deriv⟧.

**A compact projection has finite-dimensional range** ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · finiteDimensional_range_of_isCompactOperator_of_idem⟧: if $P = P \circ P$ is compact, its range is the closed set $\{v : Pv = v\}$, $P$ restricts to a compact operator of the range which is the identity, and a normed space with compact identity is finite-dimensional (Riesz). The text takes this for granted.

### IV.3 Sc⁺-retractions and sc⁺-retracts (Definitions 4.1.4, 4.1.6 (preprint 4.4, 4.6), Lemmas 4.1.5, 4.1.7 (preprint 4.5, 4.7))

**Definition 4.1.4 (preprint 4.4).** An sc-smooth retraction $r : U \to U$ is an *sc⁺-retraction* if it is an sc⁺-map on $U$ ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · ScRetraction.IsScPlus⟧. The text's condition $r(U_m) \subseteq U_{m+1}$ is then a consequence, not a hypothesis ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · ScRetraction.IsScPlus.mem_lvlSet_succ⟧. On an open subset $V$ of an M-polyfold, an sc⁺-retraction is a map $s$, sc-smooth on $V$, with $s(V) \subseteq V$, $s \circ s = s$ on $V$, $s(V) \subseteq X_1$, and $1_0^1 \circ s : V \to X^1$ sc-smooth ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · MPolyfold.IsScPlusRetractionOn⟧; the junk value of $1_0^1$ off $X_1$ is quantified over and plays no role.

**Lemma 4.1.5 (preprint 4.5)** ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · lemma_4_5⟧. *If $t$ is an sc⁺-retraction onto $O$ and $s$ an sc-smooth retraction (of a possibly different relatively open set) onto $O$, then $s$ is sc⁺.* The text's proof: $s$ maps its domain into $O$, which lies in the domain of $t$, and $t$ fixes $O$, so $s = t \circ s$, which is sc⁺ ∘ sc-smooth. (The text writes "$s(V) = 0$" for $s(V) = O$.)

**Definition 4.1.6 (preprint 4.6).** $(O, C, E)$ is an *sc⁺-retract* if it is the retract of an sc⁺-retraction ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · IsScPlusRetract⟧; it is then a retract ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · IsScPlusRetract.isScRetract⟧, every sc-smooth retraction onto it is sc⁺ by Lemma 4.1.5 (preprint 4.5) ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · IsScPlusRetract.isScPlus⟧, and so the property is intrinsic ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · isScPlusRetract_iff_forall⟧.

**Lemma 4.1.7 (preprint 4.7)** (i) ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · lemma_4_7_i⟧. *The retract of an sc⁺-retraction consists of smooth points.* By induction on $m$: a point $y \in O$ lies in $E_m$; then $y \in U_m$, and $y = r(y) \in E_{m+1}$ by the level gain. (ii) ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · lemma_4_7_ii⟧. *At a level-$1$ point $o$ over a point of $O$, $Dr(o)E_0$ is finite-dimensional.* $o$ is smooth by (i), so $Dr(o)$ is compact (§IV.2); it is a projection because $r \circ r = r$ gives $Dr(o) \circ Dr(o) = Dr(o)$ at points of $O$ ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · ScRetraction.scDeriv_comp_self⟧; and a compact projection has finite-dimensional range. In the form with the tangent space of Definition 2.3.12 (preprint 2.17) ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · lemma_4_7_ii_tangentSpace⟧: every $o \in O$ has a smooth lift $o_1 \in E_1$ ⟦Chapter2/TameRetracts · exists_smoothLvl_one_of_mem_smooth⟧, and $T_{o_1}O$ is the range of $Dr(o_1)$ ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · tangentSpace_eq_coe_submodule⟧, finite-dimensional. Tangent spaces are indexed by level-$1$ points, which is why the statement mentions the lift.

### IV.5 Charts centred at a smooth point

Theorem 4.1.2 (ii) (preprint 4.2) asks for charts with $\psi(x) = 0$, the proofs of Theorems 4.2.7 and 4.2.11 (preprint 4.18 and 4.22) and of Lemma 5.3.3 (preprint 5.11) use them, and the text never says where they come from. Translating a chart $\varphi : V \to O \subseteq C$ by $e = \varphi(x)$ fails twice: translation by $e$ preserves every level only if $e$ is smooth, and $C - e$ is not a partial quadrant unless $e \in C \cap (-C)$. The construction (Findings, items 1, 2) uses the partial quadrant $C_e$ and the neighbourhood on which it agrees with $C$.

### IV.7 Lemmas 4.1.12 and 4.1.13 (preprint 4.11 and 4.12): uniqueness and existence on level 1

**Lemma 4.1.12 (preprint 4.11), the contraction estimate** ⟦Chapter4/ScPlusGraph · GraphData.exists_contraction_radius⟧. **Lemma 4.1.13 (preprint 4.12), existence by a contraction** ⟦Chapter4/ScPlusGraph · GraphData.exists_graph_point⟧. 2. For $y \in B_a$, $a + y \in C_1$ by the good complement property, $|y|_0 \le \varepsilon|a|_0$ coming from $|y|_1 \le \varepsilon'|a|_1$; and $|a + y|_1 \le (1 + \varepsilon')|a|_1 < r'$. So $a + y$ lies in $U_1$, where $t$ is defined — a point the text does not check.

### IV.8 The step $p(O \cap W) \subseteq C$, and the neighbourhoods

**$p$ maps points of $O$ near $0$ into $C$** ⟦Chapter4/ScPlusGraph · GraphData.p_mem_C⟧, which the conclusion "$p : U_0 \to V \subseteq T_0O \cap C$" needs and the text never checks. ### IV.16 Linear algebra at the corner of a centred chart; Lemmas 7.A and 7.B of the exposition

In a chart centred at $0$, every coordinate vanishes at $0$, so the minimal subspace there is $E^{\min}_0 = \{\lambda = 0\}$, the text's "$\{0\} \oplus W$" ⟦Chapter4/LocalStructure · mem_minimalSubspaceWith_zero⟧, and it lies in every $E_x$ ⟦Chapter4/LocalStructure · minimalSubspaceWith_zero_le⟧. **Good position from $N + E^{\min}_0 = E_0$.** Let $N$ be finite-dimensional with $N + E^{\min}_0 = E_0$. ### IV.20 Sard's theorem, vendored

- *The objects.* $\mathrm{Crit} = \{x \in U : Df(x)$ not onto$\}$ ⟦Vendor/MilnorSard/Basic · MilnorSard.Sard.critSet⟧, and $C_k$, the points where all derivatives of orders $1, \dots, k$ vanish ⟦Vendor/MilnorSard/Basic · MilnorSard.Sard.vanishSet⟧, with $\mathrm{Crit} \subseteq (\mathrm{Crit} \setminus C_1) \cup \bigcup_{1 \le i < k}(C_i \setminus C_{i+1}) \cup C_k$ ⟦Vendor/MilnorSard/Basic · MilnorSard.Sard.critSet_subset_union⟧. is an sc⁺-retraction of $E$ (partial quadrant $C$) onto $\{e : (c, e) \in t(U) \cap W,\ p(c, e) \in O'\}$: it is sc⁺ because $t$ is sc⁺ and the slice, $p$, $\rho$ (sc-smooth as a $C^\infty$ map on a finite-dimensional set, Corollary 1.2.5 (preprint 1.20)), $\Gamma$ and the second projection ⟦Chapter2/StrongBundle · ScBanach.prodSndOp⟧ are sc-smooth (§IV.2); and it is a retraction, because for $e' = \tau(e)$, $\Gamma(w) = (c, e')$ with $w = \rho(\cdot)$ on the fibre, so $t(c, e') = \Gamma(w)$, $p\Gamma(w) = w = \rho(w)$ and $\tau(e') = e'$. *Its tangent space*: if $D\rho(v_0) = 1$ on the $h$ with $(D\Gamma(v_0)h)_1 = 0$, then at the level-$1$ point over $\Gamma(v_0)_2$ the tangent space of the retract of $\tau$ contains $(D\Gamma(v_0)h)_2$ for all such $h$, by the chain rule $D\tau(y)w = (D\Gamma(v_0)D\rho(v_0)p\,Dt(c, y)(0, w))_2$, $Dt$ fixing $T(t(U)) = D\Gamma(v_0)N$ and $p \circ D\Gamma(v_0) = 1$.

### IV.24 Theorem 4.3.1 (preprint 4.23): families and Sard's theorem

**The conclusions at a point** ⟦Chapter4/Families · FibreLocal⟧: $z = (\lambda, x)$ is a stratified regular point, $A_\lambda$ is near $x$ the image of an sc⁺-retraction on an open subset of $X$, and $A_\lambda$ is in general and in good position at $x$.

**The local step** ⟦Chapter4/Families · exists_local_family⟧. *For $X$ tame and $A \subseteq \mathbb R^m \times X$ a smooth finite-dimensional submanifold in general position at every point, every $z_0 \in A$ has an open neighbourhood $U$ and a Lebesgue-null $\mathrm{CV} \subseteq \mathbb R^m$ such that the conclusions hold at every $z \in A \cap U$ with $z_1 \notin \mathrm{CV}$.* Proof:

1. *The chart.* $z_{0,2}$ is smooth ⟦Chapter3/ParamPerturbation · prod_rn_mem_smoothSet_iff⟧, so there is a tame chart $\varphi$ of $X$ centred at it (§IV.5); the product chart $\chi = (r \mapsto r - \lambda_0) \times \varphi$ is a chart of $\mathbb R^m \times X$ ⟦Chapter3/ParamPerturbation · isChart_rnChart⟧ ⟦Chapter2/FibredProduct · MPolyfold.IsChart.prod⟧ centred at $z_0$, with the tame retraction $\mathrm{id} \times r_X$ ⟦Chapter2/FibredProduct · ScRetraction.prod⟧ ⟦Chapter2/FibredProduct · ScRetraction.IsTameWith.prod⟧ and coordinates those of $\varphi$ ($\mathbb R^m$ has none).
2. *Theorem 4.2.11 (preprint 4.22) in the chart.* The chart retraction $t$ of $A$ in $\chi$ is sc⁺ (§IV.11), and general position at $z_0$ read in $\chi$ is general position at $0$; Theorem 4.2.11 (preprint 4.22) in the chart (§IV.17) gives $N = T_0(\chi A)$ with $N + E^{\min}_0 = E_0$, so the coordinates $\ell$ are onto on $N$, a closed good complement $Y \subseteq E^{\min}_0$, the projection $p$, and $\Gamma : V \to \chi(A)$ over $V = O_V \cap \{\ell \ge 0\} \subseteq N$, with $\ell \circ \Gamma = \ell$.
3. *Sard.* $f(v) = \Gamma(v)_1 + \lambda_0$, the projection $P$ read in the chart, is sc-smooth on $V$ as the first projection ⟦Chapter2/StrongBundle · ScBanach.prodFstOp⟧ composed with $\Gamma$, hence $C^\infty$ (§IV.1); $\mathrm{CV} = f(\text{stratified critical points of } f)$ is null (§IV.21). $U = W_A \cap \chi^{-1}(W)$, with $W_A$ the open set of the submanifold chart (§IV.11) and $W$ that of Proposition 4.1.9 (preprint 4.9).
4. *A point $z \in A \cap U$ with $z_1 \notin \mathrm{CV}$.* $\chi(z) \in t(U) \cap W$, so $v_0 = p(\chi z) \in V$ with $\Gamma(v_0) = \chi(z)$ and $f(v_0) = z_1$; hence $v_0$ is not stratified-critical. Since $\ell(D\Gamma(v)h) = \ell(h)$, $D\Gamma(v_0)$ maps the stratum subspace of $\sigma(v_0)$ onto $T_yA \cap E_y$, $y = \Gamma(v_0)$; so $z$ is a stratified regular point, in the chart $\chi$.
5. *The fibre.* The fibre retraction $\rho$ of $f$ at $v_0$ (§IV.22), the sc⁺-retraction $\tau$ onto the chart image of $A_{z_1}$ (§IV.23), the sc⁺-retraction $\varphi^{-1} \circ \tau \circ \varphi$ on an open subset of $X$ (§IV.18), whose image is $A_{z_1}$ near $z_2$; the tangent space of $\tau$'s retract contains the vertical tangent vectors of $A$, since $D\rho(v_0) = 1$ on $\ker Df(v_0)$; general position of $A_{z_1}$ at $z_2$ (§IV.23); good position from general position (Lemma 7.B).

**The cover** ⟦Chapter4/Families · theorem_4_23_of_subset⟧. *For $X$ tame and $A \subseteq \mathbb R^m \times X$ a smooth finite-dimensional submanifold with compact closure, in general position at every point, and any set $S \subseteq \mathbb R^m$ of parameters, there is $\Sigma \subseteq S$ with $S \setminus \Sigma$ null, consisting of stratified regular values, with the conclusions at every point of every $A_\lambda$, $\lambda \in \Sigma$.* The text covers $\{z \in A : |p(z)| \le \varepsilon\}$ "with the finitely many such neighborhoods $U(z_1), \dots, U(z_k)$". That set need not be compact: in $\mathbb R \times \mathbb R$, $A = (-2\varepsilon, 2\varepsilon) \times \{2\} \cup \{(\varepsilon(1 - x), x) : 0 < x < 1\}$ has compact closure and compact non-empty fibres over $|\lambda| \le \varepsilon$, but $\{|p| \le \varepsilon\}$ contains the open segment, which accumulates at $(\varepsilon, 0) \notin A$, and neighbourhoods of radius less than half the distance to $(\varepsilon, 0)$ have no finite subfamily covering it. A countable subcover suffices: $\mathbb R^m \times X$ is metrizable (Theorem 2.3.10 (preprint 2.16)) ⟦Chapter2/Metrizable · MPolyfold.metrizableSpace⟧, so the compact $\overline A$, and with it $A$, is separable, hence second countable; countably many of the neighbourhoods of the local step cover $A$, the countable union of their null sets is null, and $\Sigma = S \setminus \bigcup_i\mathrm{CV}(z_i)$ (Findings, item 103). Some countability is necessary: an uncountable discrete $A$ can have critical values of positive measure; here it comes from the compactness of $\overline A$, which the text assumes.

**Theorem 4.3.1 (preprint 4.23)** ⟦Chapter4/Families · theorem_4_23⟧. *Statement.* Let $X$ be tame, $A \subseteq \mathbb R^m \times X$ a smooth finite-dimensional submanifold with tame induced structure and compact closure, $\varepsilon > 0$, and (1) for $|\lambda| \le \varepsilon$ ($|\lambda|^2 = \sum\lambda_i^2$) the set $A \cap \{z_1 = \lambda\}$ is compact and non-empty, (2) $A$ is in general position at every point. Then there is $\Sigma \subseteq \{|\lambda| < \varepsilon\}$ with null complement in $\{|\lambda| < \varepsilon\}$, consisting of stratified regular values, such that for $\lambda \in \Sigma$ the fibre $A_\lambda$ is compact, a smooth finite-dimensional submanifold of $X$, in general position at each of its points, with a corner structure (Definition 4.1.1 (preprint 4.1)), and tame. *Proof.* The cover for $S = \{|\lambda| < \varepsilon\}$. $A_\lambda$ is the image of the compact $A \cap \{z_1 = \lambda\}$ under the projection to $X$, hence compact. It is a smooth finite-dimensional submanifold and in general position by the conclusions at its points; it is in good position at every point (Lemma 7.B), so Theorem 4.2.7 (preprint 4.18) gives the corner structure and tameness. Hypothesis (2), misprinted in the text ("there exists a sc-complement $T_zA$ in $T_zZ$ which is contained in $T^R_zZ$"), is Definition 4.2.10 (preprint 4.21), as the proof uses it. Not used: the tameness of the induced structure of $A$, the non-emptiness in (1), $\varepsilon > 0$ (Findings, item 101). Every stratified regular value is a regular value — $T_zA \cap E_z \subseteq T_zA$ — so $\Sigma$ consists of regular values in the text's sense; but the conclusion is not true at every ordinary regular value (§IV.23).

### IV.26 What the formalization taught us

1. *Centred charts are a construction.* They exist only at smooth points and live in the partial quadrant $C_{\varphi(x)}$, on the set where it agrees with $C$; four theorems of the chapter and Lemma 5.3.3 (preprint 5.11) rely on them, and the text never builds them (§IV.5).
2. *Proposition 4.1.9 (preprint 4.9) is a statement about fixed sets, and its proof is not the implicit function theorem.* The text's route, as we read it, gives a germ over a different complement from the one needed. The direct proof uses only the sc⁺ property — a mean value estimate on every level and the level-$1$ continuity of $t$ — and its regularity argument rests on two observations: the estimate is applied at $\Gamma(a)$ with the frame $B_{\Gamma(a)}$, not at $0$, and derivatives of $t$ only meet the finite-dimensional smooth tangent space, so the loss of levels does not accumulate (§§IV.6–IV.9). The projection must be the one along the closed good complement, not $Dt(0)$.
3. *Theorem 4.1.2 (preprint 4.2) does not need tameness*, and its compatibility claim needs charts composed with sc-diffeomorphisms between retracts (§IV.10).
4. *The sc⁺ condition has to be read in charts*, in both directions: a submanifold's chart retraction is sc⁺ (§IV.11), and an sc⁺-retraction in a chart is one on an open set (§IV.18). Every statement of §§4.2–4.3 passes through these two facts.
5. *One step of Lemma 4.2.8 (preprint 4.19) is false*, and the repair is the observation that the complement of $T_0O$ lies in $C \cap (-C)$ at the corner of a centred chart (§IV.14).
6. *General position implies good position* (§IV.16), a step the text uses in Theorem 4.2.11 (4) (preprint 4.22) and Theorem 4.3.1 (preprint 4.23) without stating it.
7. *Theorem 4.3.1 (preprint 4.23) needs stratified regular values and a countable cover*, and the fibre's manifold structure needs the implicit function theorem at corner points with the kernel in good position — the argument of Lemma 5.26 (preprint) — and Theorem 3.8.22 (preprint 3.78) in $C^\infty$ form (§§IV.21–IV.24). Sard's theorem had to be supplied in all dimensions (§IV.20).

### IV.27 Remark 4.1.3's (preprint 4.3) example: a retraction that loses a level
