## Part IV. Manifolds with boundary with corners, and sc-differential forms (HWZ Chapter 4, §§4.1–4.5)

Chapter 4 asks when an M-polyfold — a space glued from images of sc-smooth retractions — is secretly an ordinary manifold with boundary with corners, and why the fibres of a family of such manifolds are good for almost every parameter. The pivot is the *sc⁺ condition*: a retraction that gains a level. At a smooth point its derivative is compact, so its retract has finite-dimensional tangent spaces (Lemma 4.1.7 (preprint 4.7)), and near a point where the tangent space is in good position to the partial quadrant the retract is the graph of a map over that tangent space (Proposition 4.1.9 (preprint 4.9)). Theorem 4.1.2 (preprint 4.2) characterizes the manifolds with corners among M-polyfolds by these two properties; Theorem 4.2.7 (preprint 4.18) applies the characterization to submanifolds in good position; Theorem 4.2.11 (preprint 4.22) describes a submanifold near a point of general position; Theorem 4.3.1 (preprint 4.23) combines that description with Sard's theorem on the strata of the corners. The last section, rewritten in the 2021 edition, builds sc-differential forms, their exterior derivative, Stokes' theorem and a Poincaré lemma, with vector fields and their bracket in Appendix 4.5. This Part retells: finite-dimensional sc-spaces and corner charts (§IV.1); sc⁺-maps (§IV.2); sc⁺-retractions, Lemmas 4.1.5 and 4.1.7 (preprint 4.5 and 4.7) (§IV.3); Proposition 4.1.8 (preprint 4.8) with its chart form and Remark 4.1.3 (preprint 4.3) (§IV.4); charts centred at a smooth point, which the text uses and never constructs (§IV.5); Proposition 4.1.9 (preprint 4.9) with Lemmas 4.1.12 and 4.1.13 (preprint 4.11 and 4.12), by a direct proof (§§IV.6–IV.9); Theorem 4.1.2 (preprint 4.2) (§IV.10); smooth finite-dimensional submanifolds and Remark 4.2.2 (preprint 4.14) (§IV.11); Proposition 4.2.3 (preprint 4.15) (§IV.12); good and general position in charts and their chart independence (§IV.13); Lemma 4.2.8 (preprint 4.19) and Theorem 4.2.7 (preprint 4.18) (§IV.14); Remark 4.2.9 (preprint 4.20) (§IV.15); the linear algebra at the corner of a centred chart (§IV.16); Theorem 4.2.11 (preprint 4.22) (§§IV.17–IV.18) and Remark 4.3.2 (preprint 4.24) (§IV.19); Sard's theorem (§IV.20); the finite-dimensional core of Theorem 4.3.1 (preprint 4.23) (§§IV.21–IV.22); the fibre over a regular value (§IV.23); Theorem 4.3.1 (preprint 4.23) (§IV.24); what is not formalized (§IV.25); what the formalization taught us (§IV.26); Remark 4.1.3's (preprint 4.3) example (§IV.27); and the 2021 edition's Theorems 4.2.11 and 4.3.1, vector fields and the Lie bracket (§§IV.28–IV.29). The later subsections close the remaining gaps of §§4.1–4.3: Lemma 4.1.11 (preprint 4.10) (§IV.30); Remark 4.2.2 (preprint 4.14) in both wordings and Question 4.2.4 (§IV.31); the local faces of Theorem 4.2.11 (1) (preprint 4.22) (§IV.32); the chart independence of stratified regularity (§IV.33); and Lemma 4.5.1 between open subsets with the pull-back bundle $TX|X^2$ (§IV.34). **Six structural choices.** (1) *sc⁺ is a property of maps.* The text defines it for retractions and operators and uses it for maps; here a map is sc⁺ when its lift into $F^1$ is sc-smooth, and the calculus of such maps is proved first (§IV.2). (6) *Forms are read in charts, and integrals on Mathlib manifolds.* The Whitney sum $\oplus_kTX$ is never built as an M-polyfold: a form is a family of alternating forms on the tangent spaces whose chart representatives on $\oplus_kTO$ are sc-smooth (§IV.35). ### IV.1 Finite-dimensional sc-spaces and corner charts (Definition 4.1.1 (preprint 4.1))

**Finite-dimensional sc-spaces.** Definition 4.1.1 (preprint 4.1) models its charts on finite-dimensional spaces "with the constant sc-structure"; the text remarks after Definition 1.1.1 (preprint 1.1) that a finite-dimensional space has only that one. This is proved. If $E_0$ is finite-dimensional, every level $E_m$ is, since it injects into $E_0$ ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · ScBanach.finiteDimensional_lvl⟧. The range of $E_m \to E_0$ is then a finite-dimensional, hence closed, subspace; it contains the smooth points, which are dense (Definition 1.1.1 (2) (preprint 1.1)); so it is all of $E_0$ ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · ScBanach.toBase_surjective⟧. The inclusions are therefore bijective, and they form an sc-isomorphism of $E$ onto $E_0$ with the constant structure ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · ScBanach.scIsoConst⟧; in particular every point of $E_0$ is smooth ⟦Chapter4/Characterization · ScBanach.mem_smooth_of_finiteDimensional⟧. The density axiom is what makes this work: $E_0 = \mathbb R$, $E_m = 0$ for $m \ge 1$ has compact injective inclusions and levels that differ (Findings, Chapter 4, item 6).

**Definition 4.1.1 (preprint 4.1).** A chart of $X$ is a *corner chart* if its local model is $(U, C, V)$ with $V$ a finite-dimensional real normed space carrying the constant sc-structure, $C$ a partial quadrant in $V$, $U$ relatively open in $C$, and the identity retraction ⟦Chapter4/Characterization · MChart.IsCornerChart⟧. Its model is then relatively open in a partial quadrant ⟦Chapter4/Characterization · MChart.IsCornerChart.isRelOpenInQuadrant⟧, finite-dimensional ⟦Chapter4/Characterization · MChart.IsCornerChart.finiteDimensional⟧, a tame retract — the identity retraction is tame ⟦Chapter2/Tame · ScRetraction.id_isTame⟧ ⟦Chapter4/Characterization · MChart.IsCornerChart.isTameRetract⟧ — and an sc⁺-retract by Remark 4.1.3 (preprint 4.3) (§IV.4) ⟦Chapter4/Characterization · MChart.IsCornerChart.isScPlusRetract⟧; every point of its model space is smooth ⟦Chapter4/Characterization · MChart.IsCornerChart.mem_smooth⟧. $X$ *has a corner structure* if it has an atlas of corner charts equivalent to its own atlas ⟦Chapter4/Characterization · MPolyfold.HasCornerStructure⟧. The dimension of $V$ may vary from chart to chart, as in the text. The text's "sc-smoothly compatible charts", "$\varphi$ an sc-diffeomorphism", is read as compatibility with the atlas of $X$, as for tameness (Definition 2.5.7 (preprint 2.37)).

*The definition is local* ⟦Chapter4/Characterization · MPolyfold.hasCornerStructure_iff⟧: $X$ has a corner structure iff every point lies in a corner chart that is a chart of the M-polyfold structure. One direction takes a chart of the equivalent atlas through the point. For the other, the corner charts that are charts of $X$ form an atlas: they cover $X$ by hypothesis, any two charts of $X$ are compatible ⟦Chapter2/MPolyfold · MPolyfold.IsChart.compatible⟧, and the atlas is equivalent to that of $X$ because each of its members is a chart of $X$. *A corner structure makes $X$ tame* ⟦Chapter4/Characterization · MPolyfold.HasCornerStructure.isTame⟧: the same atlas is an atlas of tame charts.

**The transition maps are $C^\infty$.** For a chart whose model is relatively open in a partial quadrant, every overlap $\chi(V \cap V')$ is relatively open ⟦Chapter4/Characterization · MChart.isRelOpenInQuadrant_overlap⟧. For compatible corner charts, the transition map is sc-smooth between the overlap retracts; composed with the identity retraction of the overlap it is sc-smooth on a relatively open subset of a partial quadrant in a finite-dimensional space with the constant structure, and such maps are exactly the $C^\infty$ maps with derivatives within the set ⟦Chapter1/ExpositionLemmas · isScSmooth_iff_contDiffOn_const⟧ ⟦Chapter4/Characterization · MChart.IsCornerChart.contDiffOn_transition⟧. That is the text's "classically smooth"; "the atlas defines the structure of a smooth manifold with boundary with corners" is not formalized beyond it, because Mathlib's manifolds with corners have a single model space and here the partial quadrants vary from chart to chart.

**Arbitrary finite-dimensional models.** The constant structure is no restriction. A partial quadrant of a finite-dimensional $E$ is one of $E_0$ with the constant structure ⟦Chapter4/Characterization · IsPartialQuadrant.toConst⟧ ⟦Chapter4/Characterization · IsRelOpenInQuadrant.toConst⟧, and the identity of $E_0$ is an sc-diffeomorphism between the two models ⟦Chapter4/Characterization · IsRelOpenInQuadrant.isScDiffeoQuadrant_toConst⟧. Composing a chart with it (the construction $\mathtt{compDiffeo}$ of Chapter 3) ⟦Chapter3/GlobalIFTCenter · MChart.compDiffeo⟧ gives, with the same map and domain, a corner chart of the same dimension ⟦Chapter4/Characterization · MChart.exists_isCornerChart_of_finiteDimensional⟧. Hence $X$ has a corner structure iff it has an equivalent atlas of charts onto relatively open subsets of partial quadrants in sc-Banach spaces with finite-dimensional $E_0$ ⟦Chapter4/Characterization · MPolyfold.hasCornerStructure_iff_finiteDimensional⟧ — verbatim the definition of the manifolds with corners of locally constant dimension of Theorem 3.2.3 (preprint 3.27) (§III.36) — and the manifolds with corners of dimension $n$ of Theorem 3.2.3 (preprint 3.27) are the corner structures all of whose charts have dimension $n$ ⟦Chapter4/Characterization · MPolyfold.isManifoldWithCorners_iff⟧ ⟦Chapter4/Characterization · MPolyfold.IsManifoldWithCorners.hasCornerStructure⟧.

### IV.2 Sc⁺-maps

The text uses "sc⁺-map" in the proof of Lemma 4.1.7 (preprint 4.7) and composes sc⁺-retractions with sc-smooth maps in Lemma 4.1.5 (preprint 4.5), but defines sc⁺ only for retractions (Definition 4.1.4 (preprint 4.4)) and operators (Definition 1.1.5 (preprint 1.4)). The formalization defines it for maps on any set, as the neighbouring notions of the project are (sc⁺-operators are sc-operators into $F^1$, sc⁺-sections sc-smooth maps into $K[1]$).

**The lift.** For $x \in E_1 \subseteq E_0$, $1_0^1 x$ is the point of $E_1$ over $x$, with a junk value off $E_1$ ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · ScBanach.liftOne⟧ ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · ScBanach.toBase_liftOne⟧ ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · ScBanach.liftOne_toBase⟧.

**Definition.** $f$ is *sc⁺ on $U$* if $f(U) \subseteq F_1$ and the lift $x \mapsto 1_0^1 f(x)$, a map $U \to F^1$, is sc-smooth ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · IsScPlus⟧. Then $f(U_m) \subseteq F_{m+1}$: the lift is sc⁰, so it maps $U_m$ into $(F^1)_m = F_{m+1}$ ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · IsScPlus.mem_range_succ⟧. On an sc-domain the canonical lift can be replaced by any sc-smooth $g : U \to F^1$ with $\iota \circ g = f$, which is how the text phrases it ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · IsScPlus.iff_exists⟧. The calculus: an sc⁺-map is sc-smooth, being $\iota \circ$ (lift) with the sc-operator $\iota : F^1 \to F$ ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · IsScPlus.isScSmooth⟧; the property depends only on the values on $U$ ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · IsScPlus.congr⟧ and passes to smaller sc-domains ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · IsScPlus.mono⟧; *sc⁺ ∘ sc-smooth is sc⁺*, the lift of $g \circ f$ being (lift of $g$) $\circ f$, sc-smooth by the chain rule ⟦Chapter1/ChainRule · IsScSmooth.comp⟧ ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · IsScPlus.comp_isScSmooth⟧; *sc-smooth ∘ sc⁺ is sc⁺*, the lift of $g \circ f$ being $g^1 \circ$ (lift of $f$) with $g^1 : V^1 \to G^1$ the map $g$ induces on level $1$, sc-smooth by Proposition 1.2.2 (preprint 1.17) ⟦Chapter1/Levels · IsScSmooth.shift⟧ ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · IsScSmooth.comp_isScPlus⟧; and an sc⁺-operator is an sc⁺-map ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · ScPlus.isScPlus⟧.

**The derivative at a smooth point** ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · IsScPlus.exists_scPlus_deriv⟧. Let $f$ be sc⁺ on an sc-domain $U$ and $x \in U_1$ smooth. The lift $g$ is sc-smooth into $F^1$, so its derivative at the smooth point $x$ is an sc-operator $E \to F^1$ (the remark after Proposition 1.2.1 (preprint 1.16)), that is an sc⁺-operator $S : E \to F$. Since $f = \iota \circ g$ on $U$, the chain rule and the uniqueness of the derivative on an sc-domain give $Df(x) = \iota \circ Dg(x)$, the level-$0$ operator of $S$. This is the text's step "$Dr(o)$ is a sc⁺-operator, since $r$ is a sc⁺-map". Consequently $Df(x)$ is compact, and so is every level operator of $S$ ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · IsScPlus.isCompactOperator_deriv⟧.

**A compact projection has finite-dimensional range** ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · finiteDimensional_range_of_isCompactOperator_of_idem⟧: if $P = P \circ P$ is compact, its range is the closed set $\{v : Pv = v\}$, $P$ restricts to a compact operator of the range which is the identity, and a normed space with compact identity is finite-dimensional (Riesz). The text takes this for granted.

### IV.3 Sc⁺-retractions and sc⁺-retracts (Definitions 4.1.4, 4.1.6 (preprint 4.4, 4.6), Lemmas 4.1.5, 4.1.7 (preprint 4.5, 4.7))

**Definition 4.1.4 (preprint 4.4).** An sc-smooth retraction $r : U \to U$ is an *sc⁺-retraction* if it is an sc⁺-map on $U$ ⟦Chapter4/ScPlus · ScRetraction.IsScPlus⟧. 5. Its fixed point $y$ (Banach's theorem on the complete $B_a$) gives $u = a + y$ with $(1 - p)(t(u) - u) = 0$, i.e. $w - u \in N$ for $w = t(u)$. Now $w \in O$, $|w|_1 < r$ by the choice of $\delta$, $t(w) = w$, and $Dt(0)(w - u) = w - u$. The estimate between $u$ and $w$ reads $|t(w) - t(u) - Dt(0)(w - u)|_1 = |w - u|_1 \le \theta|w - u|_1$, so $w = u$: $u = t(u) \in O$ — the text's argument "$a_1 = a$".

So $u \in O_1$, $p(u) = a$, $u - a \in Y$, $|u - a|_1 \le \varepsilon'|a|_1$ and $|u|_1 < r$. The text's "$|a(\tau)|_1 \le \varepsilon_1$ if $|a|_0 < \varepsilon$" is the level-$1$ continuity of $t$ at $0$ used in step 5.

### IV.8 The step $p(O \cap W) \subseteq C$, and the neighbourhoods

**$p$ maps points of $O$ near $0$ into $C$** ⟦Chapter4/ScPlusGraph · GraphData.p_mem_C⟧, which the conclusion "$p : U_0 \to V \subseteq T_0O \cap C$" needs and the text never checks. **The neighbourhoods** ⟦Chapter4/ScPlusGraph · GraphData.Nbhd⟧ ⟦Chapter4/ScPlusGraph · GraphData.exists_nbhd⟧. For every $\theta_0 > 0$ the proof chooses, in this order:

**Continuity into every level.** $\Gamma(V) \subseteq O \subseteq E_\infty$ ⟦Chapter4/ScPlusGraph · GraphData.Nbhd.Γ_mem_smooth⟧, so $\Gamma$ has lifts $\Gamma_m : V \to E_m$ ⟦Chapter4/ScPlusGraph · GraphData.Nbhd.Γ_lvl⟧ with values in $O_m$ ⟦Chapter4/ScPlusGraph · GraphData.Nbhd.Γ_lvl_mem_retract⟧. $\Gamma_1$ is Lipschitz: with $u = \Gamma(a)$, $u' = \Gamma(a')$, $|u' - u|_1 \le |p(u' - u)|_1 + |(1 - p)(u' - u)|_1 \le \|\mathrm{lift}_N\||a' - a| + \frac12|u' - u|_1$, so $|u' - u|_1 \le 2\|\mathrm{lift}_N\||a' - a|$ ⟦Chapter4/ScPlusGraph · GraphData.Nbhd.norm_Γ_lvl_one_sub_le⟧ ⟦Chapter4/ScPlusGraph · GraphData.Nbhd.continuousOn_Γ_lvl_one⟧. **The abstract step.** On a set $S$ of a normed space, let $s$ satisfy the mean value estimate with derivative $D_0$ at $u_0$ and constant $\theta$, let $q$, $B$ be continuous linear with $q \circ B = 1$ and $(1 - Bq)D_0 = 0$, and $\theta\|1 - Bq\| \le \frac12$. **The frame formula** ⟦Chapter4/ScPlusGraph · GraphData.Nbhd.hasFDerivWithinAt_Γ_lvl⟧. $$D\Gamma(a) = Dt(\Gamma(a))|_N \circ \big(p \circ Dt(\Gamma(a))|_N\big)^{-1}\quad\text{into every level } E_{k+1}.$$

**The $C^k$-bootstrap.** By Proposition 1.2.3 (preprint 1.18) for the tangent map of the lift $t^1$, $(x, w) \mapsto Dt_{j+1}(x)w$ is $C^k$ on $U_{j+k+1} \times E_{j+k+1}$, read through the inclusions into level $j + 1$ ⟦Chapter4/ScPlusGraph · GraphData.contDiffOn_lvlDeriv_apply⟧. ### IV.16 Linear algebra at the corner of a centred chart; Lemmas 7.A and 7.B of the exposition

In a chart centred at $0$, every coordinate vanishes at $0$, so the minimal subspace there is $E^{\min}_0 = \{\lambda = 0\}$, the text's "$\{0\} \oplus W$" ⟦Chapter4/LocalStructure · mem_minimalSubspaceWith_zero⟧, and it lies in every $E_x$ ⟦Chapter4/LocalStructure · minimalSubspaceWith_zero_le⟧. **Closed complements.** If $N$ is finite-dimensional, $K$ closed and $N + K$ the whole space, $N$ has a closed complement $Y \subseteq K$: take a continuous projection $q$ onto the finite-dimensional $N \cap K$ and $Y = K \cap \ker q$ ⟦Chapter4/LocalStructure · exists_isClosed_isCompl_le⟧. **Good position from $N + E^{\min}_0 = E_0$.** Let $N$ be finite-dimensional with $N + E^{\min}_0 = E_0$. A complement inside $E^{\min}_0$ is good with every $\varepsilon$ ⟦Chapter4/LocalStructure · isGoodComplement_of_le_zero⟧. Hence $N$ is in good position, with a closed good complement $Y \subseteq E^{\min}_0$ ⟦Chapter4/LocalStructure · exists_goodComplement_le_zero⟧. **The degeneracy index on the slice** ⟦Chapter4/LocalStructure · degIndex_slice⟧: $N \cap C$ is the partial quadrant of $N$ presented by $v \mapsto (\lambda(v), v - s(\lambda(v)))$, $s$ a linear section of $\lambda|_N$, and $d_{N \cap C}(v) = d_C(v)$ — both count the vanishing coordinates. **The kernel at a stratified regular point is in good position** ⟦Chapter4/FamiliesLocal · exists_goodComplement_stratSub⟧ — the argument of Lemma 5.26 (preprint; not in the 2021 edition), which the text needs here and does not give. Let $K + \{\ell_i = 0, i \in \sigma\} = V$ (for $K = \ker Df(v_0)$, $\sigma = \sigma(v_0)$, this is stratified regularity). The stratum subspace is closed, so $K$ has a closed complement $Y$ inside it (§IV.16). Adding a vector of $Y$ changes none of the coordinates $\ell_i$, $i \in \sigma$, so $Y$ is a good complement with $\varepsilon = 1$. For the interior points: choose $x$ with $\ell(x) = (1, \dots, 1)$ and write $x = k + y$; then $\ell_i(k) = 1$ for $i \in \sigma$, and the open set $\{k' \in K : \ell_i(k') > 0, i \in \sigma\}$ contains $k$ and lies in $K \cap \{\ell_i \ge 0, i \in \sigma\}$.

**The fibre retraction** ⟦Chapter4/FamiliesLocal · exists_fibre_retraction⟧. *Let $\ell$ be onto, $f$ $C^\infty$ on $O \cap C$ and $v_0 \in O \cap C$ not stratified-critical. There are an open $O' \ni v_0$, $O' \subseteq O$, and $\rho$, $C^\infty$ on $O' \cap C$, mapping $O' \cap C$ into itself and into $f^{-1}(f(v_0))$, equal to the identity on the fibre there, with $D\rho(v_0)$ of range $\ker Df(v_0)$ and equal to the identity on it.* Proof:

1. Stratified regularity gives $Df(v_0)$ onto (its restriction to the stratum already is) and $K + \text{stratum} = V$ for $K = \ker Df(v_0)$; so $K$ is in good position to $C_{v_0}$ with a closed good complement $Y$ in the stratum subspace.
2. Near $0$, $v_0 + C_{v_0}$ and $C$ agree: on $G_0 = \{w : \ell_j(v_0 + w) > 0, j \notin \sigma\}$, a point $v_0 + w$ is in $C$ iff $w \in C_{v_0}$, since $\ell_i(v_0) = 0$ for $i \in \sigma$ — the re-centring of §IV.5 in the model. So $U_1 = (O - v_0) \cap G_0 \cap C_{v_0}$ is relatively open in $C_{v_0}$, contains $0$, and $w \mapsto v_0 + w$ maps it into $U$.
3. $g(w) = f(v_0 + w) - f(v_0)$ is $C^\infty$ on $U_1$, $g(0) = 0$, $Dg(0) = Df(v_0)$ onto; Theorem 3.8.22 (preprint 3.78) in $C^\infty$ form gives the fibre near $v_0$ as the graph $\{v_0 + k + \tau(k)\}$ of a $C^\infty$ map $\tau : V' \to Y$, $V' \subseteq K \cap C_{v_0}$, unique for small $k$ and $y$.
4. With $q(v)$ the projection of $v - v_0$ onto $K$ along $Y$ ⟦Chapter4/LocalStructure · exists_proj_of_isCompl⟧, put $\rho(v) = v_0 + q(v) + \tau(q(v))$ on $O' \cap C$, $O'$ a small ball around $v_0$ chosen so that $q$ maps it into the uniqueness region and $\rho(O' \cap C) \subseteq O'$. 5. $D\tau(0) = 0$ gives $D\rho(v_0) = q$, whose range is $K$ and which is the identity on $K$.

### IV.23 Stratified regularity, the sc⁺-retraction onto $A_\lambda$, and its general position

**The family.** For tame $X$, $\mathbb R^m \times X$ with the product charts is tame ⟦Chapter3/ParamPerturbation · prod_rn_isTame⟧ (§III.40 for $\mathbb R^m$ as an M-polyfold ⟦Chapter3/ParamPerturbation · rnPolyfold⟧ with its translation charts ⟦Chapter3/ParamPerturbation · rnChart⟧). **Stratified regular points** ⟦Chapter4/Families · IsStratRegularPoint⟧. $z \in A$ is a stratified regular point if, for some data of Definition 4.2.1 (preprint 4.13) at $z$, some $a \in \mathbb R^m$, some chart $\varphi$ of $X$ at $z_2$, some presentation of its partial quadrant and the level-$1$ point $y$ over $\chi(z)$, every $\mu \in \mathbb R^m$ is the first component of a tangent vector $v$ of the chart model of $A$ at $y$ with $v_2 \in E_{\varphi(z_2)}$: the projection maps $T_zA \cap E_z$ — the tangent space of the stratum through $z$ — onto $\mathbb R^m$. $\lambda$ is a *stratified regular value* if every point of $A$ over $\lambda$ is a stratified regular point ⟦Chapter4/Families · IsStratRegularValue⟧. This is the text's "regular value of every $p_\sigma$". The condition does not depend on these choices (§IV.33). *The naive notion does not suffice*: in $\mathbb R \times [0, \infty)$, $A = \{(x, x) : 0 \le x < 1\} \cup (-1, 1) \times \{5\}$ and $\varepsilon = \frac12$ satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem 4.3.1 (preprint 4.23) (at $(0, 0)$, $T_zA = \mathbb R(1, 1)$ has the complement $\mathbb R \times 0 = T^R_zZ$), $Dp$ is onto everywhere, but $A_0 = \{0, 5\}$ is zero-dimensional and meets $\partial X$, so it is not in general position (Remark 4.3.2 (preprint 4.24)); $(0, 0)$ is stratified-critical, $T_zA \cap E_z = 0$.

**The slice** $e \mapsto (c, e)$: the inclusion $h \mapsto (0, h)$ ⟦Chapter4/Families · ScBanach.prodInrOp⟧ followed by the translation by the smooth vector $(c, 0)$ ⟦Chapter4/Families · mem_smooth_rn_zero⟧ is sc-smooth ⟦Chapter4/Families · isScSmooth_slice⟧ with derivative $h \mapsto (0, h)$ ⟦Chapter4/Families · scDeriv_slice⟧. **The sc⁺-retraction onto $A_\lambda$** ⟦Chapter4/Families · exists_slice_retraction⟧ — Definition 4.2.1 (preprint 4.13) requires it and the text does not construct it. In the model $\mathbb R^m \oplus E$ of a product chart, let $t$ be an sc⁺-retraction (the chart model of $A$), described near a point as the graph $\Gamma : V \to t(U) \cap W$ with inverse $p$ (Proposition 4.1.9 (preprint 4.9)), and $\rho$ a $C^\infty$ retraction of $O' \cap (\mathbb R^m \times C) \subseteq V$ onto the points $v$ with $\Gamma(v)_1 = c$ (the fibre retraction of §IV.22 for $f = (\Gamma)_1$). Then
$$\tau(e) = \big(\Gamma(\rho(p(t(c, e))))\big)_2$$
is an sc⁺-retraction of $E$ (partial quadrant $C$) onto $\{e : (c, e) \in t(U) \cap W,\ p(c, e) \in O'\}$: it is sc⁺ because $t$ is sc⁺ and the slice, $p$, $\rho$ (sc-smooth as a $C^\infty$ map on a finite-dimensional set, Corollary 1.2.5 (preprint 1.20)), $\Gamma$ and the second projection ⟦Chapter2/StrongBundle · ScBanach.prodSndOp⟧ are sc-smooth (§IV.2); and it is a retraction, because for $e' = \tau(e)$, $\Gamma(w) = (c, e')$ with $w = \rho(\cdot)$ on the fibre, so $t(c, e') = \Gamma(w)$, $p\Gamma(w) = w = \rho(w)$ and $\tau(e') = e'$. *Its tangent space*: if $D\rho(v_0) = 1$ on the $h$ with $(D\Gamma(v_0)h)_1 = 0$, then at the level-$1$ point over $\Gamma(v_0)_2$ the tangent space of the retract of $\tau$ contains $(D\Gamma(v_0)h)_2$ for all such $h$, by the chain rule $D\tau(y)w = (D\Gamma(v_0)D\rho(v_0)p\,Dt(c, y)(0, w))_2$, $Dt$ fixing $T(t(U)) = D\Gamma(v_0)N$ and $p \circ D\Gamma(v_0) = 1$.

### IV.24 Theorem 4.3.1 (preprint 4.23): families and Sard's theorem

**The conclusions at a point** ⟦Chapter4/Families · FibreLocal⟧: $z = (\lambda, x)$ is a stratified regular point, $A_\lambda$ is near $x$ the image of an sc⁺-retraction on an open subset of $X$, and $A_\lambda$ is in general and in good position at $x$.

**The local step** ⟦Chapter4/Families · exists_local_family⟧. *For $X$ tame and $A \subseteq \mathbb R^m \times X$ a smooth finite-dimensional submanifold in general position at every point, every $z_0 \in A$ has an open neighbourhood $U$ and a Lebesgue-null $\mathrm{CV} \subseteq \mathbb R^m$ such that the conclusions hold at every $z \in A \cap U$ with $z_1 \notin \mathrm{CV}$.* Proof:

1. *The chart.* $z_{0,2}$ is smooth ⟦Chapter3/ParamPerturbation · prod_rn_mem_smoothSet_iff⟧, so there is a tame chart $\varphi$ of $X$ centred at it (§IV.5); the product chart $\chi = (r \mapsto r - \lambda_0) \times \varphi$ is a chart of $\mathbb R^m \times X$ ⟦Chapter3/ParamPerturbation · isChart_rnChart⟧ ⟦Chapter2/FibredProduct · MPolyfold.IsChart.prod⟧ centred at $z_0$, with the tame retraction $\mathrm{id} \times r_X$ ⟦Chapter2/FibredProduct · ScRetraction.prod⟧ ⟦Chapter2/FibredProduct · ScRetraction.IsTameWith.prod⟧ and coordinates those of $\varphi$ ($\mathbb R^m$ has none).
2. *Theorem 4.2.11 (preprint 4.22) in the chart.* The chart retraction $t$ of $A$ in $\chi$ is sc⁺ (§IV.11), and general position at $z_0$ read in $\chi$ is general position at $0$; Theorem 4.2.11 (preprint 4.22) in the chart (§IV.17) gives $N = T_0(\chi A)$ with $N + E^{\min}_0 = E_0$, so the coordinates $\ell$ are onto on $N$, a closed good complement $Y \subseteq E^{\min}_0$, the projection $p$, and $\Gamma : V \to \chi(A)$ over $V = O_V \cap \{\ell \ge 0\} \subseteq N$, with $\ell \circ \Gamma = \ell$.
3. *Sard.* $f(v) = \Gamma(v)_1 + \lambda_0$, the projection $P$ read in the chart, is sc-smooth on $V$ as the first projection ⟦Chapter2/StrongBundle · ScBanach.prodFstOp⟧ composed with $\Gamma$, hence $C^\infty$ (§IV.1); $\mathrm{CV} = f(\text{stratified critical points of } f)$ is null (§IV.21). $U = W_A \cap \chi^{-1}(W)$, with $W_A$ the open set of the submanifold chart (§IV.11) and $W$ that of Proposition 4.1.9 (preprint 4.9).
4. *A point $z \in A \cap U$ with $z_1 \notin \mathrm{CV}$.* $\chi(z) \in t(U) \cap W$, so $v_0 = p(\chi z) \in V$ with $\Gamma(v_0) = \chi(z)$ and $f(v_0) = z_1$; hence $v_0$ is not stratified-critical. Since $\ell(D\Gamma(v)h) = \ell(h)$, $D\Gamma(v_0)$ maps the stratum subspace of $\sigma(v_0)$ onto $T_yA \cap E_y$, $y = \Gamma(v_0)$; so $z$ is a stratified regular point, in the chart $\chi$.
5. *The fibre.* The fibre retraction $\rho$ of $f$ at $v_0$ (§IV.22), the sc⁺-retraction $\tau$ onto the chart image of $A_{z_1}$ (§IV.23), the sc⁺-retraction $\varphi^{-1} \circ \tau \circ \varphi$ on an open subset of $X$ (§IV.18), whose image is $A_{z_1}$ near $z_2$; the tangent space of $\tau$'s retract contains the vertical tangent vectors of $A$, since $D\rho(v_0) = 1$ on $\ker Df(v_0)$; general position of $A_{z_1}$ at $z_2$ (§IV.23); good position from general position (Lemma 7.B).

**The cover** ⟦Chapter4/Families · theorem_4_3_1_of_subset⟧. *For $X$ tame and $A \subseteq \mathbb R^m \times X$ a smooth finite-dimensional submanifold with compact closure, in general position at every point, and any set $S \subseteq \mathbb R^m$ of parameters, there is $\Sigma \subseteq S$ with $S \setminus \Sigma$ null, consisting of stratified regular values, with the conclusions at every point of every $A_\lambda$, $\lambda \in \Sigma$.* The text covers $\{z \in A : |p(z)| \le \varepsilon\}$ "with the finitely many such neighborhoods $U(z_1), \dots, U(z_k)$". That set need not be compact: in $\mathbb R \times \mathbb R$, $A = (-2\varepsilon, 2\varepsilon) \times \{2\} \cup \{(\varepsilon(1 - x), x) : 0 < x < 1\}$ has compact closure and compact non-empty fibres over $|\lambda| \le \varepsilon$, but $\{|p| \le \varepsilon\}$ contains the open segment, which accumulates at $(\varepsilon, 0) \notin A$, and neighbourhoods of radius less than half the distance to $(\varepsilon, 0)$ have no finite subfamily covering it. A countable subcover suffices: $\mathbb R^m \times X$ is metrizable (Theorem 2.3.10 (preprint 2.16)) ⟦Chapter2/Metrizable · MPolyfold.metrizableSpace⟧, so the compact $\overline A$, and with it $A$, is separable, hence second countable; countably many of the neighbourhoods of the local step cover $A$, the countable union of their null sets is null, and $\Sigma = S \setminus \bigcup_i\mathrm{CV}(z_i)$ (Findings, item 103). Some countability is necessary: an uncountable discrete $A$ can have critical values of positive measure; here it comes from the compactness of $\overline A$, which the text assumes.

**Theorem 4.3.1 (preprint 4.23)** ⟦Chapter4/Families · theorem_4_3_1_preprint⟧. *Statement.* Let $X$ be tame, $A \subseteq \mathbb R^m \times X$ a smooth finite-dimensional submanifold with tame induced structure and compact closure, $\varepsilon > 0$, and (1) for $|\lambda| \le \varepsilon$ ($|\lambda|^2 = \sum\lambda_i^2$) the set $A \cap \{z_1 = \lambda\}$ is compact and non-empty, (2) $A$ is in general position at every point. Then there is $\Sigma \subseteq \{|\lambda| < \varepsilon\}$ with null complement in $\{|\lambda| < \varepsilon\}$, consisting of stratified regular values, such that for $\lambda \in \Sigma$ the fibre $A_\lambda$ is compact, a smooth finite-dimensional submanifold of $X$, in general position at each of its points, with a corner structure (Definition 4.1.1 (preprint 4.1)), and tame. *Proof.* The cover for $S = \{|\lambda| < \varepsilon\}$. $A_\lambda$ is the image of the compact $A \cap \{z_1 = \lambda\}$ under the projection to $X$, hence compact. It is a smooth finite-dimensional submanifold and in general position by the conclusions at its points; it is in good position at every point (Lemma 7.B), so Theorem 4.2.7 (preprint 4.18) gives the corner structure and tameness. Hypothesis (2), as printed ("there exists a sc-complement $T_zA$ in $T_zZ$ which is contained in $T^R_zZ$"), is read as Definition 4.2.10 (preprint 4.21), as the proof uses it. Not used: the tameness of the induced structure of $A$, the non-emptiness in (1), $\varepsilon > 0$ (Findings, item 101). Every stratified regular value is a regular value — $T_zA \cap E_z \subseteq T_zA$ — so $\Sigma$ consists of regular values in the text's sense; but the conclusion is not true at every ordinary regular value (§IV.23).

### IV.26 What the formalization taught us

1. *Centred charts are a construction.* They exist only at smooth points and live in the partial quadrant $C_{\varphi(x)}$, on the set where it agrees with $C$; four theorems of the chapter and Lemma 5.3.3 (preprint 5.11) rely on them, and the text never builds them (§IV.5).
2. *Proposition 4.1.9 (preprint 4.9) is a statement about fixed sets, and its proof is not the implicit function theorem.* The text's route, as we read it, gives a germ over a different complement from the one needed. The direct proof uses only the sc⁺ property — a mean value estimate on every level and the level-$1$ continuity of $t$ — and its regularity argument rests on two observations: the estimate is applied at $\Gamma(a)$ with the frame $B_{\Gamma(a)}$, not at $0$, and derivatives of $t$ only meet the finite-dimensional smooth tangent space, so the loss of levels does not accumulate (§§IV.6–IV.9). The projection must be the one along the closed good complement, not $Dt(0)$.
3. *Theorem 4.1.2 (preprint 4.2) does not need tameness*, and its compatibility claim needs charts composed with sc-diffeomorphisms between retracts (§IV.10).
4. *The sc⁺ condition has to be read in charts*, in both directions: a submanifold's chart retraction is sc⁺ (§IV.11), and an sc⁺-retraction in a chart is one on an open set (§IV.18). Every statement of §§4.2–4.3 passes through these two facts.
5. *One step in the proof of Lemma 4.2.8 (preprint 4.19) does not hold as we read it* ($C = [0, \infty)$, $z = 1$, $n + y = -\frac12$), and the step is replaced by the observation that the complement of $T_0O$ lies in $C \cap (-C)$ at the corner of a centred chart (§IV.14).
6. *General position implies good position* (§IV.16), a step the text uses in Theorem 4.2.11 (4) (preprint 4.22) and Theorem 4.3.1 (preprint 4.23) without stating it.
7. *Theorem 4.3.1 (preprint 4.23) needs stratified regular values and a countable cover*, and the fibre's manifold structure needs the implicit function theorem at corner points with the kernel in good position — the argument of Lemma 5.26 (preprint) — and Theorem 3.8.22 (preprint 3.78) in $C^\infty$ form (§§IV.21–IV.24). Sard's theorem had to be supplied in all dimensions (§IV.20).
8. *In a tame M-polyfold the good position of Theorem 4.1.2 (2) (preprint 4.2) is automatic, and for $d \le 1$ tameness is not needed.* The case that would block good position, a boundary coordinate vanishing on the tangent space, is excluded by the graph argument of 2021 Lemma 9.2.13. This makes the sentence of Remark 4.2.2 (preprint 4.14) on $d_A = 1$ a theorem without hypotheses (§IV.31).
9. *The formula for $d\omega$ on p. 197 does not hold as we read it with the printed sign.* The text's bracket is the negative of the classical one, so the classical sign fails too; $(-1)^{\ell+j+1}$ is the sign that works (§IV.38). The formal $d$ is defined by the local alternating sum of base derivatives, and it is chart independent by the symmetry of second derivatives (§§IV.36–IV.37).
10. *Forms on $\partial X$ need a reading, and the reading closes.* $\partial X$ is not an M-polyfold; forms on it are forms on $bX$ that agree over $B$. That $d$ preserves agreement needs no corner strata as M-polyfolds, only the map $r \circ P_i \circ P_j$ into two faces at once (§IV.40).
11. *Stokes' theorem needs the faces as manifolds, signed charts and an added reading.* "A union of smooth faces" is read as face-structured. $[0, 1]$ with the quadrant model has no oriented atlas, so integrals use signed charts. The face orientation is the outward vector first (§§IV.41–IV.43).
12. *The Poincaré lemma needs a smooth centre other than $0$.* In the proof of Lemma 4.4.8 the convex set $B_\delta(\varphi x) \cap C$ need not contain $0$. The homotopy about a smooth centre works with the same proof, and its sc-smoothness is a theorem on parametric integrals of its own (§§IV.44–IV.45).
13. *The text uses $\wedge$ without defining it.* With the classical normalization the Leibniz sign $(-1)^k$ holds as we read it, and associativity and graded commutativity are identities between permutation sums that need no alternation (§IV.39).

### IV.27 Remark 4.1.3's (preprint 4.3) example: a retraction that loses a level

Remark 4.1.3 (preprint 4.3) says that "$X^1 \ne X$" is possible as M-polyfolds although the underlying sets agree, and gives no example. By Proposition 4.1.8 (preprint 4.8) in charts (§IV.4) an example is an sc-retract $O = r(U)$ with $O = O_\infty$ that is not an sc⁺-retract. It cannot come from a retraction whose level maps $U_m \to E_m$ are $C^1$ — the maps that Proposition 1.2.4 (preprint 1.19), Corollary 1.2.5 (preprint 1.20) and their composites produce: then $T_oO = Dr(o)E_0$ is finite-dimensional by a Baire category argument ($O$ is a countable union of the compact sets $\mathrm{cl}_0\{x \in O : |x|_1 \le n\}$), and the topologies of $O$ in $E_0$ and $E_1$ agree near $o$ (Findings 141; an argument, not formalized). ### IV.30 Lemma 4.1.11 (preprint 4.10): the section $u \mapsto (1 - p)(u - t(u))$ is sc-Fredholm

The text proves Proposition 4.1.9 (preprint 4.9) through this lemma and the implicit function theorem. The formalization proves the proposition directly (§§IV.6–IV.9), so the lemma is not used there. It is proved here on its own, in the 2021 notion of an sc-Fredholm section.

**Statement** ⟦Chapter4/RetractionSectionFredholm · lemma_4_1_11⟧. Take the data of Proposition 4.1.9 (preprint 4.9): an sc⁺-retraction $t : U \to U$ of a relatively open $U \subseteq C$ onto $O = t(U) \ni 0$, $N = T_0O$ in good position, a good complement $Y$ with $E = N \oplus Y$ an sc-splitting, and $p$ the projection onto $N$ along $Y$. Then the section $u \mapsto (u, (1 - p)(u - t(u)))$ of the local strong bundle $U \triangleleft Y \to U$ is an sc-Fredholm section in the sense of 2021 Definition 3.1.16: it is sc-smooth, it is regularizing, and it is an sc-Fredholm germ at every smooth point. It holds on all of $U$, not only near $0$. That $E = N \oplus Y$ is an sc-splitting is the text's "good sc-complement" (Definition 3.1.24 asks for an sc-complement), not an added hypothesis. $N$ is smooth and finite-dimensional by Lemma 4.1.7 (preprint 4.7) (§IV.3).

**The bundle and the section.** The local strong bundle $U \triangleleft Y$ lives over the M-polyfold $U$, which has one chart and the identity retraction. It is a strong bundle with one strong bundle chart ⟦Chapter4/LocalStrongBundle · LocalBundle.bundle⟧. The section ⟦Chapter4/LocalStrongBundle · LocalBundle.sec⟧ is sc-smooth ⟦Chapter4/LocalStrongBundle · LocalBundle.isScSmoothSection_sec⟧: $t$ is sc-smooth, and $1 - p$ is an sc-operator $E \to Y$ ⟦Chapter4/RetractionSectionSplitting · qOp⟧.

**Regularizing.** Let $u \in U_m$ with $(1 - p)(u - t(u)) \in Y_{m+1}$. Write
$$u = (1 - p)(u - t(u)) + t(u) + p(u - t(u)).$$
Here $t(u) \in E_{m+1}$ because $t$ is sc⁺, and $p(u - t(u)) \in N \subseteq E_\infty$. So $u \in E_{m+1}$.

**The germ at a smooth point $e$.** Go into the strong bundle chart centred at $e$ ⟦Chapter4/LocalStrongBundle · LocalBundle.centred⟧. Its retraction is pointwise the identity, so the principal part fills itself and no filler has to be built ⟦Chapter4/LocalStrongBundle · isFilling2021_of_R_eq_self⟧. In this chart the principal part is $g(v) = (1 - p)(v + e - t(v + e))$ ⟦Chapter4/LocalStrongBundle · LocalBundle.principal_centred⟧. The text's sc⁺-section is $s(v) = (1 - p)(e - t(v + e))$. It is sc⁺ because $t$ is, read as a map into $Y^1$. Then $g - s = (1 - p)v$, and it remains to conjugate this germ to a basic germ.

**The splitting $E = (N \oplus B) \oplus W$.** Let $\lambda$ be the coordinates of the partial quadrant. Their values on the smooth points of $Y$ already fill $\lambda(Y)$: the smooth points are dense and $\lambda(Y)$ is finite-dimensional ⟦Chapter4/RetractionSectionSplitting · map_le_map_smooth⟧. So there are smooth $e_1, \dots, e_d \in Y$ and functionals $\ell_j$ with $\ell_i(e_j) = \delta_{ij}$ such that, on $Y$, the $\ell_j$ vanish exactly where $\lambda$ does ⟦Chapter4/RetractionSectionSplitting · exists_splitData⟧. Put $B = \operatorname{span}(e_j)$, a smooth subspace inside $Y_\infty$, and $W = Y \cap \bigcap_j\ker\ell_j$ ⟦Chapter4/RetractionSectionSplitting · splitW⟧. $W$ is an sc-subspace ⟦Chapter4/RetractionSectionSplitting · isScSubspace_splitW⟧, and $Y = B \oplus W$. Adding a vector of $W$ changes no coordinate. So near $0$ the partial quadrant is $((N \oplus B) \cap C) \oplus W$, and the quadrant on $N \oplus B$ comes from the corner coordinates, not from good position ⟦Chapter4/RetractionSectionSplitting · isPartialQuadrant_preimage_quadrant⟧. In the coordinates $(a + b, w)$, $a \in N$, $b \in B$, $w \in W$, the germ $(1 - p)v$ becomes $(a + b, w) \mapsto (b, w)$. This is a basic germ with contraction part $0$ ⟦Chapter4/RetractionSectionSplitting · isConjugateToBasicGerm_qOp⟧.

The proof follows the text. ### IV.39 The pull-back $f^*$, the wedge product, and the graded differential algebra

**The pull-back** (p. 198; the 2017 text and the preprint state only that $f$ induces $f^*$). Let $f : X \to Y$ be sc-smooth. On tangent fibres, $Tf$ is a continuous linear map ⟦Chapter4/ScFormsPullback · MPolyfold.tangentFiberMap⟧. Define $f^*\omega(x)(\alpha_\bullet) = \omega(f(x))(Tf\alpha_\bullet)$ ⟦Chapter4/ScFormsPullback · MPolyfold.formPullback⟧. In charts it is $\omega_\psi \circ \oplus_kT(\psi f\varphi^{-1})$ ⟦Chapter4/ScFormsPullback · MPolyfold.chartForm_formPullback⟧, so it maps $\Omega^k(Y)$ to $\Omega^k(X)$ ⟦Chapter4/ScFormsPullback · MPolyfold.formPullback_mem_scForms⟧. It satisfies $d f^*\omega = (f^1)^*d\omega$ ⟦Chapter4/ScFormsPullback · MPolyfold.extD_formPullback⟧, by naturality of the local $d$ (§IV.36). The text leaves this identity to the reader; it holds as we read it. Pull-back is functorial ⟦Chapter4/ScFormsPullback · MPolyfold.formPullback_comp⟧ ⟦Chapter4/ScFormsPullback · MPolyfold.formPullback_id⟧ and commutes with (4.4) ⟦Chapter4/ScFormsPullback · MPolyfold.formLower_formPullback⟧. On the lifts, the maps $(f^i)^*$ ⟦Chapter4/ScFormsPullback · BundledMPolyfold.formsPullback⟧ pass to the limit ⟦Chapter4/ScFormsPullback · BundledMPolyfold.formsInftyPullback⟧. There $d f^* = f^* d$ ⟦Chapter4/ScFormsPullback · BundledMPolyfold.formsInftyD_comp_formsInftyPullback⟧, pull-back is functorial ⟦Chapter4/ScFormsPullback · BundledMPolyfold.formsInftyPullback_comp⟧, and it induces a map on $H^k_{sc}$ ⟦Chapter4/ScFormsPullback · BundledMPolyfold.scDeRhamPullback⟧.

**The wedge product.** The text uses $\wedge$ through "$(\Omega^*_\infty(X), d)$ is a graded differential algebra" but never defines it. We take the classical normalization:
$$(a \wedge b)(v) = (k!\,l!)^{-1}\sum_{\sigma \in S_{k+l}} \operatorname{sign}\sigma\, a(v_{\sigma(1)}, \dots, v_{\sigma(k)})\, b(v_{\sigma(k+1)}, \dots, v_{\sigma(k+l)})$$
⟦Chapter4/ScFormsWedge · wedgeFun⟧ ⟦Chapter4/ScFormsWedge · altWedge⟧ ⟦Chapter4/ScFormsWedge · coe_altWedge⟧. Write $\delta A(v) = \sum_j (-1)^jA(v_j)(v_0, \dots, \hat v_j, \dots)$ for the alternating sum that defines $d$. Two identities hold:
$$\delta(A \wedge b) = \delta A \wedge b, \qquad \delta(a \wedge B) = (-1)^k a \wedge \delta B$$
⟦Chapter4/ScFormsWedge · deltaFun_wedgeFun_left⟧ ⟦Chapter4/ScFormsWedge · deltaFun_wedgeFun_right⟧. They are identities between sums over permutations. They use the decomposition $S_{n+1} \cong \{0, \dots, n\} \times S_n$ by insertion, and they need no alternation. With the product rule, they give the local Leibniz rule ⟦Chapter4/ScFormsWedge · ambientD_lwedge⟧.

On an M-polyfold, the pointwise wedge ⟦Chapter4/ScFormsWedge · MPolyfold.formWedge⟧ maps $\Omega^k(X) \times \Omega^l(X)$ into $\Omega^{k+l}(X)$ ⟦Chapter4/ScFormsWedge · MPolyfold.formWedge_mem_scForms⟧. It satisfies
$$d(\omega \wedge \tau) = d\omega \wedge \iota^*\tau + (-1)^k\iota^*\omega \wedge d\tau$$
⟦Chapter4/ScFormsWedge · MPolyfold.extD_formWedge⟧ and $f^*(\omega \wedge \tau) = f^*\omega \wedge f^*\tau$ ⟦Chapter4/ScFormsWedge · MPolyfold.formPullback_formWedge⟧. It passes to $\Omega^*_\infty(X)$ ⟦Chapter4/ScFormsWedge · BundledMPolyfold.formsInftyWedge⟧ ⟦Chapter4/ScFormsWedge · BundledMPolyfold.formsInftyD_formsInftyWedge⟧.

Associativity and graded commutativity are again identities between permutation sums ⟦Chapter4/ScFormsWedgeAssociativity · wedgeFun_assoc⟧ ⟦Chapter4/ScFormsWedgeAssociativity · wedgeFun_comm⟧. The sign $(-1)^{kl}$ is that of a rotation of $k + l$ places by $k$. The unit is the constant $1$ ⟦Chapter4/ScFormsWedgeAssociativity · MPolyfold.formOne_mem_scForms⟧. On $\Omega^*_\infty(X)$ ⟦Chapter4/ScFormsWedgeAssociativity · BundledMPolyfold.formsInftyWedge_assoc⟧ ⟦Chapter4/ScFormsWedgeAssociativity · BundledMPolyfold.formsInftyWedge_comm⟧, they make $\bigoplus_k\Omega^k_\infty(X)$ a graded $\mathbb R$-algebra ⟦Chapter4/ScFormsWedgeAssociativity · BundledMPolyfold.formsInftyGAlgebra⟧ that is graded commutative ⟦Chapter4/ScFormsWedgeAssociativity · BundledMPolyfold.formsInftyGraded_of_mul_of_comm⟧. $d$ is a derivation of degree $+1$ ⟦Chapter4/ScFormsWedgeAssociativity · BundledMPolyfold.formsInftyGradedD_of_mul_of⟧ with $d^2 = 0$ (§IV.37). This is the graded differential algebra of p. 198. $f^*$ is a morphism of graded algebras ⟦Chapter4/ScFormsWedge · BundledMPolyfold.formsInftyPullback_formsInftyWedge⟧ ⟦Chapter4/ScFormsWedgeAssociativity · BundledMPolyfold.formsInftyPullback_formsInftyOne⟧. The Leibniz sign $(-1)^k$ holds as we read it.

### IV.40 Forms on $\partial X$, $j^*$, and the relative complex (pp. 198–199)

For a tame $X$ the text says that the inclusion $\partial X \to X$ is sc-smooth on local faces, so differential forms on $\partial X$ "make sense". It gives no definition. $\partial X$ is not an M-polyfold (Findings, Chapter 4, item 122).

**The reading.** Forms on $\partial X$ are read through the tame boundary $B : bX \to X$ of Theorem 2.7.13, an sc-smooth map from an M-polyfold that is locally the inclusion of a local face. Let $f : X \to Y$ be sc-smooth. A family $\tau$ on $X$ *agrees over $f$* ⟦Chapter4/BoundaryForms · MPolyfold.IsAgreeingOver⟧ if $\tau_p(v) = \tau_{p'}(v')$ whenever $f(p) = f(p')$ and $Tf\,v_j = Tf\,v'_j$ for all $j$. The base-point condition matters in degree $0$. A form on $\partial X^i$ is an sc-form on $bX^i$ that agrees over $B^i$ ⟦Chapter4/BoundaryForms · MPolyfold.IsFaceCompatible⟧. So on two local faces through a point, the form coincides on vectors with the same image in $TX$ (reading R-a; Findings, Chapter 4, item 173).

The definition ⟦Chapter4/BoundaryForms · MPolyfold.bForms⟧ also lists the agreement of $d\tau$, which makes $\Omega^*(\partial X)$ the largest $d$-stable family of agreeing forms ⟦Chapter4/BoundaryForms · MPolyfold.le_bForms⟧. With it, $d$ maps $\Omega^k(\partial X^i)$ to $\Omega^{k+1}(\partial X^{i+1})$ ⟦Chapter4/BoundaryForms · MPolyfold.extD_mem_bForms⟧.

**The agreement of $d\tau$ follows from that of $\tau$** ⟦Chapter4/BoundaryFormsAgreement · MPolyfold.isFaceCompatible_extD⟧. So the extra condition is automatic, and $\Omega^k(\partial X^i)$ is the set of agreeing forms ⟦Chapter4/BoundaryFormsAgreement · MPolyfold.bForms_eq_agreeForms⟧ ⟦Chapter4/BoundaryFormsAgreement · MPolyfold.mem_bForms_iff⟧. The general statement is for any sc-smooth $f$ with two properties ⟦Chapter4/BoundaryFormsAgreementGeneral · BundledMPolyfold.isAgreeingOver_extD⟧:
- $Tf$ is injective on each tangent space ⟦Chapter4/BoundaryFormsAgreementGeneral · BundledMPolyfold.IsTangentInjective⟧;
- $f$ has a *corner cover* ⟦Chapter4/BoundaryFormsAgreementGeneral · BundledMPolyfold.HasCornerCover⟧. For $p \ne p'$ with $f(p) = f(p')$, there are sc-smooth $g, g' : Z \to X$ with $f \circ g = f \circ g'$ and $g(z) = p$, $g'(z) = p'$ such that every pair of tangent vectors at $p$, $p'$ with the same image is $(Tg\,v, Tg'\,v)$.

Given these, $g^*\tau = g'^*\tau$, hence $g^*d\tau = g'^*d\tau$ by $df^* = f^*d$ ⟦Chapter4/BoundaryFormsAgreementGeneral · MPolyfold.formPullback_extD_eq_of_isAgreeingOver⟧. The two properties are stated on level $0$ and transported to every $X^i$.

For $B$, $TB$ is the face inclusion in tangent charts ⟦Chapter4/BoundaryFormsAgreement · MPolyfold.tangentChartMap_bdryMap⟧, so it is injective ⟦Chapter4/BoundaryFormsAgreement · MPolyfold.isTangentInjective_bdryMap⟧. The corner cover is built as follows ⟦Chapter4/BoundaryFormsAgreement · MPolyfold.hasCornerCover_bdryMap⟧:
1. At two faces through $x$, take a tame chart with retraction $r$ and coordinates $\lambda_i$, $\lambda_j$ vanishing on them ⟦Chapter4/BoundaryFormsAgreement · MPolyfold.CornerChart⟧.
2. The map $r \circ P_i \circ P_j$, where $P_i$ kills $\lambda_i$, sends a relatively open piece of the partial quadrant into both faces.
3. Its two lifts to $bX$ have the same composite with $B$.
4. Every vector of $T_xF \cap T_xF'$ is in the image of its tangent map ⟦Chapter4/BoundaryFormsAgreement · MPolyfold.CornerChart.exists_tangent⟧, since $D(r P_iP_j)(\varphi x)h = Dr(\varphi x)h = h$ for $h$ with $\lambda_i h = \lambda_j h = 0$.

No corner strata are built as M-polyfolds, and the point $x$ need not be smooth. The text writes $d\tau$ without comment; this holds as we read it.

**$j^*$.** The pull-back along $B$, by §IV.39, maps $\Omega^k(X)$ into $\Omega^k(\partial X)$ ⟦Chapter4/BoundaryForms · MPolyfold.formsPullback_mem_bForms⟧. It passes to the limits ⟦Chapter4/BoundaryForms · MPolyfold.bFormsInfty⟧ ⟦Chapter4/BoundaryForms · MPolyfold.jStar⟧, with $dj^* = j^*d$ ⟦Chapter4/BoundaryForms · MPolyfold.jStar_comp_D⟧. The text states this identity without proof (Findings, Chapter 4, item 121).

**The relative complex.** In degree $0$ it is $\Omega^0_\infty(X)$, and in degree $k + 1$ it is $\Omega^{k+1}_\infty(X) \times \Omega^k_\infty(\partial X)$ ⟦Chapter4/BoundaryForms · MPolyfold.RelForms⟧. The differential is $d(\omega, \tau) = (d\omega, j^*\omega - d\tau)$ ⟦Chapter4/BoundaryForms · MPolyfold.relD⟧. It squares to $0$ ⟦Chapter4/BoundaryForms · MPolyfold.relD_comp_relD⟧, because $d^2 = 0$ and $dj^* = j^*d$. $H^k_{dR}(X, \partial X)$ is its cohomology ⟦Chapter4/BoundaryForms · MPolyfold.relDeRham⟧ ⟦Chapter4/BoundaryForms · MPolyfold.relDeRhamMk_eq_iff⟧. If $\partial X = \emptyset$, then $bX$ is empty and $H^k_{dR}(X, \partial X) \cong H^k_{sc}(X)$ ⟦Chapter4/BoundaryForms · MPolyfold.relDeRhamEquivOfNoBoundary⟧. The text calls the relative complex a differential algebra. The wedge product on it is §IV.46.

### IV.41 Integration and Stokes' theorem on quadrants

Theorem 4.4.7 (preprint 4.28) integrates over finite-dimensional sub-M-polyfolds with corners. The integration is built from the bottom: first in $\mathbb R^{n+1}$, then on Mathlib manifolds with corners (§IV.42), then for sc-forms (§IV.43).

**Top forms in $\mathbb R^{n+1}$.** For a top-degree form $\omega$, define $\int_s\omega = \int_s\omega(x)(e_0, \dots, e_n)\,dx$ on the standard frame ⟦Chapter4/EuclideanForms · stdFrame⟧ ⟦Chapter4/EuclideanForms · topFormIntegral⟧. Pull-back ⟦Chapter4/EuclideanForms · formPullback⟧ multiplies the frame value by the determinant ⟦Chapter4/EuclideanForms · alternatingMap_comp_eq_det_mul⟧. So the change of variables formula holds with the sign of the determinant ⟦Chapter4/EuclideanForms · topFormIntegral_image⟧.

**Faces and their orientation.** The quadrant is $Q_S = \{x_i \ge 0, i \in S\}$. Its boundary differs from that of the open quadrant by a null set ⟦Chapter4/EuclideanForms · openQuadrant_ae_eq_quadrant⟧, and the corners are null ⟦Chapter4/EuclideanForms · image_faceEmbedding_openQuadrant⟧. The face $\{x_i = 0\}$ is parametrized by $\iota_i : \mathbb R^n \to \mathbb R^{n+1}$, inserting a zero at place $i$ ⟦Chapter4/EuclideanForms · faceEmbedding⟧. It carries the pulled-back form ⟦Chapter4/EuclideanForms · facePullback⟧ and the sign $(-1)^{i+1}$ ⟦Chapter4/EuclideanForms · faceSign⟧. This is the sign of the frame $(-e_i, \text{face frame})$ ⟦Chapter4/EuclideanForms · det_cons_neg_stdFrame_faceFrame⟧, the induced orientation with the outward vector $-e_i$ first (2021 Definition 9.3.8).

**Stokes on a quadrant** ⟦Chapter4/EuclideanStokes · stokes_quadrant⟧. For a $C^1$ form $\omega$ of degree $n$ vanishing off a compact set,
$$\int_{Q_S} d\omega = \sum_{i \in S}(-1)^{i+1}\int_{\mathbb R^n_{\ge 0}}\iota_i^*\omega.$$
The engine is Mathlib's divergence theorem on a box ⟦Chapter4/EuclideanStokes · integral_divergence_piQuadrant⟧. Take the box $[a, b]$ with $a_i = 0$ for $i \in S$, $a_i = -R$ otherwise, and $b_i = R$. The faces $x_i = -R$ and $x_i = R$ contribute nothing, because $\omega$ vanishes there. A form defined only on an open $U$ is extended by $0$ ⟦Chapter4/EuclideanStokes · stokes_quadrant_inter_open⟧.

### IV.42 Forms, integrals and Stokes' theorem on Mathlib manifolds with corners

**Forms and $d$.** A $k$-form on a manifold $M$ modelled on $[0, \infty)^{n+1}$ is a family of alternating forms on the tangent spaces ⟦Chapter4/ManifoldForms · ManifoldForm⟧. It has chart representatives ⟦Chapter4/ManifoldForms · chartForm⟧, which are pull-backs of one another ⟦Chapter4/ManifoldForms · chartForm_eq_formPullback⟧, and a smoothness class ⟦Chapter4/ManifoldForms · ContMDiffForm⟧. $d$ is the derivative of the chart representative within the range of the model ⟦Chapter4/ManifoldForms · ManifoldForm.extDeriv⟧. It commutes with pull-back by chart changes ⟦Chapter4/ManifoldForms · extDerivWithin_formPullback⟧, so it has the right representative in every chart ⟦Chapter4/ManifoldForms · chartForm_extDeriv⟧.

**Integration.** The interval $[0, 1]$, with the quadrant model, has no oriented atlas: a chart at $1$ reverses the orientation. So the charts are signed ⟦Chapter4/ManifoldIntegral · signedOr⟧ ⟦Chapter4/ManifoldIntegral · orSign⟧. The integral in one chart carries the sign of the orientation there ⟦Chapter4/ManifoldIntegral · chartIntegral⟧ ⟦Chapter4/ManifoldIntegral · chartIntegral_eq_integral_sign⟧. *Integration data* ⟦Chapter4/ManifoldIntegral · IntegrationData⟧ consist of finitely many charts, regions on which the orientation has a constant sign, and $C^\infty$ functions $\rho_j$ summing to $1$ on the compact support. They exist ⟦Chapter4/ManifoldIntegral · exists_integrationData⟧, by a subordinate smooth partition of unity. Any two give the same sum ⟦Chapter4/ManifoldIntegral · IntegrationData.integral_eq⟧, by the change of variables in the overlaps. This defines $\int_M\omega$ ⟦Chapter4/ManifoldIntegral · ManifoldForm.integral⟧ ⟦Chapter4/ManifoldIntegral · ManifoldForm.integral_eq_integrationData⟧. The integral is additive, and it changes sign with the orientation ⟦Chapter4/ManifoldIntegral · ManifoldForm.integral_neg_orientation⟧.

**The boundary integral.** In one chart, the boundary integral is $\sum_i(-1)^{i+1}\int\iota_i^*\omega_e$ ⟦Chapter4/ManifoldStokes · chartBoundaryIntegral⟧. Through integration data this defines $\int_{\partial M}\omega$ ⟦Chapter4/ManifoldStokes · ManifoldForm.boundaryIntegral⟧. It does not depend on the data ⟦Chapter4/ManifoldStokes · IntegrationData.boundaryIntegral_eq⟧. The proof of independence goes through Stokes: both sums equal $\int_M d\omega$ for an extension. The orientation of the faces has to be compatible across charts. The chart change maps a face to a face ⟦Chapter4/ManifoldBoundary · exists_face_chartChange⟧, inward vectors to inward vectors, with $(De_i)_j > 0$. The sign identity $\mathrm{faceSign}(i)\det A = (Ae_i)_j\,\mathrm{faceSign}(j)\det\bar A$, where $\bar A$ is the face restriction ⟦Chapter4/ManifoldStokes · faceSign_mul_det_eq⟧, gives the compatibility ⟦Chapter4/ManifoldBoundary · orientation_face_chartChange⟧.

**Stokes' theorem** ⟦Chapter4/ManifoldStokes · stokes_of_compactSpace⟧. For $M$ compact with a continuous orientation and $\omega$ of class $C^1$, $\int_M d\omega = \int_{\partial M}\omega$. The proof has two steps:
1. Stokes in one chart is §IV.41 ⟦Chapter4/ManifoldStokes · chartIntegral_extDeriv_eq⟧.
2. With integration data, $d\omega = \sum_j d(\rho_j\omega)$, so the general case is the sum of the chart cases ⟦Chapter4/ManifoldStokes · IntegrationData.stokes⟧ ⟦Chapter4/ManifoldStokes · integral_extDeriv_eq_boundaryIntegral⟧.

**Forms on the faces of $M$.** These are face data in the charts ⟦Chapter4/ManifoldFaceForms · IsChartFaceForm⟧ ⟦Chapter4/ManifoldFaceForms · ChartFaceForm⟧. On each chart face there is a smooth form, compatible under chart changes at points of degeneracy index $1$, and agreeing at corners on vectors tangent to both faces. Every smooth form restricts to such data ⟦Chapter4/ManifoldFaceForms · ChartFaceForm.ofForm⟧, so they are satisfiable. Compactly supported face data extend to a form on $M$ ⟦Chapter4/ManifoldFaceForms · exists_extension⟧, by an inclusion–exclusion sum over the faces of box charts. This defines the integral of face data as the boundary integral of an extension ⟦Chapter4/ManifoldFaceForms · ChartFaceForm.integral⟧. It also gives $\int_{\partial M}d\tau = 0$ ⟦Chapter4/ManifoldFaceForms · ChartFaceForm.integral_d_eq_zero⟧, by Stokes and $d^2 = 0$ ⟦Chapter4/ManifoldFaceForms · ManifoldForm.extDeriv_extDeriv⟧.

### IV.43 Theorem 4.4.7 (preprint 4.28): Stokes' theorem for sc-forms, with the sum over the faces

**Statement** ⟦Chapter9/StokesFaceSum · MPolyfold.theorem_4_4_7_hyp⟧. Let $X$ be an M-polyfold and $S \subseteq X$ a compact sub-M-polyfold. Assume:
- $S$ has a corner structure of dimension $n + 1$ (Definition 4.1.1 (preprint 4.1));
- $S$ is face-structured;
- $S$ has a continuous tangent orientation $o$.

Then for $\omega \in \Omega^n_\infty(X)$,
$$\int_S (d\omega)|_S = \sum_F \int_F (\omega|_S)|_F,$$
the sum over the finitely many faces $F$ of $S$. Each face is a manifold modelled on $[0, \infty)^n$ with the induced orientation, outward vector first.

The text's "boundary $\partial N$, a union of smooth faces $F$" is read as: $S$ is face-structured (Definition 2.5.10), so every point lies on exactly $d_S(x)$ faces. This hypothesis is added; it is not derived from the text. It is satisfiable. It holds when $S$ has no boundary ⟦Chapter9/StokesFaceSum · MPolyfold.isFaceStructured_of_forall_degIndex_eq_zero⟧, and then the theorem says $\int_S d\omega = 0$. It also holds for the quadrants ⟦Chapter11/EpEquivalenceGeometryFaces · isFaceStructured_quadrant⟧. The corner structure and the continuity of $o$ are the meaning of "a finite-dimensional oriented manifold with boundary with corners" in the text. The 2021 text writes $\int_N$, the preprint and 2017 texts $\int_M$ (Findings, Chapter 4, items 123, 171, 172).

**Step 1: the restriction of sc-forms to $S$.** The corner structure makes $S$ a Mathlib manifold, and $S \subseteq X_\infty$ ⟦Chapter6/MathlibFormsBridge · MPolyfold.subset_smoothSet_of_hasCornerStructureOfDim⟧. An sc-form $\omega$ on $X^i$ restricts to a classical form on $S$ ⟦Chapter6/MathlibFormsBridge · MPolyfold.scFormRestrict⟧, through the pull-back of §IV.39 along the inclusion lifted to $S \to X^i$. This works because $S = S^1 = S^i$ as sets. In a chart of $X$ the restricted form is $\omega_\varphi$ evaluated on the model derivatives of the corner chart ⟦Chapter6/MathlibFormsBridge · MPolyfold.scFormRestrict_apply_modelDeriv⟧. It commutes with (4.4) ⟦Chapter6/MathlibFormsBridge · MPolyfold.scFormRestrict_formLower⟧ and passes to $\Omega_\infty$ ⟦Chapter6/MathlibFormsBridge · MPolyfold.formsInftyRestrict⟧. It is $C^\infty$ ⟦Chapter6/MathlibFormsBridge · MPolyfold.contMDiffForm_formsInftyRestrict⟧, and $d(\omega|_S) = (d\omega)|_S$ ⟦Chapter6/MathlibFormsBridge · MPolyfold.extDeriv_formsInftyRestrict⟧. With §IV.42 this gives $\int_S (d\omega)|_S = \int_{\partial S}\omega|_S$ ⟦Chapter6/MathlibFormsBridge · MPolyfold.stokes_formsInftyRestrict⟧. Such $S$ exist for every tame $X$ with a submanifold in good position ⟦Chapter6/MathlibFormsBridge · exists_hasCornerStructureOfDim_of_tame⟧.

**Step 2: the faces of $S$.** A compact tame M-polyfold has finitely many faces ⟦Chapter4/StokesFaces · MPolyfold.finite_setOf_isFace⟧. In a face-structured one, near each of its points and in any chart modelled on a tame retract, a face is exactly a coordinate face ⟦Chapter4/StokesFaces · MPolyfold.exists_face_eq_coordHyper⟧. In corner coordinates of $S$ this gives, at $x \in F$, a coordinate $i$ and an open set on which $F = \{y_i = 0\}$ ⟦Chapter6/StokesFaceCharts · MPolyfold.CornerCoords.exists_faceData⟧. Removing the coordinate $i$ gives a corner chart of $F$. So *every face has a corner structure of dimension $n$* ⟦Chapter6/StokesFaceCharts · MPolyfold.hasCornerStructureOfDim_face⟧, as the sub-M-polyfold of Theorem 2.5.17.

**Step 3: the induced orientation.** Take a chart of a face and the chart of $S$ it comes from. The chart change of two such pairs has derivative the face restriction of the chart change of $S$ ⟦Chapter4/StokesFaceOrientation · FaceChartPair.chartChangeDeriv_face⟧. This derivative maps the face to the face and inward vectors to inward vectors, at every point, corners included ⟦Chapter4/StokesFaceOrientation · FaceChartPair.inward⟧. The orientation induced on the face reads, in every chart pair, as the sign of $o$ times $(-1)^{i+1}$ ⟦Chapter4/StokesFaceOrientation · IsInducedOrientation⟧. It exists, is continuous and is unique ⟦Chapter4/StokesFaceOrientation · inducedOrientation⟧ ⟦Chapter4/StokesFaceOrientation · isContinuous_inducedOrientation⟧ ⟦Chapter4/StokesFaceOrientation · IsInducedOrientation.unique⟧. On $S$ it is ⟦Chapter9/StokesFaceSum · MPolyfold.faceOrientation⟧.

**Step 4: the face sum is the boundary integral** ⟦Chapter4/StokesFaceIntegral · sum_integral_restrictForm_eq_boundaryIntegral⟧. The form $\omega|_F$ is the pull-back along $F \hookrightarrow S$ ⟦Chapter4/StokesFaceIntegral · restrictForm⟧. Its representative in a face chart is $\iota_i^*$ of that of $\omega$ ⟦Chapter4/StokesFaceIntegral · FaceChartPair.chartForm_restrictForm⟧. Locally, on a *good region*, the face sum equals the chart boundary integral ⟦Chapter4/StokesFaceIntegral · GoodRegion.sum_integral_eq⟧. A good region is a signed region inside the face chart domains and the neighbourhood of Proposition 2.7.5 (3) ⟦Chapter9/StokesFaceSum · MPolyfold.goodRegion⟧. Inside it, distinct faces through a point get distinct coordinates, because a point of degeneracy index $1$ lies on exactly one face ⟦Chapter9/StokesFaceSum · MPolyfold.face_eq_of_degIndex_eq_one⟧. Integration data subordinate to good regions ⟦Chapter4/StokesFaceIntegral · ManifoldForm.exists_integrationData_of_regions⟧ sum the local identities. With Step 1 the theorem follows.

### IV.44 Parametric integrals, and Lemma 4.5.2: the Poincaré lemma on a convex piece of a partial quadrant

**Parametric integrals** ⟦Chapter4/ParametricIntegrals · isSck_intervalIntegral⟧ ⟦Chapter4/ParametricIntegrals · isScSmooth_intervalIntegral⟧. If $g$ is sc$^k$ (sc-smooth) on $[0, 1] \times U$, with $[0, 1]$ carrying the constant sc-structure, then $x \mapsto \int_0^1 g(t, x)\,dt$ is sc$^k$ (sc-smooth) on $U$. Its derivative is the integral of the partial derivative ⟦Chapter4/ParametricIntegrals · scDeriv_intervalIntegral⟧. The proof is an induction on $k$ through the tangent map, $T\int g = \int Tg$. The derivative is differentiation under the integral sign, uniform by the compactness of $[0, 1]$, within the relatively open $U$. The level-wise $C^{l+1}$ criteria lose one level too many and are not used.

**The homotopy operator** (2021 p. 203) ⟦Chapter5/Poincare · homotopyForm⟧. For a closed $(k+1)$-form $\omega$ on a convex $D$ and a centre $c$, put
$$\tau(y)(v_1, \dots, v_k) = \int_0^1 t^k\,\omega(c + t(y - c))(y - c, v_1, \dots, v_k)\,dt.$$
$\tau$ is sc-smooth if $c$ is smooth ⟦Chapter5/Poincare · isScSmooth_homotopyForm⟧: the integrand is sc-smooth on $[0, 1] \times D$ because $y \mapsto c + t(y - c)$ is, on every level. Then $d\tau = \omega$ on $D^1$ ⟦Chapter5/Poincare · ambientD_homotopyForm⟧. This is the classical computation:
1. Write $d\tau(y)(v_0, \dots, v_k)$ with the alternating sum $\delta$ of §IV.39.
2. Use $d\omega = 0$ to trade the alternating sum of base derivatives for the radial derivative of $\omega$.
3. The integrand becomes $\frac{d}{dt}\bigl(t^{k+1}\omega(c + t(y - c))(v_0, \dots, v_k)\bigr)$, whose integral is $\omega(y)(v_\bullet)$.

The vectors are taken on level $1$ at points of $D^1$, so no density argument is needed.

### IV.45 Lemma 4.4.8 (2021, p. 199): the Poincaré lemma on an M-polyfold

**Forms on open subsets.** An open $U \subseteq X$ is an M-polyfold (§IV.34). Restriction maps $\Omega^k(X)$ to $\Omega^k(U)$ ⟦Chapter5/OpenForms · MPolyfold.formRestrict⟧ and commutes with $d$ ⟦Chapter5/OpenForms · MPolyfold.extD_formRestrict⟧. On the limits, for nonempty $U$ as a bundled M-polyfold ⟦Chapter5/OpenForms · BundledMPolyfold.openSub⟧, there are restrictions $\Omega^k_\infty(X) \to \Omega^k_\infty(U)$ and $\Omega^k_\infty(U) \to \Omega^k_\infty(V)$ for $V \subseteq U$ ⟦Chapter5/OpenForms · BundledMPolyfold.formsInftyResOpen⟧ ⟦Chapter5/OpenForms · BundledMPolyfold.formsInftyResOpenOpen⟧. The text's "$[\omega] = [\tau]$ on $V$" of (4.7) ⟦Chapter5/OpenForms · BundledMPolyfold.FormsInftyEqOn⟧ means: for representatives on one level $i$, the restrictions agree on $V^\ell$ for some $\ell \ge i$ ⟦Chapter5/OpenForms · BundledMPolyfold.formsInftyEqOn_of_iff⟧.

**Statement** ⟦Chapter5/Poincare · lemma_4_4_8⟧. Every $x \in X$ has open sets $V(x) \subseteq U(x)$ with the following property. For every $k$, every closed $a \in \Omega^{k+1}_\infty(U(x))$ has $b \in \Omega^k_\infty(V(x))$ with $db = a|_{V(x)}$. The text's $k \ge 1$ is $k + 1$ here.

Put $U(x) = \varphi^{-1}(B_\varepsilon(\varphi x))$ and $V(x) = \varphi^{-1}(B_\delta(\varphi x))$. Since $r$ is the identity on the model, this is the text's $r(B_\delta \cap r^{-1}(B_\delta))$.
2. **The two maps.** $D = B_\delta(\varphi x) \cap C$ is convex and relatively open, with a smooth point ⟦Chapter5/Poincare448 · exists_smooth_mem_relOpen⟧. ### IV.46 The wedge product on the forms on $\partial X$ and on the relative complex (gap G.80)

The text (p. 199) calls $\Omega^*_\infty(X, \partial X)$ a differential algebra and gives no product. Two products are formalized.

**On the forms on $\partial X$.** A form on $\partial X$ is an sc-form on $bX$ that agrees over $B$ (§IV.40). The wedge product of two forms that agree over an sc-smooth $f$ agrees over $f$, because at a point it is computed from the values of the two forms on the same tangent vectors ⟦Chapter4/BoundaryWedge · MPolyfold.IsAgreeingOver.formWedge⟧ ⟦Chapter4/BoundaryWedge · BundledMPolyfold.agreeDFormsWedge⟧. It passes to the limits ⟦Chapter4/BoundaryWedge · BundledMPolyfold.agreeDFormsInftyWedge⟧ ⟦Chapter4/BoundaryWedge · BundledMPolyfold.agreeDFormsInftyWedge_of_of⟧ and, up to the casts of degrees ⟦Chapter4/BoundaryWedge · BundledMPolyfold.agreeDFormsInftyCast⟧, it satisfies the Leibniz rule ⟦Chapter4/BoundaryWedge · BundledMPolyfold.agreeDFormsInftyD_agreeDFormsInftyWedge⟧, is associative ⟦Chapter4/BoundaryWedge · BundledMPolyfold.agreeDFormsInftyWedge_assoc⟧ and has the constant $1$ as a unit on both sides ⟦Chapter4/BoundaryWedge · BundledMPolyfold.agreeDFormsInftyOne⟧ ⟦Chapter4/BoundaryWedge · BundledMPolyfold.agreeDFormsInftyWedge_agreeDFormsInftyOne⟧ ⟦Chapter4/BoundaryWedge · BundledMPolyfold.agreeDFormsInftyCast_agreeDFormsInftyOne_wedge⟧. The inclusion into $\Omega^*_\infty(bX)$ is multiplicative ⟦Chapter4/BoundaryWedge · BundledMPolyfold.agreeDFormsInftyIncl_agreeDFormsInftyWedge⟧, and so is the pull-back ⟦Chapter4/BoundaryWedge · BundledMPolyfold.agreeInftyPullback_formsInftyWedge⟧. For $f = B$ this is the wedge product on $\Omega^*_\infty(\partial X)$, and $j^*(\omega \wedge \omega') = j^*\omega \wedge j^*\omega'$ ⟦Chapter4/BoundaryWedge · MPolyfold.jStar_formsInftyWedge⟧.

**On the relative complex: the product of the mapping cone of $j^*$.** For $(\omega, \tau)$ of degree $k$ and $(\omega', \tau')$ of degree $l$,
$$(\omega, \tau)(\omega', \tau') = (\omega \wedge \omega', (-1)^k\, j^*\omega \wedge \tau')$$
⟦Chapter4/RelativeWedge · MPolyfold.relWedge⟧ ⟦Chapter4/RelativeWedge · MPolyfold.relFst_relWedge⟧ ⟦Chapter4/RelativeWedge · MPolyfold.relSnd_relWedge_succ⟧ ⟦Chapter4/RelativeWedge · MPolyfold.relSnd_relWedge_zero⟧. This choice is ours: the text gives no product, and this is the usual product on the mapping cone of a multiplicative map of complexes.
- *$d$ is a derivation of degree $+1$:* $d(xy) = (dx)y + (-1)^k x\,dy$ ⟦Chapter4/RelativeWedge · MPolyfold.relD_relWedge⟧. With $d(\omega, \tau) = (d\omega, j^*\omega - d\tau)$ (§IV.40), both sides have first component $d\omega \wedge \omega' + (-1)^k\omega \wedge d\omega'$ and second component $j^*\omega \wedge j^*\omega' - (-1)^k j^*d\omega \wedge \tau' - j^*\omega \wedge d\tau'$, by the Leibniz rules on $X$ and on $\partial X$ and the multiplicativity of $j^*$.
- *Associativity* ⟦Chapter4/RelativeWedge · MPolyfold.relWedge_assoc⟧, from the associativity on $X$ and on $\partial X$ and $j^*(\omega \wedge \omega') = j^*\omega \wedge j^*\omega'$.
- *The unit.* $(1, 0)$ is a unit on the left ⟦Chapter4/RelativeWedge · MPolyfold.relOne⟧ ⟦Chapter4/RelativeWedge · MPolyfold.relCast_relWedge_relOne⟧. On the right, $(\omega, \tau)(1, 0) = (\omega, 0)$ ⟦Chapter4/RelativeWedge · MPolyfold.relWedge_relOne⟧, so it is not a unit there; relative cohomology has no unit in general either. The product is not graded commutative on forms.
- *The graded ring.* With Mathlib's graded multiplication ⟦Chapter4/RelativeWedge · MPolyfold.relGMul⟧ ⟦Chapter4/RelativeWedge · MPolyfold.relGNonUnitalNonAssocSemiring⟧ ⟦Chapter4/RelativeWedge · MPolyfold.relGraded_of_mul_of⟧ and associativity ⟦Chapter4/RelativeWedge · MPolyfold.relGraded_mul_assoc⟧, $\bigoplus_k \Omega^k_\infty(X, \partial X)$ is an associative ring without unit ⟦Chapter4/RelativeWedge · MPolyfold.relGradedNonUnitalRing⟧. The differential on it ⟦Chapter4/RelativeWedge · MPolyfold.relGradedD⟧ squares to $0$ ⟦Chapter4/RelativeWedge · MPolyfold.relGradedD_comp_self⟧ and is a derivation ⟦Chapter4/RelativeWedge · MPolyfold.relGradedD_of_mul_of⟧. The projection to $\Omega^*_\infty(X)$ is multiplicative.

So the relative complex is read as a differential graded algebra without a right unit (V4.91). The pull-back of forms on $\partial X$ along a general sc-smooth map is not formalized; the one use the text makes of it is (§IV.25). Gap G.80 is closed (Findings, Chapter 4, item 178).
