## Part XVI. Polyfolds (HWZ Chapter 16, §§16.1–16.6)

Chapter 16 (Springer 2021, pp. 705–740; 2017 numbers in parentheses) passes from ep-groupoids to polyfolds. A polyfold is a topological space $Z$ together with an equivalence class of ep-groupoid structures on it, and every notion of the earlier chapters (bump functions, partitions of unity, the degeneracy index, tameness, the tangent, sc-differential forms, strong bundles, auxiliary norms, sections, branched ep⁺-subgroupoids, sc⁺-multisections, sc-Fredholm sections, orientations and the invariants of Chapter 15) is carried to the class. The method is uniform: a notion of a polyfold is a notion of one structure in the class, and a theorem of invariance under equivalences of ep-groupoids, or under generalized isomorphisms, shows that it does not depend on the structure. §16.1 defines polyfold structures, their equivalence and polyfolds (Definitions 16.1.1–16.1.3 (2017: Definitions 16.1–16.3)), shows that bump functions and partitions of unity are invariant (Proposition 16.1.4, Definition 16.1.5 (2017: Proposition 16.2, Definition 16.4)), defines the maps between polyfolds (Definitions 16.1.6, 16.1.8, 16.1.10, 16.1.11, Remarks 16.1.7, 16.1.9 (2017: Definitions 16.5–16.8, Remarks 16.1, 16.2)), transports the degeneracy index, the boundary and tameness (Lemma 16.1.13, Definitions 16.1.14, 16.1.17, Lemma 16.1.19, Definition 16.1.20 (2017: Lemma 16.1, Definitions 16.9, 16.11, Lemma 16.2, Definition 16.12)), and introduces the raised polyfolds $(Z, c)^i$. It also treats the lifts $Z_i$, subpolyfolds and faces (Proposition 16.1.12, Definition 16.1.15, Remark 16.1.16, Example 16.1.18, Definition 16.1.21, Proposition 16.1.22), which are not formalized. §16.2 builds the tangent $T(Z, c)$, the tangent functor and the complex of sc-differential forms. §16.3 defines strong polyfold bundles, their maps, auxiliary norms, reflexivity, mixed convergence and sections. §16.4 defines branched finite-dimensional suborbifolds of a polyfold, their orientations and integrals, and proves Stokes' theorem. §16.5 defines sc⁺-multisections of strong polyfold bundles and proves the extension theorem 16.5.7. §16.6 defines sc-Fredholm sections, their compactness properties and orientations, and proves the integration theorem 16.6.6.

**The decisions that shape the chapter.**

(1) *Polyfolds as quotients.* A polyfold structure is a Lean structure holding the ep-groupoid, its two properties (paracompact orbit space, (ZHOU)) and the homeomorphism; a polyfold on $Z$ is an element of the quotient of the polyfold structures on $Z$ by the equivalence of Definition 16.1.2. Every notion of a polyfold is "some structure in $c$ has the property", and it comes with the theorem that then every structure in $c$ has it. The same pattern gives strong polyfold bundles (quotients of strong polyfold bundle structures), sc-smooth maps (quotients of morphisms of PS), forms, sections, branched suborbifolds and multisections (quotients of representatives).

(2) *Bump functions on the objects.* "Admits sc-smooth bump functions" in Proposition 16.1.4 (1) and Definition 16.1.5 (1) is read for the object M-polyfold of the ep-groupoid, pointwise (Definition 5.3.1), as the text's proof uses it; by Theorem 12.3.2 (1) (Part XII) this is equivalent to Definition 12.3.1 in the pointwise reading. Partitions of unity are read in the ep-groupoid sense of Definition 7.5.5, and shown equivalent to partitions of unity of the orbit space whose pull-backs to the objects are sc-smooth.

(3) *A base structure instead of a gluing.* The text builds $T(Z, c)$, and the raised polyfold $(Z, c)^1$, by gluing over all structures of $c$ along canonical maps. The formalization chooses one structure $(X_0, \alpha_0)$ of $c$ (the representative chosen by the quotient) and takes $T(Z, c) = |TX_0|$, $(Z, c)^1 = |X_0^1|$; every other structure is attached to it by the maps $T\alpha$, $\alpha^1$, which are proved independent of every choice. This avoids the gluing and makes visible that what the gluing needs is a canonical map on orbit spaces, not a canonical generalized isomorphism.

(4) *Effective polyfolds for composition.* As we read it, Definition 16.1.10 does not give a well-defined composite on all polyfolds (§XVI.4). The formalization keeps the category PS of polyfold structures for all structures, keeps sc-smooth maps of all polyfolds and everything that is defined map by map (the tangent of a map, the pull-back of forms), and builds the category of polyfolds on the effective ones, where Theorem 10.3.19 holds and the composite is well defined.

(5) *Functions determine the class-level objects.* In §§16.3, 16.5 and 16.6 the relation between representatives is stated with a generalized strong bundle isomorphism, which need not be unique. The formalization proves in each case that the relation holds iff the functions on $Y$ or $Z$ agree (the section $f$, the multisection $\lambda$, the function $\theta = |\Theta| \circ \alpha^{-1}$), so the non-uniqueness of the isomorphisms does not reach these notions.

(6) *Added hypotheses are named and witnessed.* Where the text's proof reduces to a statement of an earlier chapter that carries a hypothesis the polyfold statement does not print (Stokes on the ep-groupoid level, the point Remark 9.5.19 leaves open; the reflexive local step of Theorem 12.2.13), the hypothesis is added to the polyfold statement, named, and witnessed (§XVI.28).

### XVI.1 Definitions 16.1.1–16.1.3 (2017: 16.1–16.3): polyfold structures and polyfolds, pp. 705–707

**Statement** (same). *Definition 16.1.1* (p. 705) ⟦Chapter16/PolyfoldStructure · PolyfoldStructure⟧: a polyfold structure on a topological space $Z$ is a pair $(X, \beta)$ of an ep-groupoid $X$ and a homeomorphism $\beta : |X| \to Z$ such that (1) the orbit space $|X|$ is paracompact and (2) $X$ satisfies (ZHOU). *Definition 16.1.2* (p. 707) ⟦Chapter16/PolyfoldStructure · PolyfoldStructure.Equiv⟧: two polyfold structures $(X, \alpha)$, $(Y, \beta)$ on $Z$ are equivalent if there is a generalized isomorphism $f : X \to Y$ with $\alpha = \beta \circ |f|$. *Definition 16.1.3* (p. 707) ⟦Chapter16/PolyfoldStructure · PolyfoldClass⟧: a polyfold is a pair $(Z, c)$ with $c$ an equivalence class of polyfold structures on $Z$. *The remark after Definition 16.1.1* (same) ⟦Chapter16/TangentPolyfold · PolyfoldStructure.metrizableSpace⟧: if $Z$ carries a polyfold structure, $Z$ is metrizable.

*Readings.* $Z$ lives in the universe of the object and morphism sets of $X$, so that $\beta$ is a homeomorphism between types of one universe; a polyfold on $Z$ is therefore a class of structures whose ep-groupoids live in that universe. The 2017 Definition 16.1 had neither (1) nor (2); both are part of the formal structure, and every theorem of this Part that needs (LR), (LUC), metrizability or the existence of auxiliary norms obtains it from them. The pair $(X, \beta)$ is an orbit chart in the sense of Remark 10.3.18 ⟦Chapter16/PolyfoldStructure · PolyfoldStructure.toOrbitChart⟧, and Definition 16.1.2 is the relation of Remark 10.3.18 on orbit charts ⟦Chapter16/PolyfoldStructure · PolyfoldStructure.equiv_iff_rel⟧.

**Proof that Definition 16.1.2 is an equivalence relation.** The text calls the relation an equivalence relation without proof; it is proved in three steps.

*Step 1, reflexivity* ⟦Chapter16/PolyfoldStructure · PolyfoldStructure.Equiv.refl⟧. The identity $1_X$ of the localization is a generalized isomorphism and $|1_X| = \mathrm{id}_{|X|}$, so $\alpha = \alpha \circ |1_X|$.

*Step 2, symmetry* ⟦Chapter16/PolyfoldStructure · PolyfoldStructure.Equiv.symm⟧. If $f : X \to Y$ is a generalized isomorphism with $\alpha = \beta \circ |f|$, its inverse $f^{-1}$ in the localization is a generalized isomorphism with $|f^{-1}| = |f|^{-1}$ (Proposition 10.3.11, Part X). Then $\beta = \alpha \circ |f|^{-1} = \alpha \circ |f^{-1}|$.

*Step 3, transitivity* ⟦Chapter16/PolyfoldStructure · PolyfoldStructure.Equiv.trans⟧. For $f : X \to Y$ and $g : Y \to V$ with $\alpha = \beta \circ |f|$ and $\beta = \gamma \circ |g|$, the composite $g \circ f$ is a generalized isomorphism with $|g \circ f| = |g| \circ |f|$ (Proposition 10.3.11), so $\alpha = \gamma \circ |g \circ f|$.

The quotient by this relation ⟦Chapter16/PolyfoldStructure · PolyfoldStructure.setoid⟧ is the type of polyfolds on $Z$. A consequence used throughout: if $(X, \alpha) \sim (Y, \beta)$ through $f$, then $|f| = \beta^{-1} \circ \alpha$ ⟦Chapter16/PolyfoldStructure · PolyfoldStructure.orbitMap_eq_of_equiv⟧, since $\beta$ is a bijection. So the orbit map of a realizing generalized isomorphism is determined by the two structures, even though (§XVI.2) the generalized isomorphism itself is not.

**(ZHOU) gives (LR)** ⟦Chapter16/PolyfoldStructure · PolyfoldStructure.isLR⟧. *Proof.* Proposition 7.4.22 (Part VII) ⟦Chapter8/SmoothRegularAssembly · proposition_7_4_22_of_isZhou⟧ shows that an ep-groupoid with (ZHOU) has the local regularity property. This is the hypothesis the text has in mind when it cites Theorem 10.3.19 after Definition 16.1.2, and the hypothesis of Proposition 10.1.10 and Proposition 11.2.9, which carry §16.2 (§§XVI.7–XVI.10).

**Proof of the remark: polyfolds are metrizable.** *Step 1.* $|X|$ is paracompact by Definition 16.1.1 (1). *Step 2.* Theorem 7.3.1 (Part VII) gives that $|X|$ is Hausdorff, regular and locally metrizable; with paracompactness, $|X|$ is metrizable ⟦Chapter7/OrbitTopology · theorem_7_3_1_metrizable⟧. *Step 3, the metrization step.* It is the theorem of Chapter 2 (Part II) that a paracompact Hausdorff space which is locally metrizable is metrizable ⟦Chapter2/Metrizable · metrizableSpace_of_locally⟧, proved through the pull-back of a metrizable topology along an embedding ⟦Chapter2/Metrizable · metrizableSpace_of_pullback⟧. The Nagata–Smirnov theorem, which the text's appeal to metrization theorems suggests, is not needed. *Step 4.* $\beta$ is a homeomorphism, so the metrizable topology of $|X|$ carries over to $Z$. The text's 2017 Proposition 16.1 is this remark.

**The polyfold $\mathbb R$ with isotropy $\mathbb Z/3$.** This example is the witness of every definition of §16.1 and the counterexample of §XVI.2, so it is built with care. Let $X = \mathbb Z/3 \times \mathbb R \rightrightarrows \mathbb R$ be the action groupoid of the trivial action of $\mathbb Z/3$ on $\mathbb R$: objects $\mathbb R$, morphisms $\mathbb Z/3 \times \mathbb R$, source and target the projection.

### XVI.30 What the formalization taught us

**(ZHOU) does not give uniqueness of isomorphisms.** A polyfold structure in the 2021 sense adds two conditions to the 2017 definition, paracompactness of $|X|$ and (ZHOU), and the text derives from (ZHOU) that the generalized isomorphism realizing an equivalence of structures is unique. Formalized, (ZHOU) gives (LR), and (LR) is the hypothesis of Theorem 10.3.19. But Theorem 10.3.19 does not hold as we read it (Part X), and its example, $\mathbb Z/3$ acting trivially on $\mathbb R$, satisfies (ZHOU) and has a paracompact orbit space. It is therefore a polyfold structure, and the two generalized isomorphisms $[1_X]$ and $[X \xleftarrow{1} X \xrightarrow{\mathrm{Inv}} X]$ both realize the identity of $\mathbb R$. Five statements of the chapter do not hold as we read them for this reason: the uniqueness after Definition 16.1.2, Remark 16.1.7 with the sentence on pp. 709–710, Lemma 16.2.1, the claim of Definition 16.1.10 and the uniqueness after Definition 16.3.3. All five hold for an effective target or middle structure, where Theorem 10.3.19 holds. The isotropy that acts trivially is exactly what (ZHOU) allows and effectiveness forbids.

**Where the uniqueness bites, and where it does not.** None of the definitions of §§16.1 and 16.3 uses the uniqueness: an equivalence of structures only asks that some generalized isomorphism exist. The polyfold category is where it bites. Definition 16.1.6 lets the isomorphism $(k, \mathrm{Id}_W)$ of the middle structure be any generalized isomorphism over $\mathrm{Id}_W$; when the middle structure has isotropy acting trivially, two equivalent representatives of $\hat f$ composed with one representative of $\hat g$ give inequivalent composites. Presumably the text intends effective polyfolds, or morphisms with a chosen isomorphism; the formalization builds the category of effective polyfolds, while the category PS of polyfold structures, the sc-smooth maps of all polyfolds, their tangents and the pull-back of forms need no restriction, because they are defined map by map and Definition 16.1.6 carries its isomorphisms as data.

**Canonical on orbit spaces is enough for §16.2.** The text glues $T(Z, c)$ along "the unique" $\mathfrak f$ with $\beta \circ |\mathfrak f| = \alpha$, and Lemma 16.2.1 is there to make the gluing maps canonical. What the gluing uses is only $|T\mathfrak f|$, and that is unique: (LR) gives, for functors with the same orbit map, local families of morphisms between them (Proposition 10.1.10 (1)), and these give the same tangent orbit maps (Proposition 10.1.10 (2)). So $T(Z, c)$, $T\alpha$, (16.5), Lemma 16.2.2 and Definition 16.2.3 go through as printed, with one structure of $c$ as base point instead of the gluing over all. The same argument, Proposition 11.2.9, makes $h_*\omega$ independent of $h$, which is the remark before Definition 16.2.5. The raised polyfolds follow the same pattern, through the raise of generalized maps.

**The tangent of an effective polyfold needs (ZHOU) too.** The tangent functor lives on effective polyfolds, because the tangent of an effective polyfold is effective. The proof uses (ZHOU) as well as effectiveness: effectiveness is a statement at level $0$, while the open sets of $TX$ see only level $1$, so an isotropy element acting trivially near a tangent vector is first moved to a smooth point, where (ZHOU) turns the triviality of the linearized action into triviality near the point.

**Invariance statements are statements about equivalences.** The invariance statements of §16.1 are statements about equivalences of ep-groupoids, transported to classes. Bump functions are local; partitions of unity are best read on $|X|$; and the text's "easy exercise" in Proposition 16.1.4 (2) is the descent of sc-smoothness along an equivalence. The metrizability of a polyfold follows from Theorem 7.3.1 and the metrization theorem for locally metrizable paracompact Hausdorff spaces, without the Nagata–Smirnov theorem.

**§16.4 is Chapters 9 and 11 on classes, with two implicit points.** An orientation $\hat T_\Theta$ is read only on $X_\infty$, so a representative fixes it to be $0$ elsewhere, without which $1^*\hat T = \hat T$ fails. And Theorem 16.4.6 reduces to Stokes on the ep-groupoid level, which the text takes for granted (p. 732), while Theorems 9.5.17 and 9.5.18 prove it only with sc-smooth partitions of unity and Remark 9.5.19 leaves the general case open; the polyfold theorem is proved as printed from that assertion, and with partitions of unity, which by Definition 16.1.5 hold for every representative as soon as they hold for one, unconditionally. The tameness of branched ep⁺-subgroupoids moves along equivalences through the transport charts of Theorem 11.4.1, so the integrals live on classes. Proposition 16.4.3 is the same transport once more: the two legs of a generalized isomorphism restrict to equivalences of the sub-ep-groupoids on the supports, and the transition between the induced structures is, near each point, a composite of local sc-diffeomorphisms. The structures of Proposition 9.1.9, which are existential, need not be chosen, since any two give the same comparison.

**Functions on $Y$ and $Z$ determine the class-level objects.** §§16.3, 16.5 and 16.6 survive the non-uniqueness because their relations are determined by functions. Two representatives of a section are related as soon as their maps $f$ agree: the condition $\mathfrak A_*F = F'$ follows from $\Gamma' \circ |\mathfrak A| = \Gamma$, since a functor is determined on orbits. Likewise the pull-back of a multisection functor along any generalized strong bundle isomorphism compatible with $\Gamma$ is $\lambda \circ \Gamma$, whichever isomorphism is chosen; on the $\mathbb Z/3$ example both isomorphisms are checked to pull back every functor alike. A branched suborbifold is determined by its function $\theta$, which makes $\lambda \circ f$ a well-defined branched suborbifold. Several conditions the text lists are consequences: the continuity and surjectivity of $p$, $p' \circ A = a \circ p$ for morphisms, the base condition of Definition 16.3.3.

**Small readings.** Read literally, the domain support of Definition 16.5.2 is all of $Z$, since the weights over each fibre sum to $1$, so that some $y$ over every $z$ has $\lambda(y) > 0$; with $y \ne 0_z$, as in Definition 13.2.2, it is the intended set. The continuity of $n(\lambda)$ in (16.8) is local: in a local section structure $N(\Lambda)$ is the maximum of the continuous functions $N \circ s_i$, and it descends to $Z$ through a quotient map.

**Reflexivity is a transport too.** Reflexivity of the $1$-fibres pulls back along a local strong bundle isomorphism by transporting a chart, whose fibre model does not change; the push direction needs the same for the action $\mu$ along a morphism, which Chapter 10 provides. Mixed convergence moves along a local strong bundle isomorphism because the isomorphism is linear and bounded on the fibres, so bounded sequences stay bounded and weak limits are preserved; the independence of the model for the reflexive notions is again a transport.

**§16.6 is a transport, with two facts the text uses silently.** Once the definitions are on classes, every notion of §16.6 equals the ep-groupoid notion in every representative, and Theorem 16.6.4 is Theorem 12.4.2 there. Theorem 16.5.7 is Theorem 14.1.9 in one model. For Theorem 16.6.6 the integrals live on the polyfold and depend only on the class of the form by Stokes alone, and the independence of $\lambda$ is the cylinder of Corollary 15.4.4 in the model of $o$. Two facts are used without being stated: general position does not depend on the model, and the orientation inherited from $(f, o)$ does not depend on the model carrying $o$. The first is the invariance of linearizations under local strong bundle isomorphisms; the second is that the formula of Theorem 15.4.1 commutes with strong bundle equivalences, together with the observation that general position, carried along a diagram, gives good position at boundary points in the other model. In index $0$ the frame convention of the cylinder becomes a statement about signs: the curve over a point leaves the end with $dt$ equal to the sign of the point.

**Definition 16.6.5 rests on Proposition 10.4.7.** The text says that an orientation of one representative determines orientations of all, by Theorem 12.5.7. Over a non-effective base the generalized isomorphism between two representatives is not unique (§XVI.11), so the claim cannot rest on uniqueness. It rests on Proposition 10.4.7: two isomorphisms with the same orbit map are related near each point by an sc-smooth family of morphisms, and the orientation maps of Theorem 12.5.7 are invariant under morphisms. Proposition 10.4.7 needs the local regularity property, which is why Definition 16.3.1 includes it; read as Definition 8.4.2 prints it, it is the field of the structure, and it is used in its germ form.
