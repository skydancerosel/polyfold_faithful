### VI.1 Definition 6.2.1 (preprint 6.1): the space $\mathrm{Lin}(f, x)$

§6.2 (preprint §6.1) (pp. 237–240 (preprint pp. 200–201)) works at a smooth point $x$. Since $f(x)$ is a smooth point of the fibre, there is an sc⁺-section $s$ with $s(x) = f(x)$ — in a chart, $s(y) = R(y, f(x))$ — and $(f - s)'(x) : T_xX \to Y_x$ is sc-Fredholm. Definition 6.2.1 (preprint 6.1) collects these linearizations, perturbed by sc⁺-operators, into $\mathrm{Lin}(f, x)$; the index is the index of any member, and Proposition 6.2.2 (preprint 6.2) says that it is constant along sc-smooth paths.

**The chart point** (V6.1). A chart point of $x$ is a strong bundle chart $c$ of the atlas whose base chart contains $x$, with the level-$1$ point $z$ of the model over $\varphi(x)$ ⟦Chapter6/LinSet · ChartPoint⟧. In it $T_xX$ is $T_zO = Dr(z)E$, the range of the derivative of the retraction ⟦Chapter6/LinSet · ChartPoint.T⟧, and $Y_x$ is $K_z = \rho(z)F$, the range of the fibre projection over $z$ ⟦Chapter6/LinSet · ChartPoint.K⟧; both are closed, as ranges of continuous projections, hence Banach spaces. An sc⁺-operator $T_xX \to Y_x$ is level $0$ of an sc⁺-operator between these sc-subspaces ⟦Chapter6/LinSet · ChartPoint.IsScPlusOp⟧.

**Definition 6.2.1 (preprint 6.1)** (V6.1). $\mathrm{Lin}(f, x)$, at the chart point $p$, is the set of the operators $(f + s)'(x) + a : T_zO \to K_z$ for an sc⁺-section $s$ with $(f + s)(x) = 0$ and an sc⁺-operator $a$ ⟦Chapter6/LinSet · ChartPoint.linSet⟧ ⟦Chapter6/LinSet · ChartPoint.mem_linSet_iff⟧. The linearization of $f + s$ at its zero $x$ is the derivative at $z$ of the principal part composed with $r$, restricted to $T_zO$ — the $\mathrm{Lin}(z)$ of Part III. Replacing the text's $s$ by $-s$ turns $f - s$ with $s(x) = f(x)$ into $f + s$ with $(f + s)(x) = 0$.

**Two sections differ by an sc⁺-operator.** For two sections $s$, $t$ with $(f + s)(x) = (f + t)(x) = 0$, the principal parts of $f + s$ and $f + t$ differ by that of the sc⁺-section $s - t$, whose derivative at $z$, followed by $\rho(z)$, is level $0$ of an sc⁺-operator: the derivative of an sc⁺-map is sc⁺ on the shifted levels. So $\mathrm{Lin}(f, x)$ is $(f + s)'(x)$ plus all the sc⁺-operators, for any one $s$ ⟦Chapter6/LinSet · ChartPoint.exists_isScPlusOp_linAt_eq⟧, and two members differ by an sc⁺-operator ⟦Chapter6/LinSet · ChartPoint.isScPlusOp_sub_of_mem_linSet⟧. The paragraph before the definition puts this as "$(f - s)'(x)$ is an sc-Fredholm operator if and only if $(f - s)'(x)$ is"; it is read with $(f - t)'(x)$ in the second place (possible misprint; Findings, Chapter 6, item 41).

**Not empty.** The text makes its section locally, in a chart. The formalization uses global sc⁺-sections: $\mathrm{Lin}(f, x)$ is not empty when $P$ has sc⁺-sections through the smooth points of its fibres, one through $-f(x)$ giving a member ⟦Chapter6/LinSet · ChartPoint.linSet_nonempty⟧, and bump functions give such sections ⟦Chapter3/IndexLocallyConstantLocal · MPolyfold.AdmitsBumpFunctions.hasScPlusSectionsThrough⟧. This is the hidden hypothesis of the definition: an sc⁺-section through $f(x)$.

**Fredholm, of one index.** Every member is Fredholm on level $0$, with the index $\mathrm{ind}(f, x)$ of the germ at $x$ (§III.49) ⟦Chapter6/LinSet · ChartPoint.isFredholm_isIndexAt_of_mem_linSet⟧ ⟦Chapter6/LinSet · ChartPoint.exists_index_linSet⟧ ⟦Chapter6/LinSet · ChartPoint.fredholmIndex_of_mem_linSet⟧. The proof: $f + s$ has germ data at $x$ (an sc-Fredholm germ plus an sc⁺-germ is one), so its linearization at the zero $x$ is Fredholm of the index of the germ, through the conjugation to a basic germ (Proposition 3.6.5 (preprint 3.52), §III.30); adding an sc⁺-operator, compact on level $0$, keeps the Fredholm property and the index; and the index of the germ does not depend on the choices. That the members are sc-Fredholm on every level, as the text says, is not stated: the determinant lines use level $0$ only (gap G.64; Findings, Chapter 6, item 40).

**Convex** ⟦Chapter6/LinSet · ChartPoint.convex_linSet⟧. $(1 - \tau)(L + a) + \tau(L + b) = L + ((1 - \tau)a + \tau b)$, and the sc⁺-operators form a vector space. The text notes this on preprint p. 219, where Proposition 6.6.1 (preprint 6.26) needs it.

**Proposition 6.2.2 (preprint 6.2)** (V3.98). The constancy of the index along sc-smooth paths was formalized with Chapter 3 and is retold in §III.49: for paths on $\mathbb R$, with the text's hypotheses — tame germ data, no bump functions, the locally defined sc⁺-sections of Definition 6.2.1 (preprint 6.1). It is not repeated here. The facts on paths recalled before it on preprint p. 201 — an M-polyfold is locally path connected, and two smooth points in one path component are joined by an sc⁺-smooth path — are formalized only inside a chart (§VI.18; gap G.56; Findings, Chapter 6, item 42).

### VI.2 Determinant lines, Definition 6.3.1 (preprint 6.3), Proposition 6.3.2 (preprint 6.4)

**$\lambda(E)$ and $\lambda^*(E)$** (V6.2). For a finite-dimensional real space $E$, $\lambda(E) = \Lambda^{\max}E$ is the top exterior power ⟦Chapter6/DetLine · maxPow⟧ and $\lambda^*(E)$ its dual ⟦Chapter6/DetLine · maxPowDual⟧; both are lines ⟦Chapter6/DetLine · finrank_maxPow⟧, $\lambda(\{0\}) = \mathbb R$ being the zeroth exterior power. A family $a_1, \dots, a_n$ with $n = \dim E$ has its wedge $a_1 \wedge \dots \wedge a_n$ ⟦Chapter6/DetLine · wedge⟧. Two rules carry every computation of the chapter. A change of basis multiplies the wedge by its determinant ⟦Chapter6/DetLine · wedge_eq_det_smul⟧, also when only one block of a two-block wedge $a \wedge b$ is changed ⟦Chapter6/DetLine · wedge_append_basis_left⟧ ⟦Chapter6/DetLine · wedge_append_basis_right⟧; and adding to the vectors of one block vectors of the span of the other does not change the wedge ⟦Chapter6/DetLine · wedge_append_add_left⟧ ⟦Chapter6/DetLine · wedge_append_add_right⟧. For $v \ne 0$ in a line, $v^*$ is the functional with $v^*(v) = 1$ ⟦Chapter6/DetLine · dualVec⟧ ⟦Chapter6/DetLine · dualVec_self⟧, and that value determines it ⟦Chapter6/DetLine · eq_dualVec_of_apply_eq_one⟧. For $\dim E = \dim F$ and $\Phi : E \to F$, $\lambda(\Phi)(a_1 \wedge \dots \wedge a_n) = \Phi a_1 \wedge \dots \wedge \Phi a_n$ ⟦Chapter6/DetLine · maxPowMap⟧ ⟦Chapter6/DetLine · maxPowMap_wedge⟧, with $\lambda(\Psi\Phi) = \lambda(\Psi)\lambda(\Phi)$ ⟦Chapter6/DetLine · maxPowMap_comp⟧, and $\lambda(\Phi) \ne 0$ iff $\Phi$ is bijective ⟦Chapter6/DetLine · maxPowMap_ne_zero_iff⟧; $\lambda^*(\Phi)$ is its dual ⟦Chapter6/DetLine · maxPowDualMap⟧ (pp. 241–243 (preprint pp. 203–204)).

**Definition 6.3.1 (preprint 6.3)** (V6.3). $\iota : \lambda(E^*) \to \lambda^*(E)$ is $\iota(e_1^* \wedge \dots \wedge e_n^*)(a_1 \wedge \dots \wedge a_n) = \det(e_i^*(a_j))$ ⟦Chapter6/DetLine · iotaDual⟧ ⟦Chapter6/DetLine · iotaDual_wedge_wedge⟧. The formula is alternating and multilinear in both families, so it descends to the exterior powers; that is the text's well-definedness. $\iota$ is a nonzero map of lines ⟦Chapter6/DetLine · iotaDual_ne_zero⟧, and for a basis with its dual basis $\iota(e_1^* \wedge \dots \wedge e_n^*) = (e_1 \wedge \dots \wedge e_n)^*$ ⟦Chapter6/DetLine · iotaDual_wedge_dualBasis⟧, since the matrix is the identity. The text's matrix $(e_i^*(a_j))$ is the transpose of the one of Mathlib's pairing, with the same determinant (preprint p. 204).

**Proposition 6.3.2 (preprint 6.4)** (V6.4) ⟦Chapter6/DetLine · proposition_6_3_2⟧. For an isomorphism $\Phi : E \to F$, $\lambda^*(\Phi) \circ \iota = \iota \circ \lambda(\Phi^*)$. Both sides, evaluated at $f_1^* \wedge \dots \wedge f_n^*$ and $a_1 \wedge \dots \wedge a_n$, give $\det(f_i^*(\Phi a_j))$: the left side by the definition of $\lambda^*(\Phi)$, the right side because $(\Phi^*f_i^*)(a_j) = f_i^*(\Phi a_j)$ (p. 243 (preprint p. 205)).

### VI.3 Four-term exact sequences: $\Phi_E$, Lemma 6.3.3 (preprint 6.5), Proposition 6.3.5 (preprint 6.6)

**The data of (91)** (V6.5). Let $\mathbf E : 0 \to A \xrightarrow{\alpha} B \xrightarrow{\beta} C \xrightarrow{\gamma} D \to 0$ be an exact sequence of finite-dimensional spaces ⟦Chapter6/ExactSequence · FourTermExact⟧. A *choice* ⟦Chapter6/ExactSequence · FourTermExact.Choice⟧ consists of a complement $Z$ of $\alpha(A)$ in $B$, a complement $V$ of $\beta(B)$ in $C$, bases $a$ of $A$, $d$ of $D$, $b'$ of $Z$, and the $\gamma$-preimages $c$ in $V$ of the basis $d$. Then $\alpha(a), b'$ is a basis of $B$, and $c, \beta(b')$ is one of $C$, because $\beta$ maps $Z$ isomorphically onto $\beta(B)$ and $\gamma$ maps $V$ isomorphically onto $D$. Formula (91) assigns to the choice the value $(\alpha(a_1) \wedge \dots \wedge \alpha(a_n) \wedge b'_1 \wedge \dots \wedge b'_{m-n}) \otimes (c_1 \wedge \dots \wedge c_l \wedge \beta(b'_1) \wedge \dots \wedge \beta(b'_{m-n}))^*$ as the image of $(a_1 \wedge \dots \wedge a_n) \otimes (d_1 \wedge \dots \wedge d_l)^*$, with the text's two ordering conventions ⟦Chapter6/ExactSequence · FourTermExact.Choice.value⟧. $\Phi_E : \lambda(A) \otimes \lambda^*(D) \to \lambda(B) \otimes \lambda^*(C)$ is the linear map of lines defined by one fixed choice ⟦Chapter6/ExactSequence · FourTermExact.canonicalChoice⟧ ⟦Chapter6/ExactSequence · FourTermExact.phi⟧; it is nonzero ⟦Chapter6/ExactSequence · FourTermExact.phi_ne_zero⟧, hence an isomorphism ⟦Chapter6/ExactSequence · FourTermExact.phiEquiv⟧ (preprint p. 206).

**Lemma 6.3.3 (preprint 6.5)** (V6.6) ⟦Chapter6/ExactSequence · FourTermExact.lemma_6_3_3⟧. The text: $\Phi_E$ does not depend on the choices. The formalization proves the strongest form: $\Phi_E$, defined from one choice, maps the source element $(a_1 \wedge \dots \wedge a_n) \otimes (d_1 \wedge \dots \wedge d_l)^*$ of *any other* choice to the right-hand side of (91) computed with that choice. This is the form every later computation uses — $\gamma^P_T$ is always evaluated in bases adapted to the problem at hand (Findings, Chapter 6, item 1). The proof is §6.8.1 (preprint §6.7.1) (§VI.28).

### VI.22 Transport along paths from local push-forwards

The proof of Theorem 6.6.13 (preprint 6.37) (p. 273 (preprint p. 228)): for a path $\varphi$, "using the compactness of $[0, 1]$ and a finite covering" by orientable neighbourhoods, $\varphi_*$ is the composite of the local propagations; "by a covering argument" it depends only on $[\varphi]$; and "it is obvious that $\Gamma_f$ is unique". The formalization separates this argument from orientations: it is proved for any family of types over $X_\infty$ (Findings, Chapter 6, item 72).

**Local transport data** (V6.56) ⟦Chapter6/PathTransport · LocalTransport⟧: a family of *good* open sets covering $X_\infty$, all fibres in bijection, and for every good set $U$ a push-forward along every sc-smooth path in $U$, such that push-forwards in one good set compose and do not depend on the good set containing the path. Then the push-forward along a loop in a good set is the identity ⟦Chapter6/PathTransport · LocalTransport.push_eq_refl⟧, in a good set it depends only on the end points ⟦Chapter6/PathTransport · LocalTransport.push_eq_push⟧, and only on the path on $[0, 1]$ ⟦Chapter6/PathTransport · LocalTransport.push_congr⟧.

**The dyadic transport.** At level $0$ it is the push-forward in a chosen good set containing $\gamma([0, 1])$ (an arbitrary bijection if there is none); at level $n + 1$ it is the composite of the transports at level $n$ along the two halves ⟦Chapter6/PathTransport · LocalTransport.transportN⟧. On a path in a good set every level gives the push-forward ⟦Chapter6/PathTransport · LocalTransport.transportN_eq_push⟧, by the composition rule. The pieces of level $N$ lie in good sets ⟦Chapter6/PathTransport · LocalTransport.IsFine⟧ for some $N$: the preimages of the good sets cover the compact $[0, 1]$, so there is a Lebesgue number $\delta$ ⟦Chapter6/PathTransport · LocalTransport.IsLebesgueNumber⟧ ⟦Chapter6/PathTransport · LocalTransport.exists_isLebesgueNumber⟧, and $2^N\delta > 1$ suffices ⟦Chapter6/PathTransport · LocalTransport.isFine_of_isLebesgueNumber⟧. From such an $N$ on the levels agree ⟦Chapter6/PathTransport · LocalTransport.transportN_eq_of_isFine⟧, since at the next level each piece is split inside its good set. The *transport* is the value at such a level ⟦Chapter6/PathTransport · LocalTransport.transport⟧.

**Its properties.** It is the push-forward on a path in a good set ⟦Chapter6/PathTransport · LocalTransport.transport_eq_push⟧ — property (∗) — depends only on the path on $[0, 1]$ ⟦Chapter6/PathTransport · LocalTransport.transport_congr⟧, is the identity on paths constant on $[0, 1]$ ⟦Chapter6/PathTransport · LocalTransport.transport_const⟧, and is the composite along the two halves ⟦Chapter6/PathTransport · LocalTransport.transport_halves⟧. *Homotopy invariance* ⟦Chapter6/PathTransport · LocalTransport.transport_eq_of_isScHomotopyRel⟧ is the covering argument. For a homotopy $\Phi : \mathbb R^2 \to X$ the preimages of the good sets cover $[0, 1]^2$, with a Lebesgue number $\delta$ ⟦Chapter6/PathTransport · LocalTransport.exists_isSqLebesgueNumber⟧. *The square lemma* ⟦Chapter6/PathTransport · LocalTransport.transportN_square⟧: for a rectangle of $[0, 1]^2$ with sides $< 2^n\delta$, the transports at level $n$ along its vertical, horizontal and diagonal segments ⟦Chapter6/PathTransport · vSeg⟧ ⟦Chapter6/PathTransport · hSeg⟧ ⟦Chapter6/PathTransport · dSeg⟧ commute around its boundary — left side then top equals bottom then right side. By induction on $n$: for $n = 0$ the rectangle lies in one good set, and both composites are the push-forward along the diagonal; for $n + 1$ the rectangle is cut into four quadrants and the four commutative squares are pasted, the interior edges cancelling ⟦Chapter6/PathTransport · equiv_paste_squares⟧. Applied to $[0, 1]^2$ at a level with $2^n\delta > 1$, with the sides $\tau \mapsto \Phi(\tau, 0)$, $\tau \mapsto \Phi(\tau, 1)$ constant on $[0, 1]$ and of trivial transport, it gives the invariance. The transport is invariant under reparametrization ⟦Chapter6/PathTransport · LocalTransport.transport_reparam⟧ (§VI.20) and takes concatenations to composites ⟦Chapter6/PathTransport · LocalTransport.transport_concatPath⟧, the halves of a concatenation being reparametrizations of the two paths. *Uniqueness* ⟦Chapter6/PathTransport · LocalTransport.transport_unique⟧: a family of bijections along the sc-smooth paths that is homotopy invariant, takes concatenations to composites and is the push-forward on paths in good sets, is the transport — $G(\gamma) = G(\text{left half}) \circ G(\text{right half})$ through the reparametrization, and induction on the dyadic level. Dyadic subdivisions of $[0, 1]$ and of $[0, 1]^2$ stand in place of arbitrary finite coverings (preprint p. 228).

### VI.31 What the formalization taught us

**The sign of (94).** The main change of substance is a sign. The coordinate of $\Phi_E$ in a local determinant chart carries the factor $(-1)^{\dim\operatorname{coker}S}$, which jumps where the parity of the cokernel dimension changes; the orientations it carries along a continuous family are then not continuous, as we read it, and an example on $\mathbb R$ shows it (§VI.14). The continuity statements of pp. 265–272 (preprint pp. 223–226) are not written in coordinates in the text, and it is in coordinates that the sign appears. The normalized $\alpha(T)\Phi_E$ at both ends of every propagation changes nothing on surjective operators — the tangent orientations of Theorem 6.6.18 (preprint 6.41) are the text's — and the same sign is used in Remark 6.6.19 (preprint 6.42) (§VI.26).

**Lemma 6.10 (preprint) and the 2017 remark.** The common lower bound of two good left-projections exists, but not with the range $R(P) \cap R(P')$: a projection onto a smaller, finite-codimensional piece of it, along a complement containing both kernels, is below both (§VI.4). This keeps the preprint's construction of $\mathrm{DET}(E, F)$ — one lower bound, Corollary 6.4.12 (preprint 6.13), Lemma 6.5.5 (preprint 6.21) — intact.

**Contractibility was never needed.** Proposition 6.6.3 (1) (preprint 6.28) is not established for the neighbourhood its proof builds, as we read it, but every later use of contractibility is a use of (1′): two paths in $U$ with the same end points are homotopic in a larger region where sections along families exist. Orientable neighbourhoods become pairs $V \subseteq U$, and property (∗) of Theorem 6.6.13 (preprint 6.37) holds on every set with the local propagation property (§§VI.10, VI.19, VI.23).

**Paths on $\mathbb R$ cost reparametrizations.** With paths and homotopies on $\mathbb R$ whose conditions hold on $[0, 1]$ (decision D2), the homotopy of (1′) has the right edges only on $[0, 1]$ while push-forward data need zeros near $[0, 1]$; transitivity of homotopy, associativity of concatenation and Theorem 6.6.11 (preprint 6.35) all pass through the smooth transition function $\kappa$ (§§VI.18, VI.20).

**Charts must be compared.** The text's chart-free $\mathrm{Or}^f_x$ becomes a set read in a chosen chart, and the invariance of the local propagation under a change of chart — the naturality of the stabilization isomorphism, of the sign and of continuation — is a file of its own (§VI.21).

**"By a covering argument" and "one verifies".** The covering argument of Theorem 6.6.13 (preprint 6.37) is a dyadic transport with a square lemma, proved for any family of types (§VI.22); the "simple computation" of Lemma 6.6.8 (preprint 6.33) is an exact identity of stabilization isomorphisms, with no sign, once the appended sections come last (§VI.16); and the equivalence of Definitions 6.6.14 and 6.6.15 (preprint 6.38 and 6.39), stated without proof, needs the transport computed on dyadic pieces inside orientable neighbourhoods (§VI.24).
