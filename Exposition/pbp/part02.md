## Part II. Differential geometry based on retracts (HWZ Chapter 2, §§2.1, 2.3–2.7 (preprint §§2.1–2.6))

Chapter 2 builds a differential geometry whose local models are not open sets of Banach spaces but images of sc-smooth retractions, sets whose "dimension" may jump. The formal proof establishes the chapter in its order: retractions and retracts and the independence of their tangent from the retraction (§§II.2–II.4), M-polyfolds with their filtration, metrizability and tangent bundle (§§II.5–II.9), sub-M-polyfolds (§II.10), the degeneracy index with the boundary recognition argument (§§II.11–II.13), tame retracts and the faces of tame M-polyfolds (§§II.14–II.16), products and fibred products (§II.17), and strong bundles with their pull-backs (§§II.18–II.20). What is not formalized is the classical Proposition 2.1.2 (preprint 2.2) (motivation only), Lemma 2.4.9 (preprint 2.29) in its formulation by sc-smooth paths (its content, the sign arguments, is formalized and used instead), the second statement of Theorem 2.4.12 (preprint 2.31) in general (for tame retracts its conclusion, Proposition 2.5.6's (preprint 2.36) partial quadrant, is proved directly), the openness of $p : TX \to X_1$, and the categories $\mathcal R$, $\mathcal{BR}$, $\mathcal{SBR}$.

Three choices shape the formal route. First, a retraction is a total map $r : E_0 \to E_0$ with an explicit domain $U$, and the tangent $TO$ of a retract is described as the *fixed-point set* of $Tr$ in $TU$ (§II.2); this description, which the text's proofs use without stating it, makes Propositions 2.1.11 and 2.1.13 (preprint 2.4 and 2.6) one-line consequences of the chain rule. Second, an M-polyfold is a space with *one* atlas, and every notion is defined through the maximal atlas of all charts compatible with it (§II.5); this needs the fact, not in the text, that compatibility is a local property, proved from the chain rule and the locality of sc-smoothness (§II.1). Third, where the text computes in the model $C = [0, \infty)^k \oplus W$, the formal statements carry a presentation $T : E \cong \mathbb R^n \oplus W$ of the partial quadrant and its coordinate functionals $\lambda_i = (T\,\cdot)_i$; the subspaces $E_x$ and cones $C_x$ are defined through $T$, and are shown not to depend on it (§II.14).

Six proofs take a different road from the text's (or the exposition's). Theorem 2.3.10 (preprint 2.16) (metrizability of the levels) is proved without Lemma 2.7.3 (preprint 2.56) and Proposition 2.7.4 (preprint 2.57), which need a regularity of $X_m$ the text does not check: a partition of unity of $X$ pulled back to $X_m$ gives an explicit metric, and the same argument proves Smirnov's theorem, which Mathlib lacks (§II.7). Proposition 2.4.10 (preprint 2.30) is proved from the sign arguments at points on level $1$, not from Lemma 2.4.9 (preprint 2.29) at smooth points (§II.13). Theorem 2.4.12 (preprint 2.31) is proved for every chart of every M-polyfold by a direct isomorphism of quotients, without reducing to a chart that realizes the index (§II.13). Proposition 2.5.3 (preprint 2.35) uses only the second condition of tameness (§II.14). Proposition 2.5.6's (preprint 2.36) partial quadrant is proved for tame retracts without the equality case of Theorem 2.4.12 (preprint 2.31), the smooth vectors of its proof being written down explicitly (§II.14). The second sentence of Proposition 2.5.16 (preprint 2.44) is proved by removing all coordinates of $\sigma$ at once rather than by the exposition's induction on $\#\sigma$ (§II.16).

### II.1 Restriction and locality of sc^k maps

The text restricts sc-smooth maps to smaller sets ("$r : U \to U \cap U'$ is sc-smooth") and glues local sc-smoothness without comment. Both need an argument, because the tangent map of an $\mathrm{sc}^1$ map is built from a *chosen* derivative family (Part I, §I.11), and a chosen derivative on $U$ and one on $U' \subseteq U$ need not be the same objects.

*Restriction.* If $f$ is $\mathrm{sc}^1$ on $U$ with derivative family $D$ and $U' \subseteq U$, the same $D$ works on $U'$ ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · IsSc1With.of_subset⟧ ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · IsSc1.of_subset⟧. When $U'$ is an sc-domain (unique differentiability on every level, Part I, §I.10), derivatives at points of $U'_1$ are unique, so the tangent maps built on $U'$ and on $U$ agree on $TU'$ ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · IsSc1.tangentMap_eqOn_of_subset⟧. By induction on $k$, using that $TU'$ is again an sc-domain and that the tangent maps agree there, an $\mathrm{sc}^k$ map restricts to $\mathrm{sc}^k$ on every smaller sc-domain ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · IsSck.of_subset⟧, and so does an sc-smooth map ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · IsScSmooth.of_subset⟧; sc-smoothness depends only on the values on $U$ ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · IsScSmooth.congr⟧. Relatively open subsets of quadrants, and their intersections with open sets, are sc-domains ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · IsRelOpenInQuadrant.inter_isOpen⟧ ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · IsScDomain.inter_isOpen⟧, and $T(U \cap W) = TU \cap (W_1 \times E_0)$ ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · ScBanach.tangentSet_inter_isOpen⟧.

*Locality.* $\mathrm{sc}^0$ is local: continuity on each level is a local property ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · IsSc0.of_locally⟧. For $\mathrm{sc}^1$, suppose every $x \in U$ has an open $W_x \ni x$ with $f$ $\mathrm{sc}^1$ on $U \cap W_x$. Two such local structures give the same derivative at a common point, by uniqueness on the sc-domain ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · IsSc1With.eq_of_eq_near⟧. Glue: at a point $x \in U_1$ take the derivative of the structure on $U \cap W_{\iota_1 x}$. This family $D$ agrees near every point with a local derivative family; the three conditions of Definition 1.1.15 (preprint 1.14) (the $\mathrm{sc}^0$ property, differentiability on $U_1$, and the $\mathrm{sc}^0$ property of the tangent map on $TU$) are local and hold ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · IsSc1.of_locally⟧. The induction on $k$ applies the same argument to the tangent maps on $TU$ ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · IsSck.of_locally⟧, and gives the sc-smooth case ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · IsScSmooth.of_locally⟧. The identity is $\mathrm{sc}^1$ with derivative the identity ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · isSc1With_id⟧, its tangent map is the identity on $TU$ ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · tangentMap_id_eqOn⟧, and it is sc-smooth on every sc-domain ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · isSck_id⟧ ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · isScSmooth_id⟧.

### II.2 Retractions, retracts and their tangent (Definitions 2.1.1, 2.1.3, 2.1.12 (preprint 2.1, 2.3, 2.5); Proposition 2.1.11 (preprint 2.4))

**Definition 2.1.1 (preprint 2.1).** An *sc-smooth retraction* on a partial quadrant $C \subseteq E_0$ is a set $U$ relatively open in $C$ and a map $r : E_0 \to E_0$ with $r(U) \subseteq U$, $r$ sc-smooth on $U$ and $r(r(x)) = r(x)$ for $x \in U$ ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · ScRetraction⟧. Since $U$ is relatively open, it is an sc-domain and $r$ is $\mathrm{sc}^1$ ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · ScRetraction.isScDomain⟧ ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · ScRetraction.isSc1⟧. The *retract* is $O = r(U)$ ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · ScRetraction.retract⟧. It is contained in $U$ and is exactly the fixed-point set of $r$ in $U$: if $x = r(y)$ then $r(x) = r(r(y)) = x$ ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · ScRetraction.retract_subset⟧ ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · ScRetraction.apply_eq_self_of_mem_retract⟧ ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · ScRetraction.mem_retract_iff⟧. On level $1$, $r_1$ fixes the points of $O_1$ ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · ScRetraction.levelMap_one_eq_self⟧, and $O_m \subseteq U_m$ ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · ScRetraction.lvlSet_retract_subset⟧.

**Definition 2.1.3 (preprint 2.3).** $(O, C, E)$ is an *sc-smooth retract* if $O$ is the retract of some sc-smooth retraction on $C$ ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · IsScRetract⟧ ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · ScRetraction.isScRetract⟧. Every relatively open $U \subseteq C$ is one, with $r = \mathrm{id}$ ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · ScRetraction.id⟧ ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · IsRelOpenInQuadrant.isScRetract⟧. A *local model* is a triple $(E, C, O)$ with $C$ a partial quadrant and $O$ a retract in $C$ ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · LocalModel⟧; these are the models of M-polyfold charts.

*The tangent of a retraction.* Put $Tr(TU) := \{Tr(p) : p \in TU\} \subseteq E_1 \times E_0$ ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · ScRetraction.tangent⟧. The chain rule applied to $r \circ r = r$ (Part I, Theorem 1.3.1 (preprint 1.21)) gives $Tr \circ Tr = Tr$ on $TU$ ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · ScRetraction.tangentMap_tangentMap⟧ ⟦Chapter1/ChainRule · theorem_1_3_1⟧, since $Tr$ maps $TU$ into $TU$ ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · ScRetraction.tangentMap_mapsTo⟧. Hence the **fixed-point description**: $p \in Tr(TU)$ iff $p \in TU$ and $Tr(p) = p$ ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · ScRetraction.mem_tangent_iff⟧. Base points of $Tr(TU)$ lie in $O_1$ ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · ScRetraction.fst_mem_of_mem_tangent⟧. For $x \in O_1$ the fixed-point condition reads $Dr(x)h = h$ ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · ScRetraction.mem_tangent_iff_of_mem⟧, and the second component of $Tr \circ Tr = Tr$ at $(x, h)$ gives $Dr(x) \circ Dr(x) = Dr(x)$ ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · ScRetraction.scDeriv_comp_self⟧ — the remark after Definition 2.1.3 (preprint 2.3) that $Dr(x)$ is a projection. So for $x \in O_1$, $(x, h) \in Tr(TU)$ iff $h$ is in the range of $Dr(x)$ ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · ScRetraction.mem_tangent_iff_mem_range⟧, and $Tr(TU) = \{(r(x), Dr(x)h) : x \in U_1, h \in E_0\}$ ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · ScRetraction.mem_tangent_iff_exists⟧.

**Proposition 2.1.11 (preprint 2.4).** *If $r(U) \subseteq r'(U')$ then $r' \circ r = r$ on $U$*: for $y \in U$, $r(y) \in r'(U')$ is a fixed point of $r'$ ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · ScRetraction.comp_eq_self_of_retract_subset⟧. *And then $Tr(TU) \subseteq Tr'(TU')$*: let $p \in Tr(TU)$, so $p \in TU$ and $Tr(p) = p$. Its base point lies in $O_1 \subseteq U'_1$, so $p \in TU'$. The map $r$ sends $U$ into $U'$ (into $O \subseteq U'$), so the chain rule applies to $r' \circ r$ on $U$: $T(r' \circ r)(p) = Tr'(Tr(p)) = Tr'(p)$; and $T(r' \circ r) = Tr$ on $TU$ because $r' \circ r = r$ on $U$ (tangent maps depend only on the values on $U$). So $Tr'(p) = Tr(p) = p$, which is the fixed-point condition for $r'$ ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · ScRetraction.tangent_subset_of_retract_subset⟧. Applied in both directions, two retractions with the same retract have the same tangent image ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · proposition_2_1_11⟧. This is the text's proof, with its step "$Tr'(x, h) = (x, h)$" made the definition of membership.

**Definition 2.1.12 (preprint 2.5).** $TO$ is $Tr(TU)$ for a retraction chosen (by choice) among those onto $O$, and the empty set if $O$ is not a retract ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · retractTangent⟧; by Proposition 2.1.11 (preprint 2.4) it equals $Tr(TU)$ for *every* retraction onto $O$ ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · retractTangent_eq⟧. The tangent space at $x \in O_1$ is the fibre $T_xO = \{h : (x, h) \in TO\}$ ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · tangentSpace⟧, the range of $Dr(x)$ ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · tangentSpace_eq_range⟧, a linear subspace ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · tangentSpace_eq_coe_submodule⟧ (closed, as the range of a continuous projection).

*Restriction and locality between retracts.* An sc-smooth map between retracts restricts to $O \cap W$: a retraction onto $O \cap W$ composed with $r$ is handled by the chain rule ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · IsScSmoothRetractMap.restrict⟧; the target may be shrunk to any set containing the image ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · IsScSmoothRetractMap.mono_target⟧. Conversely, if every point of $O$ has an open $W$ with $f$ sc-smooth on $(O \cap W, C, E)$, then $f$ is sc-smooth on $(O, C, E)$: for a retraction $r$ onto $O$ and $y \in U$, the set $W$ around $r(y)$ pulls back under the continuous $r$ to an open $u \ni y$; on $U \cap u$ the map $f \circ r$ equals $(f \circ r') \circ r$ with $r'$ the restriction of $r$ onto $O \cap W$, which is sc-smooth, and the locality of §II.1 concludes ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · IsScSmoothRetractMap.of_locally⟧. The text uses this silently every time it says "the definition does not depend on the choice of charts".

### II.4 The tangent functor on retracts

*The tangent of a partial quadrant.* If $C = T_0^{-1}(\text{quadrant})$ for $T : E \cong \mathbb R^n \oplus W$, then $TC = C_1 \times E_0$ is a partial quadrant in $TE = E^1 \oplus E$: compose the tangent of $T$, an sc-isomorphism $TE \cong T(\mathbb R^n \oplus W)$, with the re-association $((a, w), (b, v)) \mapsto (a, (w, (b, v)))$ onto $\mathbb R^n \oplus (W^1 \oplus (\mathbb R^n \oplus W))$ ⟦Chapter2/TangentBundle · tangentQuadrantIso⟧ ⟦Chapter2/TangentBundle · IsPartialQuadrant.tangentSet⟧. $TU$ is relatively open in $TC$ ⟦Chapter2/TangentBundle · IsRelOpenInQuadrant.tangentSet⟧ (this is Lemma 3.B of the exposition of Chapter 1; Part I, V1.36). Sc-operators are sc-smooth on every sc-domain ⟦Chapter2/TangentBundle · ScOperator.isScSmooth⟧.

*$Tr$ is a retraction.* $Tr$ is sc-smooth on $TU$ (Part I: the tangent map of an sc-smooth map is sc-smooth) ⟦Chapter2/Retracts · LocalModel.tangent_isScSmooth⟧, maps $TU$ into itself and is idempotent; so it is an sc-smooth retraction on $TC$ ⟦Chapter2/TangentBundle · ScRetraction.tangentRetraction⟧ with retract $TO$ ⟦Chapter2/TangentBundle · ScRetraction.tangentRetraction_retract⟧. Hence $(TO, TC, TE)$ is a retract ⟦Chapter2/TangentBundle · IsScRetract.tangentSet⟧ ⟦Chapter2/TangentBundle · LocalModel.tangent⟧, base points of $TO$ lie in $O_1$ and $(x, 0) \in TO$ for $x \in O_1$ ⟦Chapter2/TangentBundle · retractTangent_fst_mem⟧ ⟦Chapter2/TangentBundle · mk_zero_mem_retractTangent⟧.

*$Tf$ is sc-smooth.* If $f$ is sc-smooth from $O$ to $O'$ then $Tf$ is sc-smooth from $(TO, TC, TE)$ to $(TO', TC', TE')$: with the retraction $Tr$ onto $TO$, $Tf \circ Tr = T(f \circ r) \circ Tr = T(f \circ r \circ r) = T(f \circ r)$ on $TU$, which is sc-smooth ⟦Chapter2/TangentBundle · IsScSmoothRetractMap.tangent⟧. $Tf$ depends only on $f|_O$ ⟦Chapter2/TangentBundle · retractTangentMap_congr⟧, and a map equal to the identity on $O$ has tangent map the identity on $TO$ ⟦Chapter2/TangentBundle · retractTangentMap_id⟧. ### II.5 Charts, atlases and M-polyfolds (Definitions 2.3.2–2.3.8 (preprint 2.11–2.14))

**Definition 2.3.2 (preprint 2.11).** A *chart* on $X$ is a local model $(O, C, E)$ and a partial equivalence $\varphi$ between $X$ and $E_0$ with open source $V$ and target $O$, continuous on $V$, with inverse continuous on $O$ ⟦Chapter2/MPolyfold · MChart⟧. The overlap $\varphi(V \cap V')$ ⟦Chapter2/MPolyfold · MChart.overlap⟧ is a retract ⟦Chapter2/MPolyfold · MChart.isScRetract_overlap⟧, the transition map is $\psi \circ \varphi^{-1}$ ⟦Chapter2/MPolyfold · MChart.transition⟧, mapping $\varphi(V \cap V')$ into $\psi(V \cap V')$ ⟦Chapter2/MPolyfold · MChart.transition_mapsTo⟧, and two charts are *compatible* if both transition maps are sc-smooth between the overlap retracts ⟦Chapter2/MPolyfold · MChart.Compatible⟧. Compatibility is symmetric, reflexive, and passes to restrictions ⟦Chapter2/MPolyfold · MChart.Compatible.symm⟧ ⟦Chapter2/MPolyfold · MChart.compatible_self⟧ ⟦Chapter2/MPolyfold · MChart.Compatible.restrict⟧.

*Compatibility is local.* Let $\varphi$, $\psi$ be charts and $S$ a family of charts each compatible with $\varphi$ and with $\psi$, whose sources cover $V \cap V'$. Then $\varphi$ and $\psi$ are compatible ⟦Chapter2/MPolyfold · MChart.compatible_of_locally⟧. By the locality between retracts (§II.3) it suffices to show that $\psi \circ \varphi^{-1}$ is sc-smooth near every point $\varphi(x)$ of the overlap. Take $\chi \in S$ around $x$ and the triple overlap $s = V \cap V' \cap V''$, with $\varphi(s) = \varphi(V \cap V') \cap W$ for an open $W$. On $\varphi(s)$, $\psi \circ \varphi^{-1} = (\psi \circ \chi^{-1}) \circ (\chi \circ \varphi^{-1})$ ⟦Chapter2/MPolyfold · MChart.transition_eq_comp_on⟧; the two factors are sc-smooth on the restricted retracts $\varphi(s)$ and $\chi(s)$, and Theorem 2.1.15 (preprint 2.8) composes them. The reverse transition is the same argument with the roles exchanged. This is the step that makes "the equivalence class of atlases" a usable notion; the text does not discuss it.

**Definition 2.3.3 (preprint 2.12).** An *atlas* is a set of pairwise compatible charts covering $X$ ⟦Chapter2/MPolyfold · MAtlas⟧; two atlases are *equivalent* if their union is an atlas ⟦Chapter2/MPolyfold · MAtlas.Equiv⟧, i.e. if every chart of one is compatible with every chart of the other ⟦Chapter2/MPolyfold · MAtlas.equiv_iff⟧. **Definition 2.3.4 (preprint 2.13).** An *M-polyfold* structure on a Hausdorff paracompact space is an atlas ⟦Chapter2/MPolyfold · MPolyfold⟧. By locality of compatibility (with $S$ the atlas), any two of them are compatible ⟦Chapter2/MPolyfold · MPolyfold.IsChart.compatible⟧; restrictions of charts are charts ⟦Chapter2/MPolyfold · MPolyfold.IsChart.restrict⟧; every point lies in one ⟦Chapter2/MPolyfold · MPolyfold.exists_isChart_mem_source⟧; and equivalent atlases have the same charts, again by locality ⟦Chapter2/MPolyfold · MPolyfold.isChart_congr⟧. **Definition 2.3.8 (preprint 2.14).** $f : X \to Y$ is *sc-smooth on the open set $U$* if it is continuous on $U$ and for all charts $\varphi$ of $X$, $\psi$ of $Y$ and every open $V \subseteq U \cap \operatorname{dom}\varphi$ with $f(V) \subseteq \operatorname{dom}\psi$, the representation $\psi \circ f \circ \varphi^{-1}$ is sc-smooth from $(\varphi(V), C, E)$ into $(O', C', E')$ ⟦Chapter2/MPolyfold · MPolyfold.IsScSmoothOn⟧; *sc-smooth* means sc-smooth on $X$ ⟦Chapter2/MPolyfold · MPolyfold.IsScSmoothMap⟧. ### II.7 Metrizability: Smirnov's theorem and Theorem 2.3.10 (preprint 2.16) (Theorem 2.7.1 (preprint 2.55))

The text proves Theorem 2.3.10 (preprint 2.16) with Smirnov's theorem (2.55), Michael's characterization of paracompactness (Lemma 2.7.3 (preprint 2.56)) and Proposition 2.7.4 (preprint 2.57) (a regular space with a locally finite closed cover by paracompact subspaces is paracompact). Proposition 2.7.4 (preprint 2.57) needs $X_m$ to be regular, which the text does not check. The formal proof needs neither 2.56 nor 2.57.

*The metrization lemma.* Let $Y$ have open subspaces $V_i$, each metrizable with a metric $d_i$, and continuous $\rho_i : Y \to [0, \infty)$ with $\operatorname{supp}\rho_i \subseteq V_i$, the supports locally finite and $\rho_i(y) > 0$ for some $i$ at every $y$ ⟦Chapter2/Metrizable · metrizableSpace_of_partition⟧. Put $\delta_i(x, y) := \min(d_i(x, y), 1)$ for $x, y \in V_i$ and $1$ otherwise ⟦Chapter2/Metrizable · truncDist⟧ — nonnegative, at most $1$, symmetric, zero only on the diagonal, and satisfying the triangle inequality inside $V_i$ ⟦Chapter2/Metrizable · truncDist_nonneg⟧ ⟦Chapter2/Metrizable · truncDist_le_one⟧ ⟦Chapter2/Metrizable · truncDist_triangle⟧ ⟦Chapter2/Metrizable · truncDist_eq_zero⟧ — and the *cone metric*
$$e_i(x, y) := |\rho_i(x) - \rho_i(y)| + \min(\rho_i(x), \rho_i(y))\,\delta_i(x, y),\qquad D(x, y) := \sum_i e_i(x, y).$$
The sum is finite (only the $i$ with $x$ or $y$ in $\operatorname{supp}\rho_i$ contribute). $D(x, x) = 0$ and $D$ is symmetric. *Triangle inequality:* termwise, by an elementary inequality for $a, b, c \ge 0$ and $\delta$'s bounded by $1$, which needs the triangle inequality for $\delta_i$ only when $\min(\rho_i(x), \rho_i(z)) > 0$ and $\rho_i(y) > 0$, i.e. when all three points lie in $V_i$ ⟦Chapter2/Metrizable · cone_triangle⟧. *Separation:* if $D(x, y) = 0$, take $i$ with $\rho_i(x) > 0$; then $\rho_i(y) = \rho_i(x)$ and $\delta_i(x, y) = 0$, so $x = y$. *Topology:* $y \mapsto D(x, y)$ is continuous (locally a finite sum of continuous functions; the term $\min(\rho_i(x), \rho_i(y))\delta_i(x, y)$ is continuous where $y \in V_i$ and vanishes near points outside $\operatorname{supp}\rho_i$), so $D$-balls are open; conversely, if $s$ is open and $x \in s$, take $i$ with $\rho_i(x) > 0$ and a $d_i$-ball of radius $r \le 1$ around $x$ inside $s$; then $D(x, y) < \frac12\rho_i(x) r$ forces $\rho_i(y) > \frac12\rho_i(x)$ (so $y \in V_i$) and $\frac12\rho_i(x)\delta_i(x, y) < \frac12\rho_i(x) r$, so $d_i(x, y) < r$ and $y \in s$. Mathlib then builds a metric space structure with this distance and the given topology ⟦Mathlib/Topology/MetricSpace/Defs · MetricSpace.ofDistTopology⟧.

*Pulled back, and Smirnov.* If $\pi : Y \to X$ is continuous into a paracompact Hausdorff $X$ and every point of $X$ has an open $U$ with $\pi^{-1}(U)$ metrizable, then $Y$ is metrizable: a partition of unity of $X$ subordinate to these $U$ ⟦Mathlib/Topology/PartitionOfUnity · PartitionOfUnity.exists_isSubordinate⟧, composed with $\pi$, satisfies the hypotheses of the lemma with $V_i = \pi^{-1}(U_i)$ ⟦Chapter2/Metrizable · metrizableSpace_of_pullback⟧. With $\pi = \mathrm{id}$ this is **Smirnov's theorem** (Theorem 2.7.1 (preprint 2.55)) ⟦Chapter2/Metrizable · metrizableSpace_of_locally⟧. Metrizable spaces are paracompact (Stone; in Mathlib through the instance for pseudo-emetric spaces) ⟦Chapter2/Metrizable · paracompactSpace_of_metrizableSpace⟧.

**Theorem 2.3.10 (preprint 2.16).** *$X$ is metrizable*: chart domains are homeomorphic to subsets $O$ of the Banach space $E_0$ ⟦Chapter2/Metrizable · MChart.sourceHomeomorph⟧ ⟦Chapter2/Metrizable · MChart.metrizableSpace_source⟧, and Smirnov applies ⟦Chapter2/Metrizable · MPolyfold.metrizableSpace⟧. *$V_m \cong O_m$*: the lift $x \mapsto \text{lift}_m(x)$ is a topological embedding of $V \cap X_m$ into $E_m$ ⟦Chapter2/Metrizable · MPolyfold.isEmbedding_levelLift⟧. *The fibres.* $P_f^{-1}(z) \to P^{-1}(f(z))$, $(z, y) \mapsto y$, is a bijection ⟦Chapter2/StrongBundlePullback · pullbackFiberEquiv⟧; transporting the linear structure along it ⟦Chapter2/StrongBundlePullback · pullbackFiber_addCommGroup⟧ ⟦Chapter2/StrongBundlePullback · pullbackFiber_module⟧ makes it a linear homeomorphism ⟦Chapter2/StrongBundlePullback · pullbackFiberCLE⟧, so the fibres of $f^*Y$ are Banachable.

*The charts.* The data: a strong bundle chart $c$ of $Y$, a chart $\psi$ of $Z$ with $f(\operatorname{dom}\psi) \subseteq \operatorname{dom}\varphi$, a retraction $r_Z$ onto the model of $\psi$, and the sc-smoothness of $g = \varphi \circ f \circ \psi^{-1}$ ⟦Chapter2/StrongBundlePullback · PullbackChartData⟧ ⟦Chapter2/StrongBundlePullback · PullbackChartData.g⟧ ⟦Chapter2/StrongBundlePullback · PullbackChartData.g_smooth⟧. ### II.26 The 2021 edition: faces of open sets and the corner axioms (§2.7.4, Propositions 2.7.5, 2.7.7)

Appendix §2.7.4 is new in the 2021 edition. It builds from a tame M-polyfold $X$ a space $bX$ of "points with a chosen face" and a map $B : bX \to X$. The formalization proves all of it for an abstract $d : X \to \mathbb N$ satisfying *corner axioms*, then specializes to the degeneracy index of a tame M-polyfold. The same proofs serve for the object spaces of tame ep-groupoids in 2021 §7.6.5.

**Faces of open sets.** For an open $V$ and $p \in V$ with $d(p) = 1$, the face of $V$ through $p$ is the closure in $V$ of the connected component of $V \cap \{d = 1\}$ through $p$ ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundary · TameBoundary.faceAt⟧ ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundary · TameBoundary.IsFaceIn⟧. $F_X(y, V)$ is the set of faces of $V$ through $y$ ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundary · TameBoundary.facesThrough⟧. For an M-polyfold the faces of $X$ itself are those of Definition 2.5.10 (preprint 2.39) ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundary · MPolyfold.isFaceIn_univ_iff⟧. The text's faces of $V$ are those of $V$ with its induced structure, whose degeneracy index is $d_X|V$ (2021 Proposition 2.4.4), so defining them from $d_X$ is the same thing.

**The corner axioms** ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundary · TameBoundary.IsCornerIndex⟧. First, $d$ is upper semicontinuous (Lemma 2.4.3 (preprint 2.23)). Second, every $x$ has an open $D$ such that every $y \in D$ has *local pieces* in arbitrarily small neighbourhoods $N$: an open $B \ni y$ in $N$ and $d(y)$ preconnected sets $\ell_i \subseteq N \cap \{d = 1\}$ accumulating at $y$ and covering $B \cap \{d = 1\}$ ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundary · TameBoundary.LocalPieces⟧. Third, the pieces are separated in $D$: no preconnected subset of $D \cap \{d = 1\}$ meets two of them. In a tame chart the pieces are the images under $r$ of the sets $\{z \in C : |z - \varphi(y)| < \varepsilon,\ I(z) = \{i\}\}$, $i \in I(\varphi(y))$, from the local face structure of §II.15 ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundary · MPolyfold.exists_localPieces_chart⟧. So the degeneracy index of a tame M-polyfold satisfies the axioms ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundary · MPolyfold.isCornerIndex_degIndex⟧, and $d \equiv 0$ does trivially ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundary · TameBoundary.isCornerIndex_zero⟧. Faces of nested opens are compatible: a face of $V$ meets an open $U \subseteq V$ in a union of faces of $U$ ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundary · TameBoundary.faceAt_inter_eq⟧. A face $\theta$ of $U$ lies in a unique face of $V$, the union $\theta'$ of the faces of $V$ containing it ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundary · TameBoundary.faceExt⟧ ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundary · TameBoundary.faceExt_unique⟧. Every face of $V$ through a point of $U$ comes from a face of $U$ ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundary · TameBoundary.exists_faceExt_eq⟧.

**Proposition 2.7.5.** *Face-structured neighbourhoods exist* ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundary · TameBoundary.exists_nbhd_forall_isFaceStructuredOpen⟧. Take $D$ from the axioms. For an open $V \subseteq D$ and $y \in V$, the local pieces of $y$ inside $V$ lie on faces of $V$, and every face through $y$ contains a piece. Distinct pieces lie on distinct faces: a face through two pieces would give a preconnected subset of $D \cap \{d = 1\}$ meeting both, which separation forbids. So $y$ lies on exactly $d(y)$ faces of $V$. Then $V = D \cap \{d \le d(x)\}$, open by semicontinuity, gives (1) and (2). For (3), on any open $V \ni x$, take the set $B$ of the local pieces of $x$ inside $V$: a face of $V$ through a point of $B$ contains one of the pieces of $x$, which accumulate at $x$, and faces are closed in $V$ ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundary · TameBoundary.exists_nbhd_faces_mem⟧. Together these prove the proposition ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundary · TameBoundary.proposition_2_7_5⟧ ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundary · MPolyfold.proposition_2_7_5⟧.

**Definition 2.7.6 and Proposition 2.7.7.** The definitions:
- an open $V$ is *face-structured* if every $y \in V$ lies on exactly $d(y)$ faces of $V$ ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundary · TameBoundary.IsFaceStructuredOpen⟧ ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundary · TameBoundary.faceStructuredOpens⟧;
- $N_X$ is the set of the nonempty face-structured $V$ with $d|V$ bounded ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundary · TameBoundary.faceStructuredNbhds⟧;
- $N_{X,x}$ is the set of those $V \in N_X$ with $x \in V$ and $d \le d(x)$ on $V$ ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundary · TameBoundary.faceStructuredNbhdsAt⟧.

$N_X = \bigcup_x N_{X,x}$, because a $V \in N_X$ contains a point where the bounded $d|V$ is maximal ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundary · TameBoundary.faceStructuredNbhds_eq_iUnion⟧. An open subset of a face-structured set is face-structured: its faces through a point are the traces of those of the larger set, by the local pieces ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundary · TameBoundary.IsFaceStructuredOpen.mono⟧. Hence Proposition 2.7.7:
- $N_{X,x}$ is nonempty (Proposition 2.7.5) ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundary · TameBoundary.faceStructuredNbhdsAt_nonempty⟧, so $N_X$ covers $X$ ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundary · TameBoundary.sUnion_faceStructuredNbhds⟧;

- nonempty intersections of members of $N_X$ are in $N_X$ ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundary · TameBoundary.inter_mem_faceStructuredNbhds⟧;

*Quadrants.* In $[0, \infty)^n \oplus W$, $d$ counts the vanishing half-line coordinates ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundaryExample · TameBoundaryExample.dq_eq⟧. $\{d = 1\}$ is the union of the strata $\{p_k = 0,\ p_l > 0\ (l \ne k)\}$ ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundaryExample · TameBoundaryExample.part⟧ ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundaryExample · TameBoundaryExample.dq_eq_one_iff⟧. ### II.31 The 2021 edition, continued: Remark 2.1.6 is strict (the K-topology)

Two such charts have the transition map $\pi' \circ (\varphi' \circ \varphi^{-1}) \circ \iota$, $\iota$ the face inclusion, which is sc-smooth ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundaryStructure · MPolyfold.BChartData.chart_transition_isScSmooth⟧, so they are compatible ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundaryStructure · MPolyfold.BChartData.chart_compatible⟧. By the local faces, every point of $\theta$ lies in the $V_1$ of a datum with the triple $(x, V, \theta)$, and every class has a centred representative ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundaryStructure · TameBoundary.BSpace.exists_centred_rep⟧; so the charts cover $bX$ ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundaryStructure · MPolyfold.exists_bChartData⟧ and form an atlas ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundaryStructure · MPolyfold.bAtlas⟧.

**Theorem 2.7.13 (2)–(4).** $T_{bX}$ is metrizable (§II.29), hence paracompact ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundaryStructure · MPolyfold.paracompactSpace_tameBoundarySpace⟧, and with the atlas $bX$ is an M-polyfold ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundaryStructure · MPolyfold.tameBoundaryPolyfold⟧, tame because the models are tame retracts ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundaryStructure · MPolyfold.tameBoundaryPolyfold_isTame⟧ (2). **The characterization, centred representatives.** A face $\theta$ of a face-structured open $V$ is a sub-M-polyfold of $X$ ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundaryStructure · MPolyfold.isSubMPolyfold_of_isFaceIn⟧, which we take as "the structure induced from $V$" ⟦Chapter2/TameBoundaryStructure · MPolyfold.facePolyfold⟧.
