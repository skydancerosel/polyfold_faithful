**Seven structural choices.** (1) *The tangent is Part I's.* $TX$ and $T\mathbf X$ are Part I's tangent M-polyfolds and $Ts$, $Tt$, $Tu$, $T\iota$ Part I's tangent maps (§§II.8, II.9); for $X$ in `Type u` they live in `Type (max u 1)`, and so does the tangent functor of Theorem 8.1.12 (2017: 8.3). The fibres $T_xX$ carry no linear structure (gap G.7), so no statement about the linearity of a tangent map is formalized. (2) *The composition of $T\mathbf X$ is defined by choice*: total, with a junk value off composable pairs, as Chapter 7's composition, and unique on composable pairs. (3) *Fibre injectivity.* $s$ being a local sc-diffeomorphism, two vectors of $T\mathbf X$ with the same base point and the same image under $Ts$ are equal; most identities of §8.1 are proved this way instead of by differentiating identities. (4) *Tangents of maps defined on open sets* are built from the charts, as Part I builds $Tf$, with a junk value off $p^{-1}(U)$. (5) *Condition (4) in sheet form*, as in Chapter 7 (§VII.2): for $T\mathbf X$, for the functorial picture of a strong bundle and for the ep-groupoid of a geometric lift; Lemma 8.1.2 (2017: 8.1) is stated in this form. (6) *Strong bundle maps between strong bundles over M-polyfolds are a reading*: Part I defines strong bundle maps only between local models, and the global notion is a map covering a map of the bases, linear on the fibres, sc-smooth on $[0]$ and on $[1]$ (§VIII.10). (7) *Data, not hypotheses.* "A connected and sufficiently small $U(x)$ admitting the natural $G_x$-action" in Theorems 8.5.3 and 8.5.8 (2017: 8.5 and 8.6) is a datum, a chart of Theorem 8.5.3 (2017: 8.5), as natural representations are in Chapter 7; Definition 8.5.5 (2017: 8.9) is stated with Mathlib's groupoids, $C$ being `SingleObj G`, and its finiteness enters as instance arguments of the results that use it.

### VIII.2 Theorem 8.1.1 (2017: 8.1), and Lemma 8.1.2 (2017: 8.1)

**Theorem 8.1.1 (2017: 8.1)** (V8.3) ⟦Chapter8/TangentEp · EpGroupoid.theorem_8_1_1⟧. *$T(X, \mathbf X) = (TX, T\mathbf X)$ with the structure maps (8.3)–(8.9) is an ep-groupoid; it is tame if $X$ is; and the projections form an sc-smooth functor $(p, \mathbf p) : T(X, \mathbf X) \to (X, \mathbf X)^1$.* The text proves it on pp. 363–365 (2017 pp. 263–264) by collecting Lemmas 8.1.2–8.1.9 (2017: 8.1–8.8), and the formalization does the same. The groupoid laws are Lemmas 8.1.3–8.1.6 (2017: 8.2–8.5) (§VIII.3) ⟦Chapter8/TangentEp · EpGroupoid.tangentGroupoidStr⟧; conditions (1), (2) and (4) of Definition 7.1.3 (2017: 7.3) are Lemma 8.1.7 (2017: 8.6) and condition (3) is Lemma 8.1.8 (2017: 8.7) (§VIII.4); together they make the ep-groupoid ⟦Chapter8/TangentEp · EpGroupoid.tangentEpGroupoid⟧, and `theorem_8_1_1` records that it has the M-polyfolds $TX$, $T\mathbf X$, the structure maps of the text, and the base point and source of a composite. ### VIII.28 What the formalization taught us

**Fibre injectivity replaces calculus on fibred products.** The text proves the groupoid laws of $T(X, \mathbf X)$, Theorem 8.1.11 (2017: 8.2) and Proposition 8.1.14 (2017: 8.1) by differentiating identities on $\mathbf X\,{}_s{\times}_t\,\mathbf X$, which needs the tangent of a fibred product. Since $s$ is a local sc-diffeomorphism, a vector of $T\mathbf X$ is determined by its base point and its image under $Ts$; base points are computed in $\mathbf X$ and images under $Ts$ by Lemma 8.1.3 (2017: 8.2). Only one identity of §8.1, the target formula of Lemma 8.1.3 (2017: 8.2), is genuinely differentiated, and the identification $T(\mathbf X\,{}_s{\times}_t\,\mathbf X) = T\mathbf X\,{}_{Ts}{\times}_{Tt}\,T\mathbf X$ is never needed (§§VIII.3, VIII.7, VIII.8).

**The tangent calculus of §8.1 lives on open sets.** Every proof of §8.1 differentiates a map defined only near a point: a local inverse of $s$, the map $\alpha$ of Lemma 8.1.3 (2017: 8.2), $\hat\psi$. Part I differentiates only globally sc-smooth maps. Tangents of maps on open sets, with the chain rule and the tangents of local sc-diffeomorphisms, are the infrastructure the chapter rests on, and Lemma 8.1.7's (2017: 8.6) "by construction" is exactly their last property (§VIII.1).

**Definition 8.5.1 (3) (2017: 8.8) carries Theorem 8.5.3 (2017: 8.5).** The text builds the sheets $\mathbf U(\psi_0)$ from natural representations of the groups $G_y$ in $Y$ and never invokes condition (3). With (3), the sheets are images of the continuous sections $z \mapsto L_F^{-1}(\Gamma(g, F z), z)$ of $s$, and connectedness of $U(y)$ does the rest; neither the finiteness of the fibre nor condition (1) is used. The same condition counts the morphisms over a fibre in §8.6.1 (2017 §8.5.1) and gives the unique lifting behind Theorem 8.5.8 (2017: 8.6) (§§VIII.17, VIII.21, VIII.23).

**A functor commutes with $s$, not with "$s$ on one side and $t$ on the other".** Theorem 8.5.3 (5) (2017: 8.5) as printed equates $F(s(\psi))$ with $t(F(\psi))$; a non-trivial element of $G_x$ moves points, so the square fails for the reflection of $\mathbb R$ in every chart at $0$. The two squares that functoriality gives, with $s$ on both sides and with $t$ on both sides, are what the proofs downstream use (§VIII.17).

**Empty groupoids and non-tame products.** Two statements needed a hypothesis that the text does not state: Proposition 8.5.7 (2017: 8.3) needs $D \ne \emptyset$, because Definition 8.5.5 (2017: 8.9) admits the empty groupoid, over which the functor of Proposition 8.5.7 (2017: 8.3) is not surjective; and the degeneracy remark after Definition 8.5.1 (2017: 8.8) needs tameness, the only form of the product formula $d_{\mathbf X \times Y} = d_{\mathbf X} + d_Y$ that Part I proves. In both cases the hypothesis holds where the text uses the statement (§§VIII.15, VIII.20).

**Metrizability is the shorter road to paracompactness, again.** As for Proposition 7.5.3 (2017: 7.12), Theorem 8.1.15 (2017: 8.4) follows from Smirnov's theorem pulled back along $|TX| \to |X^1|$: $|TX|$ is metrizable, which is stronger than paracompact, and the closed paracompact sets $\tilde A_z$, the regularity of $|TX|$ and Proposition 2.7.4 (2017: 2.17) drop out. What remains of the text's argument is the metrizability of $|TU(x_z)|$, and for it the open embedding $G_x\backslash TU(x) \to |TX|$, which the text leaves implicit (§VIII.9).

**Strong bundles over ep-groupoids need more of Part I than Part I states.** A global notion of strong bundle map, the atlas and universal property of the pull-back on both levels $[0]$ and $[1]$, the sum of sc-smooth sections, and the strong bundle structure on $\mathbf V\,{}_s{\times}_A\,W$ are all used in §8.3 and Definition 8.5.9 (2017: 8.10) without being defined or proved in Part I. With them in place, the claims of §8.3 that the text states without proof, the functorial picture and the identity of Definitions 8.3.2 and 8.3.3 (2017: 8.5 and 8.6), are routine (§§VIII.10–VIII.14, VIII.22).
