**Six structural choices.** (1) *Equivalences as a structure.* `EpFunctor.IsEquivalence` has the four conditions of Definition 10.1.1 as fields, with full and faithful stated on the sets of morphisms; Mathlib's `Functor.IsEquivalence` of the underlying functor is derived (§X.1). (2) *Indexed coverings.* The refinement $X_{\mathcal U}$ takes a family $(U_i)_{i \in \iota}$ of open sets; objects and morphisms are open subsets of products with the discrete $\iota$ (§X.2). (3) *Iterated fibred products.* The weak fibred product is built from Part I's fibred products, twice, so that its M-polyfold structures come with their universal properties; the admissibility, $F$ or $G$ a local sc-diffeomorphism on objects, is all that the construction uses (§X.9). (4) *Total pull-backs and push-forwards.* $\Phi^*$ and $\Phi_*$ are defined on all maps, by the zero of the fibre where needed, and the push-forward with one choice of $x$ and $\sigma$ made for all; all statements are about section functors, where the choice does not matter (§X.12). (5) *Diagrams one universe up.* A diagram contains its middle ep-groupoid as an object of $\mathcal{EP}$, so the classes of diagrams form a type in the universe of the objects of $\mathcal{EP}$ and $\mathcal{EP}(E^{-1})$ is a large category like $\mathcal{EP}$; compositions are written in Mathlib's order, $d.\mathrm{comp}\,e$ for the text's $[e] \circ [d]$ (§X.16). (6) *The weak fibred product of strong bundles as a pull-back.* The text leaves it to the reader; it is the pull-back of $W''$ along $\pi_Z$, so that one projection is a strong bundle equivalence by construction and the squares in the proofs of the composition commute strictly, by the fibrewise injectivity of strong bundle equivalences; the generalized strong bundle maps, defined as the classes of the equivalence relation generated by having a common refinement, are the text's classes by the transitivity (§§X.21, X.22).

### X.26 What the formalization taught us

The equivalences of §10.1 are the sc-smooth versions of equivalences of categories, and most proofs of the section are categorical arguments plus one local fact: a functor which is a local sc-diffeomorphism on objects at $s(\varphi)$ is one at $t(\varphi)$. The text uses this fact silently in Proposition 10.1.6, where the argument covers only the image of $F$; with it the proof is two lines. Proposition 10.1.5, quoted without proof, and the fullness and faithfulness of $TF$, left to the reader, are short once tangent morphisms are known to be determined by their base and source vector. The invariance results new in 2021 split into two kinds: those about $\mathbf X^T$ ((LUC), the reduced groupoids, (SLUC)) go through Theorem 7.2.22 and one closure argument; those about natural representations ((LR), (SLR), Proposition 10.1.10) need natural representations carried along $Q = \hat\psi \circ F$, which is done by restricting a natural representation at the target rather than by transporting $\Gamma$. The local families of Proposition 10.1.10 are not natural in general, and that is where Theorem 10.3.19 does not hold as we read it: on $\mathbb Z/3 \times \mathbb R \rightrightarrows \mathbb R$ with the trivial action, which has (LR) because its orbits are points, the identity and the inversion have the same orbit map and are not naturally equivalent; over an effective target the families are unique and glue, and the theorem holds without the weak fibred product of its proof. In §10.2 the weak fibred product needs much less than an equivalence: one local sc-diffeomorphism on objects for the structure, nothing for properness. In §10.3 the two places where the text argues with spaces, "the same space" for the associativity and the direct check that $L$ is an equivalence, become functors and two-out-of-three arguments, and the local half of the hard direction of Theorem 10.3.10 needs a different route, through a local right inverse and local injectivity. In §10.4 the push-forward is well defined on section functors because of fullness (10.22), and once (10.22) is in hand its sc-smoothness is a local formula with two local inverses; Propositions 10.4.2–10.4.4 do not need sc-smoothness at all. In §10.5 the push-forward along a generalized strong bundle isomorphism is best seen through the orbit spaces: a section functor is determined by the orbits of its values, so the push-forward depends only on $|[D]|$, which gives the independence of the representative and the last assertion of Theorem 10.5.4 at once; and the example the text does not give for Remark 10.5.5 needs an invariant finer than the orbit maps, the relation of the isotropy homomorphisms $\gamma$, which also settles the non-uniqueness left informal in Remark 10.3.18. The weak fibred product of strong bundles, which the text leaves to the reader, is best taken as a pull-back: one projection is then a strong bundle equivalence by construction, and since strong bundle equivalences are injective on fibres, the squares in the proofs of transitivity, composition and associativity commute strictly, so that the proofs of §10.3 carry over.
