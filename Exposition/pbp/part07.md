**Seven structural choices.** (1) *A groupoid is a structure of maps.* Objects and morphisms are types; $s, t, u, \iota$ are maps and the composition $m$ is a total map whose value on a non-composable pair is a junk value never used; the groupoid laws are required on composable pairs. A bridge to Mathlib's groupoids makes "full" and "faithful" Mathlib's. (2) *Properness is Mathlib's.* Definition 7.1.3 (3) (2017: 7.3) is the properness, in Mathlib's sense, of $t$ restricted to $s^{-1}(\mathrm{cl}_X V)$, for some open $V \ni x$, per point; on metrizable spaces it is equivalent to the sequential form the text uses (§VII.2). (3) *Condition (4) in two forms.* It is stated literally, on the fibred product of Part I's Proposition 2.5.20 (2017: 2.15; preprint 2.46, §II.17), and used in the equivalent *sheet form*: for every local inverse $\sigma$ of $s$, the map $\psi \mapsto m(\sigma(t\psi), \psi)$ is sc-smooth. (4) *Natural representations are data.* "An open neighbourhood $U(x)$ equipped with the natural $G_x$-action" is always a datum $(U, \Phi, \Gamma)$ with $U$ open, connected and containing $x$, never a hypothesis "for every such $U(x)$". (5) *$G \times U$ is a product with a discrete M-polyfold*, the finite disjoint union of copies of $U$, on which sc-smoothness is sc-smoothness on each slice (§VII.8). (6) *The reduced morphisms* $\mathbf X^R$ are a quotient whose M-polyfold structure is built under the hypotheses that it is Hausdorff and paracompact (§VII.16). (7) *Michael's theorem without Hausdorff.* Part I's Lemma 2.7.3 (2017: 2.7) and Proposition 2.7.4 (2017: 2.17) (gap G.15 there) are proved for regular spaces, and Mathlib's paracompactness, like Definition 7.5.1 (2017: 7.15), does not include Hausdorff (§VII.20).

### VII.1 Definitions 7.1.1 and 7.1.2 (2017: 7.1 and 7.2): groupoids, the orbit space, isotropy

**Definition 7.1.1 (2017: 7.1)** (V7.1). ### VII.2 Definition 7.1.3 (2017: 7.3), and properness

**Definition 7.1.3 (2017: 7.3)** (V7.3) ⟦Chapter7/EpGroupoid · EpGroupoid⟧. An ep-groupoid is a groupoid with M-polyfold structures on the objects and on the morphisms such that (1) $s$ and $t$ are local sc-diffeomorphisms, (2) $u$ and $\iota$ are sc-smooth, (3) every $x$ has an open neighbourhood $V(x)$ with $t : s^{-1}(\mathrm{cl}_X V(x)) \to X$ proper, and (4) $m$ is sc-smooth on $\mathbf X\,{}_s{\times}_t\,\mathbf X$. "In particular all structure maps are sc-smooth": $s$ and $t$ are ⟦Chapter7/EpGroupoid · EpGroupoid.isScSmoothMap_src⟧ ⟦Chapter7/EpGroupoid · EpGroupoid.isScSmoothMap_tgt⟧, and continuous ⟦Chapter7/EpGroupoid · EpGroupoid.continuous_src⟧ ⟦Chapter7/EpGroupoid · EpGroupoid.continuous_tgt⟧ ⟦Chapter7/EpGroupoid · EpGroupoid.continuous_unit⟧ ⟦Chapter7/EpGroupoid · EpGroupoid.continuous_inv⟧ (pp. 288–290 (2017 pp. 226–227)).

*Condition (3).* It is existential, one open $V(x)$ per point, as the text writes it. The universal reading, "for every open $V \ni x$", fails: in the pair groupoid of $\mathbb Z$ (objects $\mathbb Z$, one morphism between any two objects) $t$ is proper over $s^{-1}(\{x\})$ but not over $s^{-1}(\mathbb Z)$ ⟦Chapter7/NonVacuity · Examples.pair_isProperMap⟧ ⟦Chapter7/NonVacuity · Examples.pair_not_isProperMap_univ⟧ ⟦Chapter7/NonVacuity · Examples.pair_not_forall_isProperMap⟧; every smaller open neighbourhood of $x$ works as soon as one does (§VII.25) (Findings, Chapter 7, items 3, 103).

*Condition (4).* The text cites "Lemma 2.8 in [38]" for the M-polyfold structure of the fibred product; this is an external reference, and the statement used is Part I's Proposition 2.5.20 (2017: 2.15) (§II.17): since $s$ is a local sc-diffeomorphism, $\mathbf X\,{}_s{\times}_t\,\mathbf X$ is a sub-M-polyfold of $\mathbf X \times \mathbf X$ ⟦Chapter2/FibredProduct · MPolyfold.isSubMPolyfold_fibredProduct⟧, and (4) is stated on it ⟦Chapter7/EpGroupoid · IsScSmoothComp⟧. The proofs use an equivalent *sheet form* ⟦Chapter7/EpGroupoid · IsScSmoothCompSheet⟧: for every sc-diffeomorphism $s : \mathbf W \to V$ with inverse $\sigma$, the map $\psi \mapsto m(\sigma(t\psi), \psi)$ is sc-smooth on $t^{-1}(V)$. *Step 1.* The graph of $\sigma \circ t$ over $t^{-1}(V)$ is an open piece of the fibred product, and the second projection is an sc-diffeomorphism from it onto $t^{-1}(V)$, with inverse $\psi \mapsto (\sigma(t\psi), \psi)$. *Step 2.* So the literal (4) gives the sheet form by composition, and conversely the fibred product is covered by such graphs ⟦Chapter7/EpGroupoid · isScSmoothComp_iff_sheet⟧. Every ep-groupoid satisfies the sheet form ⟦Chapter7/EpGroupoid · EpGroupoid.isScSmoothCompSheet⟧. The "for every $\sigma$" of the sheet form carries no vacuity risk, being equivalent to the literal condition (Findings, Chapter 7, item 4).

**Properness in sequential form** (V7.4). The text uses properness through sequences: in Proposition 7.1.10 (2017: 7.3), Lemma 7.1.11 (2017: 7.1), Proposition 7.1.22 (2017: 7.7), Proposition 7.3.9 (2017: 7.11), Proposition 7.5.9 (2017: 7.13) and Lemma 7.6.2 (2017: 7.8). For a continuous map between metrizable spaces, properness is equivalent to: every sequence whose images converge has a convergent subsequence ⟦Chapter7/ProperMaps · isProperMap_iff_exists_tendsto_subseq⟧. *Step 1, from properness.* The images and their limit form a compact set, whose preimage is compact, hence sequentially compact ⟦Chapter7/ProperMaps · exists_tendsto_subseq_of_isProperMap⟧. *Step 2, to properness.* Preimages of compact sets are sequentially compact, hence compact in a metrizable space, and a continuous map with compact fibres of compacta into a compactly generated space is proper (Mathlib's characterization) ⟦Chapter7/ProperMaps · isProperMap_of_exists_tendsto_subseq⟧. ### VII.32 What the formalization taught us

**Continuity is the content of the remark after Definition 7.1.7 (2017: 7.7).** The formula $f(\gamma) = (s|\mathbf U(\psi))^{-1}(f(s\gamma))$ is right wherever it applies; what it needs is a neighbourhood of $\varphi$ mapped into the sheet at $f(\varphi)$, and for that the functor must be continuous on morphisms. A functor of the trivial $\mathbb Z/3$-action that swaps two isotropy elements on one side of $0$ shows that sc-smoothness on objects alone does not give it, as we read the remark (§VII.4).

**Connectedness is the hidden hypothesis of the natural representation.** Every proof that $\Phi$ is a homomorphism, that $\Gamma$ is a functor, that $\Gamma(1, a) = 1_a$ (Lemma 7.2.2 (2017: 7.2)), and that two natural representations agree compares two continuous sections of $s$ that agree at $x$; without connectedness they agree only near $x$. The text chooses a connected $U(x)$ on 2017 p. 231 and never states it again. Making $U$ connected a field of the datum, and proving that M-polyfolds are locally path connected, settles all of these at once (§§VII.7–VII.9, VII.26).

**"Small" neighbourhoods and the kernel of $\Phi$.** Since sc-smooth maps are not analytic, the kernel of $\Phi$ can depend on $U(x)$, and the text's "for $U(x)$ small, $\ker\Phi = T_x$" needs a precise form: a neighbourhood $N$ of $x$ such that every natural representation inside $N$ has kernel $T_x$, and the intrinsic description $g \in T_x$ iff $s = t$ near $g$. That description turns Definition 7.2.1 (2017: 7.11) into $T_x = \{1_x\}$, proves the effectiveness of $X_R$ in one line, and gives the conjugation $\varphi T_x\varphi^{-1} = T_y$ that the composition of $X_R$ needs and the text does not state (§§VII.15, VII.17).

**The reduced charts do not give Hausdorffness.** The proof of Proposition 7.2.10 (2017: 7.9) builds charts and sc-smooth transitions for $\mathbf X^R$ without any hypothesis; an M-polyfold also has to be Hausdorff and paracompact, and these do not follow from the charts as we read it. The formalization separates what is proved unconditionally from the two hypotheses, and shows them satisfiable (§VII.16).

**A star instead of an induction.** Proposition 7.1.15 (2017: 7.5) becomes a construction transported from one object along chosen morphisms, with nothing shrunk afterwards; the text's inductive adjustment of components, and most of its possible misprints, have no counterpart (§VII.10).

**Properness does more than sequences.** Where the text extracts convergent subsequences, the formalization mostly uses that a proper map is closed or that preimages of compact sets are compact: Proposition 7.1.10 (2017: 7.3), Proposition 7.5.10 (1) (2017: 7.14), the closedness of points behind Lemma 7.3.4 (2017: 7.6), and the closed saturations behind the sc-smoothness in Theorem 7.5.7 (2017: 7.4). With compact sets in place of sequences, Proposition 7.5.10 (2) (2017: 7.14) needs only paracompactness, no regularity (§§VII.5, VII.19, VII.25, VII.28).

**Paracompact without Hausdorff.** Definition 7.5.1 (2017: 7.15) stipulates that paracompact does not include Hausdorff, and Michael's theorem, which the text quotes for regular Hausdorff spaces, holds for regular spaces; one direction holds in every space. This closes gap G.15 of Part I and gives Corollary 7.3.8 (2017: 7.2) its finite and locally finite unions (§§VII.20, VII.21). For Proposition 7.5.3 (2017: 7.12) a shorter route proves more: $|X^1|$ is metrizable (§VII.23).

**Lemma 7.6.5 (2017: 7.10) needs its support bound.** As stated, Lemma 7.6.5 (2017: 7.10) bounds the support of $f$ by $V(x)$; the proof of Theorem 7.5.7 (2017: 7.4) needs the bound $\mathrm{cl}\,U(x)$, which the lemma's own proof gives, for the sum $\sum h_i$ to be locally finite, and it applies the lemma to sets $U''_i$ that need not contain $x_i$. Stating the lemma with the stronger conclusion and the weaker hypothesis makes the proof of Theorem 7.5.7 (2017: 7.4) go through as written, with $W(x_i)$ in the role of $V(x)$ (§§VII.27–VII.28).
