**Four structural choices.** (1) *The linearization is read in charts.* $T_xX$ carries no linear structure in the formalization (gap G.7 of Chapter 2), so $f'(x)$ is the operator $\mathrm{Lin}(z) : T_zO \to K_z$ of a strong bundle chart (Part III), transversality is surjectivity of $\mathrm{Lin}$ in a chart, and general position is stated in a tame chart; its chart independence is proved where it matters (§V.18). (2) *"Generic" is "off a null set".* Every perturbation theorem is proved first in a generic form — for $\lambda$ outside a Lebesgue null set of a small ball, $s_0 + \sum\lambda_it_i$ does the job — and the text's dense-set form is derived from it. (3) *The parametrized solution set is not built as a manifold.* The Sard step is local: at each zero of $F(\lambda, y) = g(y) + \sum\lambda_it_i(y)$ in good position, Theorem 3.6.8 (2) (preprint 3.54) gives a regular tangent germ chart, Sard's theorem is applied on its finite-dimensional model, and finitely many null sets are collected (§V.14). Where Theorem 4.3.1 (preprint 4.23) needs a submanifold, the *bridge lemma* supplies it (§V.17). (4) *Density is pointwise.* "Within $\varepsilon$" is $N(s(x) - s'(x)) \le \varepsilon$ for all $x$; this is $\rho(s, s') \le \varepsilon$ for the metric $\rho(s, s') = \sup_xN(s(x) - s'(x))$, which the perturbation theorems never needed and which was built afterwards (§V.30).

Throughout, $P : Y \to X$ is a strong bundle over an M-polyfold (both paracompact and Hausdorff), a section is a map $s : X \to Y$ with $P \circ s = \mathrm{id}$, $0_x$ is the zero of the fibre over $x$, and $\operatorname{supp}s$ is the closure of $\{s \ne 0\}$ ⟦Chapter5/BumpFunctions · sectionSupport⟧. A strong bundle chart $\Phi$ has base chart $\varphi : V \to O$, $O = r(U)$ a retract, and local bundle $K \to O$; the *principal part* of $f$ is $\hat f(u) = \Phi(f(\varphi^{-1}(u)))_2$, and $\mathrm{Lin}(z)$ is its linearization at a level-$1$ point $z$ (Part III). $|\lambda|$ is the maximum norm on $\mathbb R^m$.

### V.1 The fibre $Y_{0,1}$ and the charts $\Phi[1]$

§5.1 works on the part $Y_{0,1}$ of biregularity $(0, 1)$, the M-polyfold $Y[1]$ of §2.6 (preprint §2.5) (Part II), and assumes two things the project did not have: charts $\Phi[1]$ for *every* strong bundle chart, and a Banach structure on the fibres of $Y_{0,1}$.

**$Y_{0,1}$ in any chart.** For a strong bundle chart $\Phi$ compatible with the atlas — the text's "strong bundle isomorphism $\Phi : Y|V \to K$" — a point $y$ over $\operatorname{dom}\varphi$ lies in $Y_{0,1}$ iff $\Phi(y)_2 \in F_1$: compare with a chart of the atlas through the transition map, which is a strong bundle map and so preserves biregularity ⟦Chapter5/AuxNorm · StrongBundle.mem_oneSet_iff_of_isChart⟧. On $P^{-1}(V) \cap Y_{0,1}$ the map $w \mapsto \Phi(w)$ lifted to $K[1]$ is then a partial homeomorphism ⟦Chapter5/AuxNorm · StrongBundle.oneChartOf⟧, compatible with every chart $\Phi'[1]$ of the atlas because the transition is the $[1]$-transition of Definition 2.6.4 (preprint 2.50) ⟦Chapter5/AuxNorm · StrongBundle.oneChartOf_transition⟧ ⟦Chapter5/AuxNorm · StrongBundle.oneChartOf_compatible⟧, hence a chart of the M-polyfold $Y[1]$ ⟦Chapter5/AuxNorm · StrongBundle.isChart_oneChartOf⟧. For a chart of the atlas it is the chart of Part II ⟦Chapter5/AuxNorm · StrongBundle.oneChart_eq_oneChartOf⟧.

**The fibre.** The fibres $P^{-1}(x)$ carry linear structures; the points of $P^{-1}(x)$ in $Y_{0,1}$ form a linear subspace $(Y_{0,1})_x$, because the charts are linear on the fibres and $F_1 \subseteq F_0$ is a subspace ⟦Chapter5/AuxNorm · StrongBundle.oneFiber⟧. Its points are points of $Y[1]$ and conversely ⟦Chapter5/AuxNorm · StrongBundle.toOne⟧ ⟦Chapter5/AuxNorm · StrongBundle.ofOne⟧; its zero is the point $0_x$ of $Y[1]$ ⟦Chapter5/AuxNorm · StrongBundle.zeroOne⟧ ⟦Chapter5/AuxNorm · StrongBundle.fiberZero_mem_oneSet⟧.

**The chart norm $|h|_1$.** In a chart around $x$, the fibre component of $\Phi[1]$ is a linear map $(Y_{0,1})_x \to F_1$ ⟦Chapter5/AuxNorm · StrongBundle.liftOneFiber⟧, injective because $\Phi$ is injective on the fibre ⟦Chapter5/AuxNorm · StrongBundle.liftOneFiber_injective⟧. Its image is the slice $\{h \in F_1 \mid (\varphi(x), h) \in K[1]\}$ ⟦Chapter5/AuxNorm · StrongBundle.mem_range_liftOneFiber⟧, the fixed point set of the continuous map $h \mapsto R[1](\varphi(x), h)$, hence closed in $F_1$ ⟦Chapter5/AuxNorm · StrongBundle.isClosed_slice⟧. So a sequence of the fibre that is Cauchy for $|\Phi[1](\cdot)_2|_1$ converges: its images converge in $F_1$, the limit stays in the slice, and it is the image of a point of the fibre ⟦Chapter5/AuxNorm · StrongBundle.complete_liftOneFiber⟧. This is the text's "the fibers $Y_x$ have the structure of Banach spaces". The norm depends on the chart, but only up to equivalence, and locally uniformly: by Lemma 5.1.2's (preprint 5.2) upper bound (§V.2) applied to $g = |\Phi_1[1](\cdot)_2|_1$, the norms of two charts are comparable with one constant on the fibres over a neighbourhood of $x$ ⟦Chapter5/AuxNorm · StrongBundle.exists_norm_liftOne_le_mul⟧, and so are finitely many charts and one more ⟦Chapter5/AuxNorm · StrongBundle.exists_uniform_comparison⟧.

### V.2 Auxiliary norms and Lemma 5.1.2 (preprint 5.2)

**Definition 5.1.1 (preprint 5.1).** A function $N : Y[1] \to \mathbb R$ is a *complete norm on the fibre over $x$* if $N(v) = 0$ only for $v = 0$, $N(tv) = |t|N(v)$, $N(v + w) \le N(v) + N(w)$, and every $N$-Cauchy sequence of $(Y_{0,1})_x$ converges ⟦Chapter5/AuxNorm · StrongBundle.IsCompleteFiberNorm⟧. An *auxiliary norm* is a continuous such $N$, complete on every fibre, with condition (2): if $P(w_k) \to x$ and $N(w_k) \to 0$, then $w_k \to 0_x$ in $Y[1]$ ⟦Chapter5/AuxNorm · StrongBundle.IsAuxNorm⟧. It is non-negative ⟦Chapter5/AuxNorm · StrongBundle.IsAuxNorm.nonneg⟧ and homogeneous on the fibres ⟦Chapter5/AuxNorm · StrongBundle.IsAuxNorm.smul⟧.

**Lemma 5.1.2 (preprint 5.2), the upper bound.** Let $g : Y[1] \to \mathbb R$ be continuous at $0_x$ and positively homogeneous on the fibres near $x$, and $\Phi$ a chart around $x$. By continuity at $0_x$ — read through the chart $\Phi[1]$, whose neighbourhoods of $(\varphi(x), 0)$ are the sets $|p - \varphi(x)|_0 + |h|_1 < \varepsilon$ — $g < 1$ where $|p - \varphi(x)|_0 < \varepsilon/2$ and $|h|_1 \le \varepsilon/2$; homogeneity scales every vector of a nearby fibre to $|h|_1 = \varepsilon/2$, which gives $g(w) \le (2/\varepsilon)|h|_1$ on the fibres over a neighbourhood $V$ of $x$ ⟦Chapter5/AuxNorm · StrongBundle.exists_le_mul_norm_liftOne⟧. This is the text's first half of "(i) ⇒ (ii)".

**Lemma 5.1.2 (preprint 5.2), the lower bound.** For an auxiliary norm, $|h|_1 \le A\,N(w)$ near $x$ ⟦Chapter5/AuxNorm · StrongBundle.IsAuxNorm.exists_norm_liftOne_le⟧. Suppose not: then for every $k$ there is $w_k$ over $\varphi^{-1}(B(\varphi(x), 1/(k + 1)))$ with $|h_k|_1 > (k + 1)N(w_k)$; rescale to $|h_k|_1 = 1$. Then $N(w_k) \to 0$ and $P(w_k) \to x$ — the points of the shrinking balls converge to $x$ because $\varphi^{-1}$ is continuous on $O$, so no metrizability of $X$ is needed — hence $w_k \to 0_x$ by condition (2), and $\Phi[1](w_k) \to (\varphi(x), 0)$ by the continuity of $\Phi[1]$, contradicting $|h_k|_1 = 1$. Together: **in every chart around $x$** there are a neighbourhood $V$ and $0 < a < A$ with $aN(w) \le |h|_1 \le AN(w)$ over $V$ ⟦Chapter5/AuxNorm · StrongBundle.IsAuxNorm.exists_bounds⟧.

**Lemma 5.1.2 (preprint 5.2).** For $N$ continuous and a complete norm on every fibre, $N$ is an auxiliary norm iff every $x$ has a chart and such two-sided bounds near $x$ ⟦Chapter5/AuxNorm · lemma_5_2⟧. "(i) ⇒ (ii)" is the previous paragraph. "(ii) ⇒ (i)", "obvious" in the text: if $P(w_k) \to x$ and $N(w_k) \to 0$, the bound gives $|h_k|_1 \to 0$, so $\Phi[1](w_k) \to (\varphi(x), 0)$, and $w_k \to 0_x$ because $\Phi[1]^{-1}$ is continuous on $K[1]$ (§V.1). In the statement $V$ is a neighbourhood of $x$ on which the bounds hold; the text's $V$ is the domain of $\Phi$, but its own proof gives them "for $P(w)$ close to $x$".

### V.3 Proposition 5.1.3 (preprint 5.3)

**Existence.** For the charts $\Phi_i$ of the atlas and a partition of unity $(\rho_i)$ subordinate to their domains — it exists since $X$ is paracompact and Hausdorff, hence normal ⟦Mathlib/Topology/PartitionOfUnity · PartitionOfUnity.exists_isSubordinate⟧ — the *glued norm* is $N(w) = \sum_i\rho_i(P(w))\,|\Phi_i[1](w)_2|_1$ ⟦Chapter5/AuxNorm · StrongBundle.gluedNorm⟧ ⟦Chapter5/AuxNorm · StrongBundle.gluedNorm_eq_sum⟧. It is continuous on $Y[1]$: each term vanishes off the closed set $P^{-1}(\operatorname{supp}\rho_i)$, which lies in the open domain of $\Phi_i[1]$, where the term is continuous, and the sum is locally finite ⟦Chapter5/AuxNorm · StrongBundle.continuous_gluedNorm⟧. Near a point $x$ only finitely many $\rho_i$ are non-zero, all with $x$ in the domain of $\Phi_i$ and summing to $1$ ⟦Chapter5/AuxNorm · StrongBundle.sum_partition_eq_one⟧; their chart norms are uniformly comparable with the norm of any one chart $\Phi'$ around $x$ (§V.1), so $N$ is two-sided comparable with $|\Phi'[1](\cdot)_2|_1$ near $x$ ⟦Chapter5/AuxNorm · StrongBundle.gluedNorm_bounds⟧. On each fibre $N$ is a convex combination of norms, hence a norm, and comparable with a complete norm, hence complete ⟦Chapter5/AuxNorm · StrongBundle.complete_of_bounds⟧ ⟦Chapter5/AuxNorm · StrongBundle.isCompleteFiberNorm_gluedNorm⟧. Lemma 5.1.2 (preprint 5.2) "(ii) ⇒ (i)" concludes ⟦Chapter5/AuxNorm · proposition_5_3_exists⟧. The text gives this as one sentence.

**Comparison.** For auxiliary norms $N_0$, $N_1$, Lemma 5.1.2 (preprint 5.2) "(i) ⇒ (ii)" for both in the *same* chart around $y$ — which is why "(i) ⇒ (ii)" was proved in every chart — gives $r_y > 0$ with $r_yN_0 \le N_1$ and $r_yN_1 \le N_0$ over a neighbourhood of $y$. At each point the admissible constants $r$ form a convex set ($r$ admissible and $0 < r' \le r$ give $r'$ admissible), so a partition of unity glues the local constants into a continuous $f > 0$ with values in these sets ⟦Mathlib/Analysis/Convex/PartitionOfUnity · exists_continuous_forall_mem_convex_of_local_const⟧, and $f(x)N_0 \le N_1 \le f(x)^{-1}N_0$ on the fibre over $x$ ⟦Chapter5/AuxNorm · proposition_5_3_compare⟧. Tameness plays no role in §5.1.

### V.4 Definition 5.2.1 (preprint 5.4) and proper sections

"$N(y) \le r$" for a point $y$ of $Y$ is read with the text's convention $N(y) = \infty$ off $Y_{0,1}$: it means $y \in Y_{0,1}$ and $N(y) \le r$ ⟦Chapter5/Compactness · StrongBundle.AuxLe⟧, a property of the point only ⟦Chapter5/Compactness · StrongBundle.AuxLe.congr⟧. A section $f$ is *weakly proper* if $f^{-1}(0)$ is compact and for every auxiliary norm $N$ there is an open $U \supseteq f^{-1}(0)$ such that $\{f = s\}$ is compact for every sc⁺-section $s$ with support in $U$ and $N(s) \le 1$ ⟦Chapter5/Compactness · StrongBundle.IsWeaklyProper⟧; *proper* if instead $\operatorname{cl}_X\{x \in U \mid N(f(x)) \le 1\}$ is compact ⟦Chapter5/Compactness · StrongBundle.IsProper⟧.

*Proper implies weakly proper* for a continuous $f$ ⟦Chapter5/Compactness · StrongBundle.IsProper.isWeaklyProper⟧. Take the $U$ of properness. A solution of $f(x) = s(x)$ outside $U$ has $s(x) = 0$, so $f(x) = 0$ and $x \in f^{-1}(0) \subseteq U$ after all; inside $U$, $f(x) = s(x)$ gives $N(f(x)) = N(s(x)) \le 1$. So $\{f = s\}$ lies in the compact closure, and it is closed because $f$ and $s$ are continuous and $Y$ is Hausdorff. Weakly proper sections have compact solution sets by definition ⟦Chapter5/Compactness · StrongBundle.IsWeaklyProper.isCompact_solutionSet⟧. The text's "obviously" hides the continuity of $f$.

### V.5 Theorem 5.2.2 (preprint 5.5): the local form for basic germs

Theorem 5.2.2 (preprint 5.5) says that for sc-Fredholm sections a compact solution set already forces properness. The appendix proves it in the coordinates of a basic germ, and that local statement is the heart of the matter.

**The setting.** A basic germ (Definition 3.1.7 (preprint 3.7), Part III) is $h : \tilde C \oplus W \supseteq U_0 \to \mathbb R^N \oplus W$ whose $W$-component is $w - B(a, w)$, where $B$ is an sc⁰-contraction germ: on small balls, $w \mapsto B(a, w)$ contracts with constant $\le 1/2$ on level $0$, uniformly in $a$, and $B(0, 0) = 0$. Part III built the *parametrized fixed point* $\delta(a, z)$, the unique solution $w$ in a closed ball $\bar B(r)$ of $w = B(a, w) - z$ for $(a, z)$ in a set $\Sigma$ of small parameters, continuous in $(a, z)$ on level $0$ ⟦Chapter3/LocalCompactness · IsSc0ContractionGerm.delta_continuousOn⟧ ⟦Chapter5/Compactness · IsSc0ContractionGerm.eq_delta_zero⟧.

**The local form** ⟦Chapter5/Compactness · IsSc0ContractionGerm.exists_isCompact_bdd⟧. Assume $\tilde C$ closed. There is an open $O \ni 0$, *independent of $c$*, such that for every $c$ the points $x = (a, w) \in U_0 \cap O$ with $w - B(a, w) \in W_1$ and $|w - B(a, w)|_1 \le c$ lie in a compact set. The proof:

1. *A small ball.* $B(a, w) - w$ is continuous at $0$ within $U_0$ and vanishes there, so for $|(a, w)|_0 < \rho_1$ it has norm $< r/8$. Let $\rho = \min(\rho_1, \rho'/2, r/2)$, with $\rho'$ the radius of the parameter set, and $O = B(0, \rho)$.
2. *The compact set of parameters.* The inclusion $W_1 \to W_0$ is compact, so the image of the closed level-$1$ ball of radius $c$ lies in a compact $K_c \subseteq W_0$. The set $L = (\tilde C \cap \bar B(\rho)) \times (K_c \cap \bar B(r/8))$ is compact (here $\tilde C$ closed is used) and lies in $\Sigma$.
3. *The candidate.* The compact set is the image of $L$ under the continuous map $(a, z) \mapsto (a, \delta(a, z))$.
4. *Every bounded point is hit.* Let $x = (a, w) \in U_0 \cap O$ with $w - B(a, w) = \iota(z_1)$, $|z_1|_1 \le c$. Put $p = (a, B(a, w) - w)$. Then $a \in \tilde C$ with $|a| < \rho$; $B(a, w) - w = \iota(-z_1)$ lies in $K_c$ and has norm $< r/8$ by step 1; so $p \in L$. And $w$ lies in the ball $\bar B(r)$ and solves $w = B(a, w) - (B(a, w) - w)$, so $w = \delta(p)$ by uniqueness ⟦Chapter5/Compactness · IsSc0ContractionGerm.eq_delta_zero⟧, i.e. $x$ is the image of $p$.

Only the level-$0$ contraction is used. For a basic germ $f$ itself the $W$-component of $f(x)$ is $w - B(a, w)$, so the same $O$ works with the level-$1$ bound $|f(x)|_1 \le c$ ⟦Chapter5/Compactness · IsBasicGerm.exists_isCompact_bdd⟧. This is the appendix's (82)–(90), where the text writes "$a \in \mathbb R^n$" for $a \in \tilde C$ ($B$ is defined on $\tilde C \oplus W$ only), "$\mathbb R^n \oplus Y_1$" for $\mathbb R^N \oplus W_1$, and in (89) mixes the $\mathbb R^N$- and $W$-components; the $t$ of its "$h + t$" and its estimates (83), (85) disappear in the next step.

### V.6 Theorem 5.2.2 (preprint 5.5): the local step and the covering

**Uniform fibre bounds.** A strong bundle map $\Psi$ (Definition 2.6.1 (preprint 2.47)) is linear on the fibres and $\Psi[1]$ is continuous; at $(u_0, 0)$, where $\Psi[1](u_0, 0)_2 = 0$ ⟦Chapter5/Compactness · IsStrongBundleMap.shiftFiber_snd_zero⟧, continuity gives $|\Psi[1](u, k)_2|_1 \le 1$ for $|u - u_0|_0 < \delta$, $|k|_1 \le \eta$, and fibre linearity ⟦Chapter5/Compactness · IsStrongBundleMap.shiftFiber_snd_smul⟧ turns this into $|\Psi[1](u, k)_2|_1 \le \eta^{-1}|k|_1$ for all $k \in F_1$ ⟦Chapter5/Compactness · IsStrongBundleMap.exists_fiber_bound_one⟧. The text uses this bound silently when it replaces $N(f(x)) \le 1$ by a bound in basic germ coordinates.

**The local step** ⟦Chapter5/Compactness · exists_nbhd_isCompact_closure_bdd⟧. Let $(f, x_0)$ be an sc-Fredholm germ (Definition 3.1.11 (preprint 3.11)) — $x_0$ need not be a zero. Its data, in the text's germ reading (§III.6): a strong bundle chart $\Phi$ centred at $x_0$, a filling germ $g$ of the principal part $\hat f$ on a germ inside the domain $U$ of the retraction, an sc⁺-germ $t_0$ on a germ $G_s$, and a strong bundle isomorphism $\Psi$ with base map $\psi$ conjugating $g - t_0$ to a basic germ $h$: $h(\psi(u)) = \Psi(u, g(u) - t_0(u))_2$ for $u \in U$ near $0$. The claim is an open $V \ni x_0$, independent of $C$, such that for every $C$ the closure in $X$ of $Z_C = \{x \in V \mid |\Phi(f(x))_2|_1 \le C\}$ is compact.

1. *The neighbourhood.* Near $0$ the filler agrees with the principal part on $O$ and the conjugation identity holds (a set $N_1$); $t_0$ is continuous into $F_1$ on level $0$ of its germ, $(G_s)_0 = O_s \cap C$ with $O_s$ open — not on $U$ — so the neighbourhood is also cut with $O_s$, and $|t_0(u)|_1 \le |t_0(0)|_1 + 1$ for $u \in U \cap O_s$, $|u|_0 < \delta_2$ (with $t_0$ sc-smooth on $U$ the cut was not needed); the fibre bound of $\Psi$ holds for $|u|_0 < \delta_3$ with constant $M$. On the side of the basic germ, the local form (§V.5) gives an open $O'$; $\psi$ maps a neighbourhood of $0$ into a closed ball $\bar B(r_0) \cap C'$ inside the domain $U'$ of $\psi^{-1}$ (relative openness of $U'$). $V$ is the set of points of the chart domain whose image lies in all of these.
2. *The bound transported.* For $x \in Z_C$, $u = \varphi(x) \in O$, $g(u) = \hat f(u)$ lies in $F_1$ with $|g(u)|_1 \le C$, so $|g(u) - t_0(u)|_1 \le C + |t_0(0)|_1 + 1$, and $|h(\psi(u))|_1 = |\Psi[1](u, g(u) - t_0(u))_2|_1 \le M(C + |t_0(0)|_1 + 1) =: c'$. *Subtracting the sc⁺-part before the conjugation* is what makes the text's $t$ and its estimates unnecessary, and the neighbourhood independent of $C$.
3. *Compactness on the side of the basic germ.* So $\psi(\varphi(Z_C))$ lies in the compact set $K_{c'}$ of the local form, intersected with the closed ball $\bar B(r_0) \cap C'$; its closure $A$ is compact and inside $U'$.
4. *Back to $U$.* $\psi^{-1}$ is continuous on $U'$, so $T = \psi^{-1}(A)$ is compact, contains $\varphi(Z_C)$ and lies in $U$.
5. *Back to $O$.* The closure of $\varphi(Z_C)$ lies in $T \subseteq U$ and in the closure of $O$; since $O$ is the fixed point set of the continuous retraction $r$ on $U$, it is closed in $U$, so the closure lies in $O$.
6. *Back to $X$.* $\varphi^{-1}$ is continuous on $O$, so $\varphi^{-1}$ of that closure is compact and contains $Z_C$, hence its closure.

The text's "it suffices to find in local coordinates an open neighborhood" leaves steps 4–6 implicit: its set $\{(a, w) \in U \mid |(h + t)(a, w)|_1 \le c\}$ lives in the chart domain $U$ of the filling, which contains points not in the retract.

### V.9 The calculus of sections, and Lemma 5.3.3 (preprint 5.11)

**$\Gamma^+(P)$ and its algebra.** $\Gamma^+(P)$ is the set of sc⁺-sections (Definition 2.6.6 (preprint 2.52)) ⟦Chapter5/BumpFunctions · StrongBundle.scPlusSections⟧; their values lie in $Y_{0,1}$, because in a chart their principal parts take values in $F_1$ ⟦Chapter5/BumpFunctions · StrongBundle.IsScPlusSection.mem_oneSet⟧. ### V.12 Spanning sections

"For every $x \in S_{f+s_0}$ we find finitely many allowable sc⁺-sections such that the range of $(f + s_0)'(x)$ together with the sections span $Y_x$": the text does not say how. Two ingredients.

**Linear algebra** ⟦Chapter5/Transversality · exists_fin_mem_of_dense_of_closedComplemented⟧. Let $R \subseteq K$ be closed, complemented and of finite codimension, and $D \subseteq K$ a dense subspace. With $\pi$ a continuous projection onto $R$, $Q = \ker\pi$ is finite-dimensional, and $(1 - \pi)(D)$ is a dense subspace of $Q$, hence closed, hence all of $Q$; a basis of $Q$ lifts to finitely many $d_i \in D$, and $K = R + \operatorname{span}\{d_i\}$.

**The sections** ⟦Chapter5/Transversality · exists_spanning_sections⟧. Let $g$ be sc-Fredholm, $g(y) = 0$, $U \ni y$ open. In the chart $\Phi$ of the germ, centred at $y$, $\mathrm{Lin}(0) : T_0O \to K_0$ is $Dg(0)|_{T_0O}$ for a derivative family $Dg$ of the filling germ on its germ (the bridge for germs at the centre, ⟦Chapter3/LinearizationChartsG · IsFillingGerm.fillerRestrict_eq_linAt⟧), hence Fredholm (Proposition 3.6.5 (preprint 3.52) for germs, which exposes the germ and $Dg$) ⟦Chapter3/FredholmGermLinearizationG · proposition_3_52⟧, so its range $R$ is closed, complemented and of finite codimension in $K_0 = \rho(0)F$. The smooth vectors $\rho(0)F_\infty$ are dense in $K_0$ — $\rho(0)$ is continuous and preserves the levels at the smooth point $0$, and $F_\infty$ is dense — so finitely many smooth $k_i$ span a complement. ### V.14 Theorem 5.3.5 (preprint 5.13): the Sard step

"$\tilde S := F^{-1}(0)$ is a smooth manifold"; "taking a regular value $\lambda_0$ for the projection $\tilde S \to \mathbb R^m$, it is easily verified that $f + s_0 + s(\lambda_0, \cdot)$ is transversal". The formalization does not build $\tilde S$; it proves the needed statement near each zero ⟦Chapter5/Transversality · exists_null_isLinSurjAt_paramSlice_near⟧.

*Claim.* Let $g$ have tame germ data, $t_i$ be sc⁺, and $z_0$ a zero of $F$ in good position in a tame chart. There are an open $W \ni z_0$ and a null set $CV \subseteq \mathbb R^m$ such that for every zero $(\lambda, y) \in W$ with $\lambda \notin CV$ the slice $g_\lambda$ is transversal at $y$.

1. *The chart of $\tilde S$.* Theorem 3.6.8 (2) (preprint 3.54) gives at $z_0$ a regular tangent germ chart $D$ of the solution set of $F$ (Definition 3.2.1 (2) (preprint 3.25)): a chart $D.\varphi$ of $\mathbb R^m \times X$ with domain $W$, a finite-dimensional $N$, a relatively open $V \subseteq N \cap C$ and an sc-smooth graph map $\Gamma$ with $D.\varphi^{-1} \circ \Gamma$ parametrizing $\tilde S \cap W$ ⟦Chapter3/GlobalIFTTheoremsG · theorem_3_54_two⟧.
2. *The projection.* $q = p \circ D.\varphi^{-1} \circ \Gamma : V \to \mathbb R^m$, $p$ the projection to the parameter, is sc-smooth on a finite-dimensional space with the constant structure, hence $C^\infty$ ⟦Chapter1/ExpositionLemmas · isScSmooth_iff_contDiffOn_const⟧.
3. *Sard on the strata.* $V$ is relatively open in a partial quadrant of $N$; Sard's theorem on each stratum of the quadrant (Part IV) makes the set $CV$ of stratified critical values of $q$ null ⟦Chapter4/FamiliesLocal · measure_image_stratCritSet⟧. Since $\partial X = \emptyset$ the points over the zeros are interior and classical Sard would do, but proving that needs the degeneracy index of the induced structure; the stratified form makes the question unnecessary.
4. *The point.* A zero $(\lambda, y) \in W$ is $D.\varphi^{-1}(\Gamma(v))$ for some $v \in V$ with $q(v) = \lambda$. Let $\Phi$ be a chart centred at $y$ and $\psi = (r \mapsto r - \lambda) \times \varphi$ the product chart. On a relatively open $V' \subseteq V$ around $v$, $\mathrm{gmap} = \psi \circ D.\varphi^{-1} \circ \Gamma$ is sc-smooth (the transition between the two charts of $\mathbb R^m \times X$ restricted to the overlap), and $q = \mathrm{pr}_1 \circ \mathrm{gmap} + \lambda$ there.
5. *Kernel vectors.* If $\lambda \notin CV$, $Dq(v)$ maps the stratum through $v$ onto $\mathbb R^m$. The values of $D\,\mathrm{gmap}(v)$ lie in the tangent space $T_0O$ of the product chart, and in $\ker\mathrm{Lin}_F(0)$: the principal part of $F$ vanishes along $r \circ \mathrm{gmap}$, and the chain rule gives $\mathrm{Lin}_F(0) \circ D\,\mathrm{gmap}(v) = 0$. So for every $a \in \mathbb R^m$ there is a kernel vector with first component $a$.
6. *Conclusion.* $F$ is transversal at $(\lambda, y)$ (good position near $z_0$), and §V.11 gives the transversality of the slice at $y$.

Only $\operatorname{range}D\,\mathrm{gmap}(v) \subseteq \ker\mathrm{Lin}_F$ is used, not "$T\tilde S = \ker F'$".

### V.17 The bridge lemma, and $T_yS = \ker f'(y)$

Theorem 5.3.10 (preprint 5.18) applies Theorem 4.3.1 (preprint 4.23) to the parametrized solution set $\tilde S$, which needs $\tilde S$ to be a smooth submanifold in the sense of Definition 4.2.1 (preprint 4.13) — locally the image of an sc⁺-retraction. The implicit function theorem gives tangent germ data (Definition 3.2.1 (preprint 3.25)), and the text never connects the two.

**sc⁺ on a finite-dimensional domain** ⟦Chapter5/GeneralPosition · isScPlus_of_isScSmooth_const⟧. On a finite-dimensional space with the constant structure all levels of the domain coincide, so an sc-smooth $f$ is $C^\infty$ into every $F_m$, and its lift into $F^1$ is sc-smooth: $f$ is sc⁺.

**The bridge lemma.** Let $D$ be regular tangent germ data for $M$ at $x$ (Definition 3.2.1 (2) (preprint 3.25)), with graph map $\Gamma : V \to E$ over the finite-dimensional $N$, projection $\pi$ onto $N$, and $r$ a retraction onto the chart model. Then $\Gamma$ is sc⁺ on $V$ ⟦Chapter5/GeneralPosition · MPolyfold.TangentGermChart.Γ_isScPlus⟧, so $\tau = \Gamma \circ \pi \circ r$, on the set of points whose image under $\pi \circ r$ lies in $V$, is an sc-smooth retraction (as in the proof of Theorem 3.2.3 (preprint 3.27)) ⟦Chapter3/TangentGerm · theorem_3_27⟧ which is sc⁺ (sc⁺ ∘ sc-smooth), with $\tau \circ \Gamma = \Gamma$ on $V$ and retract the chart image of $M$ ⟦Chapter5/GeneralPosition · MPolyfold.TangentGermChart.exists_scPlusRetraction⟧. Transported to $X$ it is an sc⁺-retraction on an open set with image $M \cap V$ ⟦Chapter4/LocalStructure · MPolyfold.isScPlusRetractionOn_of_chart⟧ ⟦Chapter5/GeneralPosition · MPolyfold.TangentGermChart.exists_isScPlusRetractionOn⟧. Hence a set with regular tangent germ data at each point is a smooth finite-dimensional submanifold ⟦Chapter5/GeneralPosition · MPolyfold.isSmoothSubmanifold_of_isRegular⟧. For solution sets: if all zeros of $f$ in an open $W$ are in good position in a tame chart, Theorem 3.6.8 (2) (preprint 3.54) gives regular data at each, on charts inside $W$, and $S_f \cap W$ is a smooth submanifold ⟦Chapter5/GeneralPosition · StrongBundle.isSmoothSubmanifold_solutionSet_inter⟧.

**$T_yS \subseteq \ker f'(y)$** ⟦Chapter5/GeneralPosition · tangentSpace_subset_linKer⟧. In a chart, let $Q \subseteq O$ be a retract on which the principal part $\hat f$ vanishes, $S$ a retraction onto $Q$, $z$ a level-$1$ point of $Q$. Then $T_zQ = \operatorname{range}DS(z) \subseteq T_zO$, and $\hat f \circ r \circ S = 0$ gives $D(\hat f \circ r)(z) \circ DS(z) = 0$ by the chain rule, i.e. $T_zQ \subseteq \ker\mathrm{Lin}(z)$ ⟦Chapter5/GeneralPosition · StrongBundleChart.mem_linKer_iff⟧.

**Equality, by counting** ⟦Chapter5/GeneralPosition · exists_tangentSpace_eq_linKer⟧. Let $f$ have tame germ data and $(f, x_0)$ be a zero in good position in a tame chart. The index form of Theorem 3.6.8 (preprint 3.54) gives an open $V \ni x_0$ whose zeros are in good position, and regular data $D$ of a fixed dimension $d$ at each zero, with $\dim\ker\mathrm{Lin}(z) = d$ in *every* chart ⟦Chapter3/GlobalIFTIndexG · theorem_3_54_index⟧ ⟦Chapter3/GlobalIFTIndex · StrongBundle.IsSurjLinOfIndex⟧. On the other side, the tangent space of the bridge retraction $\tau$ at the point over $\Gamma(v)$ contains the $d$ vectors $D\Gamma(v)b_i$, $b_i$ a basis of $N$: $D\tau \circ D\Gamma(v) = D(\tau \circ \Gamma)(v) = D\Gamma(v)$, and $\pi \circ D\Gamma(v) = 1$ makes them independent ⟦Chapter5/GeneralPosition · MPolyfold.TangentGermChart.exists_linearIndependent_tangentSpace⟧. "Having $d$ independent tangent vectors" ⟦Chapter5/GeneralPosition · HasIndepTangent⟧ is carried by germs of sc-diffeomorphisms, since $D(F \circ r)(z)$ maps $T_zQ$ into $T_{F(z)}Q'$ and is injective on $T_zO$ ⟦Chapter5/GeneralPosition · HasIndepTangent.transport⟧, so it passes from the chart of $D$ to any chart $\Phi$ through the transition maps ⟦Chapter4/Submanifolds · MPolyfold.chart_change_of_transport⟧. ### V.28 What the formalization taught us

**The tameness hypothesis.** The first formal versions of Theorem 5.3.5 (preprint 5.13) and Lemma 5.26 (preprint) assumed, as the §3.6 theorems then did, that the base retraction of *every* compatible strong bundle chart — and of every chart of the pull-backs to $\mathbb R^k \times X$ — is tame. No strong bundle over a nonempty base satisfies this (a compatible chart $(1, \varphi)$ with base retraction $(a, u) \mapsto (1, r(u))$ on $[0, \infty) \times U$ is never tame), so those statements were vacuous. The repair is the text's own Definitions 3.6.3, 3.6.4, 3.6.6 (preprint 3.50, 3.51, 3.53) in a tame chart: the perturbation theorems take $f$ with tame germ data, the parametrized sections inherit them through the tame product chart, and Definition 5.3.9 (preprint 5.17) lives in a tame chart, where Lemma 5.26's (preprint) count $\dim T_zO/T^R_zO = d_C(z)$ holds.

**What "it is easily verified" costs.** The perturbation theorems of §5.3 are sketches. Their formal proofs needed the density of smooth vectors (§V.12), the compactness of the zeros over a small parameter ball (§V.13), a local, stratified Sard step (§V.14), the bridge from tangent germ data to Definition 4.2.1 (preprint 4.13) (§V.17), $T_yS = \ker f'(y)$ by a dimension count, which gives the chart independence of Definition 5.3.9 (preprint 5.17) and the openness of general position (§§V.17–V.18), and, in Theorem 5.3.12 (preprint 5.19), a route around the implicit function theorem at boundary points where its hypothesis fails (§V.22).

### V.31 Weak and pointwise bump functions

§V.8 reads Definition 5.3.1 (preprint 5.9) pointwise, $f(x) \ne 0$, because Proposition 5.5.3 (preprint 5.30) needs it, and keeps Definition 5.5.2's (preprint 5.29) "not identically zero" as the weak reading. How far apart are they? (Findings, Chapter 5, items 160–163.)

**Bump points.** $x$ is a *bump point* if every open $U \ni x$ carries an sc-smooth $f$ with $f(x) \ne 0$ and $\operatorname{supp} f \subseteq U$ ⟦Chapter5/BumpWeakPointwise · MPolyfold.HasBumpAt⟧. (The converse, weak ⟹ dense bump points, is a nested-neighbourhoods argument with Cantor's theorem in a chart; not formalized.)

**On an sc-Banach space.** An sc-Banach space $E$ is an M-polyfold with the identity chart and model $(E, E, E)$ ⟦Chapter5/BumpWeakPointwise · scChart⟧ ⟦Chapter5/BumpWeakPointwise · scPolyfold⟧, whose sc-smooth functions are the sc-smooth maps $E \to \mathbb R$ ⟦Chapter5/BumpWeakPointwise · isScSmoothMap_scPolyfold_iff⟧. *There the weak reading gives a bump point at every smooth point* ⟦Chapter5/BumpWeakPointwise · MPolyfold.AdmitsBumpFunctionsWeak.hasBumpAt_of_mem_smooth⟧: given $x \in E_\infty$ and $B(x, \varepsilon) \subseteq U$, a weak bump supported in $B(x, \varepsilon/2)$ is non-zero on a non-empty open set, which contains a smooth point $y$ because $E_\infty$ is dense; $a = y - x$ is smooth, so $v \mapsto f(v + a)$ is sc-smooth ⟦Chapter4/Recentre · isScSmooth_add_const⟧, non-zero at $x$, with support in $B(x, \varepsilon) \subseteq U$. The argument stops at $E_\infty$: translation by a vector $a \notin E_m$ is not even sc⁰, since it sends $0 \in E_m$ out of $E_m$ ⟦Chapter5/BumpWeakPointwise · not_isSc0_add_const⟧. On M-polyfolds built on sc-Hilbert spaces both readings hold at every point ⟦Chapter5/BumpFunctions · MPolyfold.admitsBumpFunctions_of_hilbert⟧. *Open*: whether the weak reading gives a bump function non-zero at a non-smooth point, on an sc-Banach space or an M-polyfold; a counterexample would need an $E_0$ without $C^1$ bump functions.
