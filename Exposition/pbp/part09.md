## Part IX. Branched ep⁺-subgroupoids (HWZ Chapter 9, §§9.1–9.5 and §9.6.1 (2017 §§9.1–9.5))

Chapter 9 continues Part II; its numbers and pages are those of the book (Springer 2021, pp. 401–458), with the 2017 text's in parentheses (2017 pp. 291–332). It introduces the objects that will carry the solution sets of perturbed sc-Fredholm sections with symmetries: functors $\Theta : X \to \mathbb Q^+$ on an ep-groupoid that are, near every point of their support, finite weighted sums of indicator functions of properly embedded smooth finite-dimensional submanifolds. §9.1 gives the definition (Definition 9.1.2 (2017: 9.1)), the local closedness of the orbit support (Lemma 9.1.5 (2017: 9.1)), symmetric local branching structures (Lemma 9.1.7 (2017: 9.2)) and the dimensional decomposition (Propositions 9.1.11 (2017: 9.2) and 9.1.12, the latter new in 2021 with its proof in Appendix §9.6.1). §9.2 attaches to $\Theta$ a section $T_\Theta$ of formal sums of tangent spaces (Theorem 9.2.1 (2017: 9.1)), proved through good and bad points and a graph representation of the sheets near a bad point (Proposition 9.2.8, Lemmas 9.2.9–9.2.11 (2017: 9.3, 9.6–9.8)); it defines the tangent $T\Theta$ (Definition 9.2.12 (2017: 9.6)) and, through the position of a submanifold relative to $\partial X$ (Lemma 9.2.13 (2017: 9.9)), the boundary $\partial\Theta$ (Proposition 9.2.15, Definition 9.2.16 (2017: 9.4, 9.7)). §9.3 orients $\Theta$ (Definition 9.3.2 (2017: 9.8)), compares orientations at good points (Lemma 9.3.4 (2017: 9.10)), gives an example (Example 9.3.5 (2017: 9.1)) and the boundary orientation (Definitions 9.3.6–9.3.10). §9.4 studies essential points and sets of measure zero of the sheets and decomposes an oriented structure (Propositions 9.4.9, 9.4.10 (2017: 9.5, 9.6)). §9.5 integrates sc-differential forms and proves Stokes' theorem: the sets of measure zero in $|\mathrm{supp}(\Theta)|$ and the σ-algebras $L(S)$, $L(\partial S)$ (Definitions 9.5.1, 9.5.3, 9.5.14, Lemma 9.5.2 (2017: Definitions 9.15–9.17, Lemma 9.16)), the branched integrals (Theorems 9.5.4, 9.5.5 (2017: 9.2, 9.3), Definition 9.5.6, Lemmas 9.5.7, 9.5.9–9.5.11 (2017: 9.17–9.19), Proposition 9.5.13), the boundary measures (Theorems 9.5.15, 9.5.16 (2017: 9.4, 9.5)) and Stokes' theorem (Theorems 9.5.17, 9.5.18 (2017: 9.6, 9.7)). Every point of §§9.1–9.4 is settled and §9.5 is formalized with the text's hypotheses, except part of Remark 9.5.20; §§IX.26–IX.39 hold the later material.

**What is proved, and what is not.** All definitions of §§9.1–9.5 and §9.6.1 are formalized. **Seven structural choices.** (1) *Tangent sets, linear algebra in charts.* When this part was first written, Part I's $T_xX$ carried no linear structure (point G.7, since closed). The tangent space $T_yM$ of a submanifold is the set of tangent vectors fixed by the tangent of the retraction that presents $M$, and $\mathrm{Gr}(x)$ is generated by such sets; in a chart the image of $T_yM$ is a linear subspace of the right dimension, and every argument that needs linear algebra is carried out there (§IX.6). (2) *Functors as functions.* $\Theta$ is a function on objects with $\Theta(s(\varphi)) = \Theta(t(\varphi))$, valued in $\mathbb Q$, nonnegativity a field. (3) *Dimension labellings.* The splitting by dimension is done with labellings $\mathrm{dim} : I \to \mathbb N$, unique when they exist (§IX.1); since the dimension is locally constant on every M-polyfold, a labelled structure exists at every point of the support (§IX.27). (4) *Slices instead of sequences.* Proposition 9.2.8 is used in the text to count the weights of a class of tangent sheets by the values of $\Theta$ at good points; the formalization counts them on one finite slice transverse to the class (§IX.9). The same device proves Proposition 9.6.5 (§IX.22). (5) *Named hypotheses, proved as theorems.* The facts of the text's §9.2 that need level-0 sc-calculus inside a partial quadrant are named hypotheses (`HasGraphChartAt`, `HasLocallyConstantDimAt`, `HasDomainInvarianceAt`, `HasContinuousLevelOneOn`), proved on $\mathbb R^m$, and theorems on every M-polyfold, with the graph representation in the weaker form of one cone per tangent space, which is all the slice arguments use (§IX.27). The statements under the hypotheses are kept. (6) *Sheet geometry and null sets as structures.* §9.4 is formalized for Mathlib manifolds with corners and, through the structure `SheetGeometry`, for sheets in an M-polyfold (§IX.17); the null sets of §9.5 are a structure `SheetNullSets` (§IX.19). Both are instantiated on every M-polyfold by the $n$-dimensional Hausdorff measure of the chart images (§IX.28). (7) *Integration as a structure.* The integral of a form over a measurable part of an oriented sheet enters §9.5 through a structure `SheetIntegration` with the properties the text uses (linearity, countable additivity, vanishing on null sets, the comparison of two sheets, invariance under morphisms); Theorems 9.5.4 and 9.5.5 and the lemmas before them are proved for every such structure (§IX.31), and the structure is then built for sc-differential forms by integrating in graph charts (§IX.32).

**The dimension** (V9.2). Remark 9.1.1 says that $m \mapsto \dim T_mM$ is locally constant. ### IX.25 What the formalization taught us

The definition of a branched ep⁺-subgroupoid is local and says nothing about how the local pictures fit together, and three statements that read as uniqueness claims, Proposition 9.1.11, Definition 9.2.16 and Lemma 9.3.4, turned out to depend on exactly that: an indicator can be split into a point and a punctured line, a characterization asked only on its own support is met by zero, and a lower-dimensional sheet through a bad point escapes the good points. In each case the repair is a condition that ties the object to the local branching structures of $\Theta$ (local decompositions, the largest support, equidimensionality). The analytic heart of §9.2, the graph representation of tangent sheets near a bad point, is a statement about one chart in which sheets of all dimensions are graphs; separated from the counting it serves, it became a single hypothesis, true on $\mathbb R^m$ by the inverse function theorem, from which Proposition 9.1.12, Theorem 9.2.1, Lemma 9.6.4 and Proposition 9.6.5 follow. Two steps the text leaves to "similarly" (Lemma 9.6.4) or asserts (the independence on p. 457) needed that both structures be compared on one common slice. Finally, "measure zero" in §9.6.1 is not needed: the arguments only use that a piece of a $k$-dimensional submanifold is not covered by finitely many thinner ones, which is topology. In §9.5 the opposite happens: the measure-theoretic part needs only that the null sets of the sheets form a local σ-ideal compatible on overlaps, and that compatibility is exactly where dimensions must agree; and of the isotropy computations that normalize the integrals, everything except the integrals themselves is finite group theory.

*The level-0 hypotheses are one fact.* Every level-0 statement of §9.2 (the cone, the graphs, invariance of domain, the local constancy of rank, the level-1 continuity of frames) follows from one observation: the lift $1_0^1 \circ r$ of an sc⁺-retraction to level $1$ is $C^1$ on the retract, so that Mathlib's inverse function theorem applies on level $1$ and the conclusions descend through the inclusion. One cone for all submanifolds through a boundary point would be more than the text uses; one cone per tangent space is enough, and holds everywhere (§IX.27). *A tacit finiteness in §9.5.* The text integrates $\omega$ over measurable subsets of the sheets, which presupposes $\int|\omega| < \infty$. For the sheets themselves this follows from compactness and the properness of $\mathrm{cl}\,U(x)$ (§IX.32); for the boundary sheets $\partial_1 M_i$ it is a hypothesis of the first forms of Theorems 9.5.15–9.5.18 (§§IX.34–IX.35), and it follows from the continuity of Part I's chart forms up to the corners and, toward the frontier of $U(x)$, from a comparison of tangent spaces with other structures, in which $\{d = 1\}$ depends on the tangent space alone (§§IX.36–IX.37). The same proof needs facts the text does not state: that the sheets are Lindelöf, so that countably many neighbourhoods suffice in Theorems 9.5.4, 9.5.5; that $M_i \cap \mathrm{supp}\,\beta_k$ is compact in the proof of Stokes' theorem; that chart changes are differentiable along a finite-dimensional submanifold for the level-0 norms, which makes Definitions 9.4.1 and 9.4.5 chart independent; and, for Proposition 9.5.13 (2), a natural action of $G^R_x$ built from that of $G_x$ under the local unique continuation property. *Some hypotheses are not used.* Proposition 9.1.9 does not use the paracompactness of $|X|$, the continuity of $o_{\partial M}$ needs no tameness, only reduced codimension $1$, and the effective clause of Lemma 9.5.11 needs no local unique continuation. *Examples are more rigid than they look.* In Example 9.3.5 every one-dimensional submanifold inside the support through the origin has tangent $\mathbb R(1,0)$ and meets every vertical line, and this rigidity determines all orientations: a three-parameter family, of which six have integer weights (§IX.29). *Paracompactness through metrizability.* In Remark 9.1.4 the step from a paracompact neighbourhood to a component compact set goes through metrizability, since a subspace of a paracompact space need not be paracompact (§IX.26).

*Remark 9.5.20 needs infinite dimensions.* In finite dimensions a finite group that fixes a boundary point and acts trivially on the face acts trivially on the inward normal as well, so the effective isotropy of a boundary point is the same read in $X$ and in $\partial X$. The remark's example has to be an M-polyfold whose local dimension jumps at its boundary, $0 \to 3 \to 0$ (§IX.39).
