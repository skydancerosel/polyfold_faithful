## Part IX. Branched ep⁺-subgroupoids (HWZ Chapter 9, §§9.1–9.5 and §9.6.1 (2017 §§9.1–9.5))

Chapter 9 continues Part II; its numbers and pages are those of the book (Springer 2021, pp. 401–458), with the 2017 text's in parentheses (2017 pp. 291–332). It introduces the objects that will carry the solution sets of perturbed sc-Fredholm sections with symmetries: functors $\Theta : X \to \mathbb Q^+$ on an ep-groupoid that are, near every point of their support, finite weighted sums of indicator functions of properly embedded smooth finite-dimensional submanifolds. §9.1 gives the definition (Definition 9.1.2 (2017: 9.1)), the local closedness of the orbit support (Lemma 9.1.5 (2017: 9.1)), symmetric local branching structures (Lemma 9.1.7 (2017: 9.2)) and the dimensional decomposition (Propositions 9.1.11 (2017: 9.2) and 9.1.12, the latter new in 2021 with its proof in Appendix §9.6.1). §9.2 attaches to $\Theta$ a section $T_\Theta$ of formal sums of tangent spaces (Theorem 9.2.1 (2017: 9.1)), proved through good and bad points and a graph representation of the sheets near a bad point (Proposition 9.2.8, Lemmas 9.2.9–9.2.11 (2017: 9.3, 9.6–9.8)); it defines the tangent $T\Theta$ (Definition 9.2.12 (2017: 9.6)) and, through the position of a submanifold relative to $\partial X$ (Lemma 9.2.13 (2017: 9.9)), the boundary $\partial\Theta$ (Proposition 9.2.15, Definition 9.2.16 (2017: 9.4, 9.7)). §9.3 orients $\Theta$ (Definition 9.3.2 (2017: 9.8)), compares orientations at good points (Lemma 9.3.4 (2017: 9.10)), gives an example (Example 9.3.5 (2017: 9.1)) and the boundary orientation (Definitions 9.3.6–9.3.10). §9.4 studies essential points and sets of measure zero of the sheets and decomposes an oriented structure (Propositions 9.4.9, 9.4.10 (2017: 9.5, 9.6)). §9.5 integrates sc-differential forms and proves Stokes' theorem; of it, this part covers the sets of measure zero in $|\mathrm{supp}(\Theta)|$ and the σ-algebras $L(S)$, $L(\partial S)$ (Definitions 9.5.1, 9.5.3, 9.5.14, Lemma 9.5.2 (2017: Definitions 9.15–9.17, Lemma 9.16)) and the isotropy factors of Lemmas 9.5.9–9.5.11 and Proposition 9.5.13 (2017: Lemmas 9.18, 9.19), not the integrals.

**What is proved, and what is not.** All definitions of §§9.1–9.4 and §9.6.1, and those of §9.5 that do not involve forms, are formalized. Theorem 9.2.1 (2017: 9.1) is proved for every branched ep⁺-subgroupoid of an ep-groupoid whose object M-polyfold is $\mathbb R^m$, and on a general M-polyfold under the graph representation of Lemmas 9.2.9–9.2.11 and the local constancy of the dimension (Remark 9.1.1), taken as hypotheses. Proposition 9.1.12 is proved in the same two forms, as are Lemma 9.6.4, Proposition 9.6.5 and Lemma 9.3.4 (the last for $n$-dimensional $\Theta$, with the level-1 continuity of frames in general). Lemmas 9.1.5, 9.1.7, 9.2.3, 9.2.5, 9.2.7 (1), 9.3.7, 9.4.2, 9.4.6, 9.4.8, 9.6.1 and 9.6.2, Propositions 9.2.15, 9.3.9, 9.4.9, 9.4.10 and the remarks with formal content are proved, several in a stronger form. Five places needed more than a reading. The uniqueness of Proposition 9.1.11 (2017: 9.2) does not hold as we read it (§IX.4); Lemma 9.2.13 (2017: 9.9) is read at points of $\partial X$, since its printed converse does not hold as we read it at interior points (§IX.12); the characterization in Definition 9.2.16 (2017: 9.7) is not unique as we read it (§IX.13); Lemma 9.3.4 (2017: 9.10) does not hold as we read it without equidimensionality, and two claims of Example 9.3.5 (2017: 9.1) do not either (§IX.15); Lemma 9.4.4 (2017: 9.13) needs sheets of one dimension (§IX.17), and so does Lemma 9.5.2 (2017: 9.16), whose proof rests on two sheets inducing the same null sets on their overlap (§IX.19). Not formalized: the global part of Proposition 9.1.11, Remark 9.1.4, Proposition 9.1.9, Lemma 9.4.3, the identification of $T_y\partial M$ with the outward direction, and in §9.5 the forms, integrals and measures, with Theorems 9.5.4, 9.5.5 and 9.5.15–9.5.18 (§IX.24).

**Six structural choices.** (1) *Tangent sets, linear algebra in charts.* Part I's $T_xX$ carries no linear structure (gap G.7). The tangent space $T_yM$ of a submanifold is the set of tangent vectors fixed by the tangent of the retraction that presents $M$, and $\mathrm{Gr}(x)$ is generated by such sets; in a chart the image of $T_yM$ is a linear subspace of the right dimension, and every argument that needs linear algebra is carried out there (§IX.6). (2) *Functors as functions.* $\Theta$ is a function on objects with $\Theta(s(\varphi)) = \Theta(t(\varphi))$, valued in $\mathbb Q$, nonnegativity a field. (3) *Dimension labellings.* Without the local constancy of the dimension a submanifold could a priori have several dimensions; the splitting by dimension takes labellings $\mathrm{dim} : I \to \mathbb N$ as hypotheses, which are unique when they exist (§IX.1). (4) *Slices instead of sequences.* Proposition 9.2.8 is used in the text to count the weights of a class of tangent sheets by the values of $\Theta$ at good points; the formalization counts them on one finite slice transverse to the class (§IX.9). The same device proves Proposition 9.6.5 (§IX.22). (5) *Hypotheses with witnesses on $\mathbb R^m$.* The facts of the text's §9.2 that need level-0 sc-calculus inside a partial quadrant are hypotheses on a general M-polyfold (`HasGraphChartAt`, `HasLocallyConstantDimAt`, `HasDomainInvarianceAt`, `HasContinuousLevelOneOn`), each proved for every ep-groupoid over $\mathbb R^m$, where the main results then hold without hypothesis (§IX.23). (6) *Two kinds of sheet geometry.* §9.4 is formalized twice: for Mathlib manifolds with corners, where Definitions 9.4.1, 9.4.5 and Lemmas 9.4.2, 9.4.6 are proved, and for sheets in an M-polyfold, where Lemmas 9.4.4 and 9.4.6 are the fields of a structure `SheetGeometry`, satisfiable by graphs in $\mathbb R^2$ (§IX.17). The null sets of §9.5 are data in the same way, a structure `SheetNullSets` (§IX.19).

Throughout, $X = (X, \mathbf X)$ is an ep-groupoid as in Part VII, $s, t$ its source and target, $\pi : X \to |X|$ the orbit map, $X^\infty$ the full subcategory of smooth objects, $G_x$ the isotropy group of $x$ with a natural representation $(\Phi, \Gamma)$ on a neighbourhood $U(x)$ (§VII.4). A *submanifold* is a smooth finite-dimensional submanifold in the sense of Part I (§IV.2): the image, near each of its points, of an sc⁺-retraction. For $\Theta : X \to \mathbb Q$, $\mathrm{supp}(\Theta) = \{\Theta > 0\}$ and $|\mathrm{supp}(\Theta)| = \pi(\mathrm{supp}(\Theta))$. A local branching structure at $x$ is $(U(x), (M_i)_{i \in I}, (\sigma_i)_{i \in I})$ as in Definition 9.1.2; we write $I_y = \{i \mid y \in M_i\}$ and $M_U = \bigcup_i M_i$.

### IX.3 The restriction remark, and Lemma 9.1.7 (2017: 9.2)

**The restriction** (V9.7). Before Lemma 9.1.7 the text replaces $M_i$ by $N_i = M_i \cap V(x_0)$ for $V(x_0) \subseteq U(x)$ and notes that some $N_i$ may be empty. Definition 9.1.2 asks $x \in M_i$, which the $N_i$ need not satisfy at $x_0$; the remark is formalized for an open $V \subseteq U(x)$ containing $x$ itself, which is the case the symmetrization uses ⟦Chapter9/Symmetric · EpGroupoid.LocalBranchingStructure.restrict⟧. The $N_i$ are submanifolds ⟦Chapter9/Symmetric · MPolyfold.IsSmoothSubmanifold.inter_isOpen⟧, and they are properly embedded in $V$ because they are closed in $U(x)$.

**Lemma 9.1.7 (2017: 9.2)** (V9.7) ⟦Chapter9/Symmetric · EpGroupoid.lemma_9_1_7⟧ ⟦Chapter9/Symmetric · EpGroupoid.lemma_9_1_7'⟧. *At $x \in \mathrm{supp}(\Theta)$ there is a local branching structure on the domain of a natural representation of $G_x$, with a $G_x$-action on the index set, $g * M_i = M_{g(i)}$ and $\sigma_{g(i)} = \sigma_i$; it can be found inside every open $V \ni x$.* **The text's proof** sets $J = I \times G_x$, $M_{(i,g)} = g * M_i$, $\sigma_{(i,g)} = \sigma_i / |G_x|$ and computes $\sum_{y \in g * M_i} \sigma_{(i,g)} = \frac{1}{|G_x|} \sum_g \Theta(g^{-1} * y) = \Theta(y)$ by functoriality. **The formalized proof** follows it ⟦Chapter9/Symmetric · EpGroupoid.LocalBranchingStructure.symmetrize⟧ ⟦Chapter9/Symmetric · EpGroupoid.SymmetricBranchingStructure⟧. *Step 1.* Restrict the given structure to the domain $U \subseteq V \cap U(x)$ of a natural representation (Theorem 7.1.13, §VII.4). *Step 2.* $\Phi(g)(M_i \cap U)$ is a submanifold properly embedded in $U$ through $x$, since $\Phi(g)$ is an sc-diffeomorphism of $U$ fixing $x$ ⟦Chapter9/Symmetric · MPolyfold.IsSmoothSubmanifold.image_of_isScDiffeoOn⟧; the text uses this without saying so. *Step 3.* The weights: $\Theta(\Phi(g)(y)) = \Theta(y)$ ⟦Chapter9/Symmetric · EpGroupoid.IsFunctorial.apply_Φ⟧ and $y \in \Phi(g)(A) \iff \Phi(g)^{-1}(y) \in A$ ⟦Chapter9/Symmetric · EpGroupoid.NaturalRep.mem_image_Φ_iff⟧ turn the text's computation into a sum over $G_x$ with $|G_x|$ = `Nat.card` of the finite group. *Step 4.* $G_x$ acts on $J$ by left multiplication on the second factor ⟦Chapter9/Symmetric · EpGroupoid.mulActionProdLeft⟧.

### IX.4 Proposition 9.1.11 (2017: 9.2): the local construction, and the uniqueness

**Proposition 9.1.11 (2017: 9.2)** (V9.9) ⟦Chapter9/Propositions · EpGroupoid.Proposition_9_1_11⟧. *A branched ep⁺-subgroupoid determines a unique sequence $\Theta_0, \Theta_1, \ldots$ of branched ep⁺-subgroupoids with $\Theta_k$ $k$-dimensional and $\Theta = \sum_k \Theta_k$, locally finite.* The formal statement: for every branched $\Theta$ there is exactly one family $(\Theta_k)$ with `IsDimDecomposition` ⟦Chapter9/Propositions · EpGroupoid.IsDimDecomposition⟧, every $\Theta_k$ branched and $k$-dimensional, at each object finitely many nonzero and $\Theta$ their sum, and every such family is locally finite ⟦Chapter9/Propositions · EpGroupoid.IsLocallyFiniteFamily⟧.

**The local construction.** The text splits the indices of a symmetric structure by the dimension of $M_i$ and puts $\Theta_k(y) = \sum_{i \in I_k, y \in M_i} \sigma_i$. Formally, for a labelling $\mathrm{dim}$, $\Theta_k$ ⟦Chapter9/Propositions · EpGroupoid.LocalBranchingStructure.dimPart⟧ has the $k$-dimensional local branching structure $(M_i)_{\mathrm{dim}\,i = k}$ ⟦Chapter9/Propositions · EpGroupoid.LocalBranchingStructure.dimPartStructure⟧ ⟦Chapter9/Propositions · EpGroupoid.LocalBranchingStructure.dimPartStructure_isOfDim⟧ and $\Theta = \sum_k \Theta_k$ on $U(x)$ ⟦Chapter9/Propositions · EpGroupoid.LocalBranchingStructure.eq_sum_dimPart⟧; $\Theta_k = 0$ if no sheet has dimension $k$ ⟦Chapter9/Propositions · EpGroupoid.LocalBranchingStructure.dimPart_eq_zero⟧, and $\Theta_n = \Theta$ for an $n$-dimensional structure ⟦Chapter9/Propositions · EpGroupoid.LocalBranchingStructure.dimPart_eq_of_isOfDim⟧. For a symmetric structure $G_x$ preserves dimension, since $\Phi(g)$ is an sc-diffeomorphism ⟦Chapter9/Propositions · EpGroupoid.SymmetricBranchingStructure.dim_smul⟧, so $\Theta_k$ is $G_x$-invariant ⟦Chapter9/Propositions · EpGroupoid.SymmetricBranchingStructure.dimPart_Φ⟧. That the local $\Theta_k$ does not depend on the structure is Proposition 9.1.12 (§IX.5).

**The uniqueness, as we read it, does not hold** ⟦Chapter9/Propositions · EpGroupoid.not_proposition_9_1_11_rn⟧. On the trivial ep-groupoid of $\mathbb R^2$, let $L$ be the $x$-axis ⟦Chapter9/Propositions · EpGroupoid.axis2⟧ and $\Theta = 1_L$. *Family A:* $\Theta_0 = 1_{\{0\}}$, $\Theta_1 = 1_{L \setminus \{0\}}$ ⟦Chapter9/Propositions · EpGroupoid.decompA⟧ ⟦Chapter9/Propositions · EpGroupoid.decompA_isDimDecomposition⟧. *Family B:* $\Theta_0 = 0$, $\Theta_1 = 1_L$ ⟦Chapter9/Propositions · EpGroupoid.decompB⟧ ⟦Chapter9/Propositions · EpGroupoid.decompB_isDimDecomposition⟧. In family A, $L \setminus \{0\}$ is a $1$-dimensional submanifold properly embedded in the open set $\mathbb R^2 \setminus \{0\}$, which is all Definition 9.1.2 asks at its points, and $1_{\{0\}}$ is $0$-dimensional; both families satisfy (1) and (2). The proof of the text constructs family B. A condition that singles it out, for instance that each $\Theta_k$ be represented near every point by a subfamily of a local branching structure of $\Theta$, is a possible repair; it and the global gluing of the local $\Theta_k$ (across morphisms, with the paracompactness of $|\mathrm{supp}(\Theta_k)|$ and local finiteness) are not formalized (gap G.96).

### IX.5 Proposition 9.1.12: the statement and its cases

**Proposition 9.1.12** (V9.10) ⟦Chapter9/Propositions · EpGroupoid.Proposition_9_1_12⟧, new in the 2021 edition. *For two local branching structures of $\Theta$ on $U(x)$ there is an open $G_x$-invariant $V(x) \subseteq U(x)$ on which the dimensional decompositions agree.* The formal conclusion is `DimPartsAgreeNear`: an open $V \ni x$ in both domains on which $\Theta_k = \Theta'_k$ for every $k$ ⟦Chapter9/Propositions · EpGroupoid.LocalBranchingStructure.DimPartsAgreeNear⟧. ### IX.25 What the formalization taught us

The definition of a branched ep⁺-subgroupoid is local and says nothing about how the local pictures fit together, and three statements that read as uniqueness claims, Proposition 9.1.11, Definition 9.2.16 and Lemma 9.3.4, turned out to depend on exactly that: an indicator can be split into a point and a punctured line, a characterization asked only on its own support is met by zero, and a lower-dimensional sheet through a bad point escapes the good points. In each case the repair is a condition that ties the object to the local branching structures of $\Theta$ (decompositions by subfamilies, (2) asked on $\mathrm{supp}(\Theta)$, equidimensionality). The analytic heart of §9.2, the graph representation of tangent sheets near a bad point, is a statement about one chart in which sheets of all dimensions are graphs; separated from the counting it serves, it becomes a single hypothesis, true on $\mathbb R^m$ by the inverse function theorem, from which Proposition 9.1.12, Theorem 9.2.1, Lemma 9.6.4 and Proposition 9.6.5 follow. Two steps the text leaves to "similarly" (Lemma 9.6.4) or asserts (the independence on p. 457) needed that both structures be compared on one common slice. Finally, "measure zero" in §9.6.1 is not needed: the arguments only use that a piece of a $k$-dimensional submanifold is not covered by finitely many thinner ones, which is topology. In §9.5 the opposite happens: the measure-theoretic part needs only that the null sets of the sheets form a local σ-ideal compatible on overlaps, and that compatibility is exactly where dimensions must agree; and of the isotropy computations that normalize the integrals, everything except the integrals themselves is finite group theory.
