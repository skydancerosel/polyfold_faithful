**V10.1 (Definition 10.1.1 (2017: 10.1), equivalences and the class $E$; `Polyfold.EpFunctor.IsEquivalence`, `Polyfold.EP.equivalences`, `Polyfold.GroupoidFunctor.full_iff`, `Polyfold.GroupoidFunctor.faithful_iff`, `Polyfold.GroupoidFunctor.essSurj_iff`, `Polyfold.EpFunctor.IsEquivalence.essSurj`, `Polyfold.EpFunctor.IsEquivalence.exists_iso`, `Polyfold.EpFunctor.IsEquivalence.exists_mor_eq`, `Polyfold.EpFunctor.IsEquivalence.mor_injective`, `Polyfold.EpFunctor.IsEquivalence.id`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.Examples.pairToPoint`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.Examples.isEquivalence_pairToPoint`).** An equivalence $F : X \to Y$ of ep-groupoids is an sc-smooth functor which is full and faithful (every $\psi : F(x) \to F(x')$ is $F(\varphi)$ for some $\varphi : x \to x'$, and $F$ is injective on each set of morphisms $x \to x'$), induces a homeomorphism $|F| : |X| \to |Y|$ of the orbit spaces, and is a local sc-diffeomorphism on objects. The equivalences form a class $E$ of morphisms of $\mathcal{EP}$ containing the identities and closed under composition. An equivalence is essentially surjective: every $y$ is the target of a morphism from some $F(x)$. The identity and the functor from the pair groupoid of $\mathbb Z$ to the point are equivalences. *Book:* Definition 10.1.1, p. 461 (2017: Definition 10.1, p. 333), with the category notions recalled on pp. 461–462. *Hidden hypotheses:* none. *Deviation (same):* none (Findings, item 1).

**V10.2 (Lemma 10.1.2 (2017: 10.1) and the remarks around it; `Polyfold.EpFunctor.lemma_10_1_2`, `Polyfold.EpFunctor.isEquivalence_iff`, `Polyfold.EpFunctor.IsEquivalence.isEquivalence_toFunctor`, `Polyfold.EpFunctor.IsEquivalence.comp`, `Polyfold.GroupoidFunctor.injective_orbitMap_of_full`, `Polyfold.GroupoidFunctor.surjective_orbitMap_iff`, `Polyfold.GroupoidFunctor.bijective_orbitMap_of_isEquivalence`, `Polyfold.EpFunctor.continuous_orbitMap`, `Polyfold.EpFunctor.isOpenMap_orbitMap`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.Examples.not_exists_leftInverse_pairToPoint`).** An sc-smooth functor which is an equivalence of categories and a local sc-diffeomorphism on objects is an equivalence, and conversely every equivalence is both. For a functor of groupoids, $|F|$ is injective if $F$ is full, surjective if and only if $F$ is essentially surjective, and bijective for an equivalence of categories; $|F|$ is continuous, and open if $F$ is open on objects. The composite of two equivalences is an equivalence, and the equivalence from the pair groupoid of $\mathbb Z$ to the point has no left inverse on objects. *Book:* Lemma 10.1.2, p. 462 (2017: Lemma 10.1, p. 334), and the remarks on pp. 461–462. *Hidden hypotheses:* none. *Deviation (stronger):* the lemma is stated with its converse; "not invertible as a functor" gets a witness.

**V10.3 (Proposition 10.1.3 (2017: 10.1), the refinement $X_{\mathcal U}$; `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.OpenCover`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.OpenCover.univ`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.OpenCover.objSet`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.OpenCover.morSet`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.OpenCover.groupoidStr`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.OpenCover.objPolyfold`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.OpenCover.morPolyfold`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.OpenCover.isLocalScDiffeo_src`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.OpenCover.isLocalScDiffeo_tgt`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.OpenCover.exists_isProperMap`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.OpenCover.isScSmoothCompSheet`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.OpenCover.epGroupoid`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.OpenCover.functor`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.OpenCover.proposition_10_1_3`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.Examples.reflectionCover`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.Examples.isEquivalence_reflectionCover`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.Examples.not_injective_reflectionCover_obj`).** For a covering of the object M-polyfold of $X$ by open sets $U_i$, $i \in \iota$, the refinement $X_{\mathcal U}$ has as objects the pairs $(i, x)$ with $x \in U_i$, an open subset of $\iota \times X$ with $\iota$ discrete, and as morphisms the triples $(i, \varphi, j)$ with $s(\varphi) \in U_i$, $t(\varphi) \in U_j$, an open subset of $(\iota \times \iota) \times \mathbf X$, with $s(i, \varphi, j) = (i, s(\varphi))$, $t(i, \varphi, j) = (j, t(\varphi))$, $(j, \psi, k) \circ (i, \varphi, j) = (i, \psi \circ \varphi, k)$, units $(i, 1_x, i)$ and inverses $(j, \varphi^{-1}, i)$. If $X$ has an object, $X_{\mathcal U}$ is an ep-groupoid and $F : X_{\mathcal U} \to X$, $(i, x) \mapsto x$, $(i, \varphi, j) \mapsto \varphi$, is an equivalence. For the covering $\{x > -1\}$, $\{x < 1\}$ of $\mathbb Z/2 \ltimes \mathbb R$ this $F$ is an equivalence which is not injective on objects. *Book:* Proposition 10.1.3, p. 462 (2017: Proposition 10.1, p. 334), with the construction of $X_{\mathcal U}$ on p. 462. *Hidden hypotheses:* the object set is nonempty (the open-subset M-polyfold structure needs a nonempty set; for an empty $X$ the statement is trivial). *Deviation (explicit):* the covering is indexed by a type, the text's index set being the covering itself; "induced from $X$ and $\mathbf X$" is read as open subsets of products with a discrete factor; the printed $(U, \varphi, v)$ in the formula for $F$ is read $(U, \varphi, V)$ (possible misprint) (Findings, item 2). *Proof:* the properness at $(i, x)$ takes a closed neighbourhood of $x$ inside $U_i$ and a neighbourhood with the properness property of $X$, and is proved with ultrafilters instead of sequences.

**V10.4 (Definition 10.1.4 (2017: 10.2), natural equivalence; `Polyfold.definition_10_1_4`, `Polyfold.EpNatTrans`, `Polyfold.EpFunctor.NaturallyEquivalent`, `Polyfold.EpFunctor.naturallyEquivalent_equivalence`, `Polyfold.EpFunctor.NaturallyEquivalent.comp_left`, `Polyfold.EpFunctor.NaturallyEquivalent.comp_right`, `Polyfold.EpNatTrans.toNatIso`).** A natural transformation $\tau : F \to G$ of sc-smooth functors $X \to Y$ is an sc-smooth map $\tau : X \to \mathbf Y$ with $\tau(x) : F(x) \to G(x)$ and $\tau(x') \circ F(\varphi) = G(\varphi) \circ \tau(x)$ for $\varphi : x \to x'$; $F \simeq G$ if one exists. $\simeq$ is an equivalence relation, and $F \simeq G$ implies $F \circ \Psi \simeq G \circ \Psi$ and $\Phi \circ F \simeq \Phi \circ G$. A natural transformation is a natural isomorphism of the underlying functors. *Book:* Definition 10.1.4, p. 463 (2017: Definition 10.2, p. 335), and the claims after it, p. 464. *Hidden hypotheses:* none. *Deviation (same):* none; the definition is Definition 8.1.13 (Findings, item 3).

**V10.5 (Proposition 10.1.5 (2017: 10.2), natural equivalence preserves equivalences; `Polyfold.EpFunctor.proposition_10_1_5`, `Polyfold.EpFunctor.IsEquivalence.of_natTrans`, `Polyfold.EpNatTrans.continuous_app`, `Polyfold.EpNatTrans.isLocalScDiffeo_app`, `Polyfold.EpNatTrans.isLocalScDiffeo_obj`).** If $F \simeq G$, then $F$ is an equivalence if and only if $G$ is. *Book:* Proposition 10.1.5, p. 464 (2017: Proposition 10.2, p. 336), quoted from the literature without proof. *Hidden hypotheses:* none. *Deviation (same):* none. *Proof:* $s \circ \tau = F$ makes $\tau$ a local sc-diffeomorphism, so $G = t \circ \tau$ is one on objects; the underlying functors are naturally isomorphic, so $G$ is an equivalence of categories, and Lemma 10.1.2 applies (Findings, item 4).

**V10.6 (Proposition 10.1.6 (2017: 10.3), the two-out-of-three property; `Polyfold.EpFunctor.proposition_10_1_6`, `Polyfold.MPolyfold.IsLocalScDiffeoAt`, `Polyfold.MPolyfold.IsLocalScDiffeoAt.comp`, `Polyfold.MPolyfold.IsLocalScDiffeoAt.congr`, `Polyfold.MPolyfold.IsLocalScDiffeoAt.of_comp_left`, `Polyfold.MPolyfold.IsLocalScDiffeoAt.of_comp_right`, `Polyfold.EpFunctor.isLocalScDiffeoAt_mor`, `Polyfold.EpFunctor.isLocalScDiffeo_mor`, `Polyfold.EpFunctor.isLocalScDiffeoAt_obj_tgt`).** If $F : X \to Y$ and $\Phi \circ F : X \to Z$ are equivalences and $\Phi : Y \to Z$ is sc-smooth, then $\Phi$ is an equivalence. A functor which is a local sc-diffeomorphism on objects at $s(\varphi)$ is one at $t(\varphi)$, and one on morphisms at $\varphi$. *Book:* Proposition 10.1.6, p. 464 (2017: Proposition 10.3, p. 336). *Hidden hypotheses:* none. *Deviation (same):* the text's argument on objects gives the local sc-diffeomorphism property of $\Phi$ only at the points $F(x)$; the transport along a morphism $F(x) \to y$ is added. ### Sign-off items

1. (V10.1) An equivalence of ep-groupoids is a full and faithful sc-smooth functor, a local sc-diffeomorphism on objects, inducing a homeomorphism of orbit spaces.
2. (V10.2) An sc-smooth functor is an equivalence iff it is an equivalence of categories and a local sc-diffeomorphism on objects.
3. (V10.3) The refinement of a nonempty ep-groupoid along an open covering is an ep-groupoid, and its projection to the ep-groupoid is an equivalence.
4. (V10.4) Natural equivalence of sc-smooth functors is an equivalence relation compatible with composition on both sides.
5. (V10.5) A functor naturally equivalent to an equivalence is an equivalence.
6. (V10.6) If $F$ and $\Phi \circ F$ are equivalences, so is $\Phi$.
7. (V10.7) The tangent of an equivalence is an equivalence.
8. (V10.8) For $\mathrm{id}$ and $-\mathrm{id}$ on $\mathbb Z/2 \ltimes \mathbb R^2$, no family through $1_0$ is even continuous at $0$, while $\alpha_z = (-1, z)$ is sc-smooth.
9. (V10.9) For an equivalence and $\psi : F(x) \to y$, $g \mapsto \psi \circ F(g) \circ \psi^{-1}$ is an isomorphism $G_x \to G_y$ mapping $T_x$ onto $T_y$.
10. (V10.10) For an equivalence, $F_R$ is continuous and open, and one reduced morphism space is Hausdorff iff the other is.
11. (V10.11) An equivalence with Hausdorff reduced morphism spaces descends to an equivalence of the reduced ep-groupoids.
12. (V10.12) The functor of Example 10.1.15 is compatible with no map of the reduced morphism spaces.
13. (V10.13) (LUC) holds for one side of an equivalence iff it holds for the other.
14. (V10.14) The Zhou condition holds for one side of an equivalence iff it holds for the other.
15. (V10.15) (SLUC) holds for one side of an equivalence iff it holds for the other.
16. (V10.16) (SLR) passes along equivalences, through Proposition 10.1.9 for the lifts.
17. (V10.17) The weak fibred product of two sc-smooth functors is a groupoid with the structure maps of Definition 10.2.1.
18. (V10.18) If $F$ or $G$ is a local sc-diffeomorphism on objects, the weak fibred product is an ep-groupoid with the iterated sub-M-polyfold structures.
19. (V10.19) For $F$ an equivalence, $\pi_Z$ is an equivalence, $F \circ \pi_X \simeq G \circ \pi_Z$, $d = d_Z \circ \pi_Z$, and tameness and paracompactness of the orbit space pass from $Z$.
20. (V10.20) For $G$ an equivalence, $\pi_X$ is an equivalence and $d = d_X \circ \pi_X$; for two equivalences, $d_X(x) = d(x, \phi, z) = d_Z(z)$.
21. (V10.21) For two equivalences both projections of the weak fibred product are equivalences, and $\tau(x, \phi, z) = \phi$ is natural.
22. (V10.22) A strong bundle equivalence is a strong bundle functor over an equivalence, an equivalence at level $[0]$, a homeomorphism of $|W[1]|$, and locally a strong bundle isomorphism on objects and morphisms.

25. (V10.25) $|\Phi| = |\Psi|$ implies $\Phi_*f = \Psi_*f$ for every section functor $f$.
26. (V10.26) $(\Psi \circ \Phi)^* = \Phi^* \circ \Psi^*$ and $(\Psi \circ \Phi)_* = \Psi_* \circ \Phi_*$.
27. (V10.27) $\Phi_*$ and $\Phi^*$ are mutually inverse bijections of sc-smooth section functors, depend only on $|\Phi|$, and preserve $\mathrm{sc}^+$-section functors.
28. (V10.28) A natural transformation of strong bundle functors is a natural family $T(w) : \Phi(w) \to \Psi(w)$ over a natural transformation $\tau$ of the base functors.
