The hypotheses. No hypothesis beyond the text's is taken on a general ep-groupoid, apart from three technical ones, each with a witness: the object set is nonempty in Proposition 10.1.3 (the open-subset M-polyfold structure of Part I needs a nonempty set; the trivial covering of any nonempty ep-groupoid and a two-set covering of $\mathbb Z/2 \ltimes \mathbb R$ are witnesses); the weak fibred product is given its ep-groupoid structure under `Polyfold.WeakFibredProduct.Admissible` ($F$ or $G$ a local sc-diffeomorphism on objects), which every equivalence supplies (`Polyfold.WeakFibredProduct.Admissible.of_isEquivalence_left`, `Polyfold.WeakFibredProduct.Admissible.of_isEquivalence_right`); and the reduction of Proposition 10.1.19 assumes Proposition 10.1.9 for the lifts, a hypothesis satisfied when the two ep-groupoids agree (`Polyfold.EP.isLR_iff_lift_self`). Proposition 10.1.14 keeps the text's hypothesis that both reduced morphism spaces are Hausdorff. In §10.3 no hypothesis is added; a diagram is a structure one universe above the object and morphism types of the ep-groupoids, so that the classes of diagrams are sets and $\mathcal{EP}(E^{-1})$ is a large category like $\mathcal{EP}$. In §10.5 no hypothesis is added; the classes of diagrams of strong bundles are taken for the equivalence relation generated by having a common refinement (V10.51).

**V10.1 (Definition 10.1.1 (2017: 10.1), equivalences and the class $E$; `Polyfold.EpFunctor.IsEquivalence`, `Polyfold.EP.equivalences`, `Polyfold.GroupoidFunctor.full_iff`, `Polyfold.GroupoidFunctor.faithful_iff`, `Polyfold.GroupoidFunctor.essSurj_iff`, `Polyfold.EpFunctor.IsEquivalence.essSurj`, `Polyfold.EpFunctor.IsEquivalence.exists_iso`, `Polyfold.EpFunctor.IsEquivalence.exists_mor_eq`, `Polyfold.EpFunctor.IsEquivalence.mor_injective`, `Polyfold.EpFunctor.IsEquivalence.id`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.Examples.pairToPoint`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.Examples.isEquivalence_pairToPoint`).** An equivalence $F : X \to Y$ of ep-groupoids is an sc-smooth functor which is full and faithful (every $\psi : F(x) \to F(x')$ is $F(\varphi)$ for some $\varphi : x \to x'$, and $F$ is injective on each set of morphisms $x \to x'$), induces a homeomorphism $|F| : |X| \to |Y|$ of the orbit spaces, and is a local sc-diffeomorphism on objects. The equivalences form a class $E$ of morphisms of $\mathcal{EP}$ containing the identities and closed under composition. An equivalence is essentially surjective: every $y$ is the target of a morphism from some $F(x)$. The identity and the functor from the pair groupoid of $\mathbb Z$ to the point are equivalences. *Book:* Definition 10.1.1, p. 461 (2017: Definition 10.1, p. 333), with the category notions recalled on pp. 461–462. *Hidden hypotheses:* none. *Deviation (same):* none (Findings, item 1).

**V10.2 (Lemma 10.1.2 (2017: 10.1) and the remarks around it; `Polyfold.EpFunctor.lemma_10_1_2`, `Polyfold.EpFunctor.isEquivalence_iff`, `Polyfold.EpFunctor.IsEquivalence.isEquivalence_toFunctor`, `Polyfold.EpFunctor.IsEquivalence.comp`, `Polyfold.GroupoidFunctor.injective_orbitMap_of_full`, `Polyfold.GroupoidFunctor.surjective_orbitMap_iff`, `Polyfold.GroupoidFunctor.bijective_orbitMap_of_isEquivalence`, `Polyfold.EpFunctor.continuous_orbitMap`, `Polyfold.EpFunctor.isOpenMap_orbitMap`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.Examples.not_exists_leftInverse_pairToPoint`).** An sc-smooth functor which is an equivalence of categories and a local sc-diffeomorphism on objects is an equivalence, and conversely every equivalence is both. For a functor of groupoids, $|F|$ is injective if $F$ is full, surjective if and only if $F$ is essentially surjective, and bijective for an equivalence of categories; $|F|$ is continuous, and open if $F$ is open on objects. The composite of two equivalences is an equivalence, and the equivalence from the pair groupoid of $\mathbb Z$ to the point has no left inverse on objects. *Book:* Lemma 10.1.2, p. 462 (2017: Lemma 10.1, p. 334), and the remarks on pp. 461–462. *Hidden hypotheses:* none. *Deviation (stronger):* the lemma is stated with its converse; "not invertible as a functor" gets a witness.

**V10.3 (Proposition 10.1.3 (2017: 10.1), the refinement $X_{\mathcal U}$; `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.OpenCover`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.OpenCover.univ`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.OpenCover.objSet`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.OpenCover.morSet`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.OpenCover.groupoidStr`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.OpenCover.objPolyfold`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.OpenCover.morPolyfold`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.OpenCover.isLocalScDiffeo_src`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.OpenCover.isLocalScDiffeo_tgt`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.OpenCover.exists_isProperMap`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.OpenCover.isScSmoothCompSheet`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.OpenCover.epGroupoid`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.OpenCover.functor`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.OpenCover.proposition_10_1_3`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.Examples.reflectionCover`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.Examples.isEquivalence_reflectionCover`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.Examples.not_injective_reflectionCover_obj`).** For a covering of the object M-polyfold of $X$ by open sets $U_i$, $i \in \iota$, the refinement $X_{\mathcal U}$ has as objects the pairs $(i, x)$ with $x \in U_i$, an open subset of $\iota \times X$ with $\iota$ discrete, and as morphisms the triples $(i, \varphi, j)$ with $s(\varphi) \in U_i$, $t(\varphi) \in U_j$, an open subset of $(\iota \times \iota) \times \mathbf X$, with $s(i, \varphi, j) = (i, s(\varphi))$, $t(i, \varphi, j) = (j, t(\varphi))$, $(j, \psi, k) \circ (i, \varphi, j) = (i, \psi \circ \varphi, k)$, units $(i, 1_x, i)$ and inverses $(j, \varphi^{-1}, i)$. 22. (V10.22) A strong bundle equivalence is a strong bundle functor over an equivalence, an equivalence at level $[0]$, a homeomorphism of $|W[1]|$, and locally a strong bundle isomorphism on objects and morphisms.

24. (V10.24) $|\Phi| = |\Psi|$ implies $\Phi^*f' = \Psi^*f'$ for every section functor $f'$.
25. (V10.25) $|\Phi| = |\Psi|$ implies $\Phi_*f = \Psi_*f$ for every section functor $f$.
26. (V10.26) $(\Psi \circ \Phi)^* = \Phi^* \circ \Psi^*$ and $(\Psi \circ \Phi)_* = \Psi_* \circ \Phi_*$.
27. (V10.27) $\Phi_*$ and $\Phi^*$ are mutually inverse bijections of sc-smooth section functors, depend only on $|\Phi|$, and preserve $\mathrm{sc}^+$-section functors.
28. (V10.28) A natural transformation of strong bundle functors is a natural family $T(w) : \Phi(w) \to \Psi(w)$ over a natural transformation $\tau$ of the base functors.

30. (V10.30) Definitions 10.4.8 and 10.4.10 agree for strong bundle functors, and naturally equivalent strong bundle functors induce the same map of orbit spaces.
31. (V10.31) Strong bundles over ep-groupoids and strong bundle functors form a category with the strong bundle equivalences as a distinguished class.
32. (V10.32) Isomorphisms of $\mathcal{EP}$ and composites of equivalences are equivalences, and so is a functor naturally equivalent to one.
33. (V10.33) A refinement of a diagram $X \leftarrow A \to Y$ is given by an equivalence of the middle ep-groupoids commuting with both legs up to natural equivalence.
34. (V10.34) A refinement of a refinement is a refinement.
35. (V10.35) A common refinement of two diagrams refines both.
36. (V10.36) Having a common refinement is transitive, through the weak fibred product (10.11).
37. (V10.37) Equivalence of diagrams is an equivalence relation.
38. (V10.38) The map $|\Phi| \circ |F|^{-1}$ of orbit spaces is continuous and unchanged under refinement.
39. (V10.39) $|[d]|$ is well defined, and a homeomorphism when both legs are equivalences.
40. (V10.40) The composition of classes of diagrams through weak fibred products is well defined, associative and unital.
