The setting. Sc-Banach spaces and maps are those of Chapter 1. A *retraction* is a total map
$r : E_0 \to E_0$ with an explicit domain $U$; an *M-polyfold* is a Hausdorff paracompact space with
one sc-smooth atlas representing its class, and every notion is defined through the maximal atlas
of all charts compatible with it. Sc-smoothness between retracts quantifies over all retractions
onto the source (equivalent to the text's "one"). Where the text computes in the model
$C = [0, \infty)^k \oplus W$, the formal statements use a presentation $T : E \cong \mathbb R^n \oplus
W$ of $C$ and the coordinate functionals $\lambda_i = (T\,\cdot)_i$; the subspaces $E_x$, the cones
$C_x$ and tameness are defined through $T$. The tangent bundle $TX$ is a quotient of tuples
$(x, \varphi, h)$ with its topology and charts; its fibres $T_xX$ are sets, identified with
$T_{\varphi(x)}O$ by the charts, with no linear structure of their own. Theorem 1.3.5 (preprint 1.25) of Chapter 1
(V1.23) is proved in `Polyfold/Chapter2/DegeneracyIndex.lean` by the argument of §2.4 (preprint §2.3).

**V2.10 (Definition 2.3.2 (preprint 2.11), charts; `Polyfold.MChart`, `Polyfold.MChart.overlap`,

    onto $C_yO'$, at every level-$1$ point.
36. (V2.36) A nonempty retract, and a nonempty relatively open subset of a partial quadrant, is an
    M-polyfold with the chart $\mathrm{id}$.
37. (V2.37) $\dim(T_xO/T^R_xO) \le d_X(x)$ in every chart of every M-polyfold at points on level
    $1$; the second statement holds without its hypothesis (V2.33).
38. (V2.38) On a relatively open subset of a partial quadrant, the degeneracy index of the
    M-polyfold equals $d_C$.
39. (V2.39) $C_e$ and $E_e$ are the cone and the subspace cut out by the coordinates vanishing at
    $e$, in a presentation of $C$.
40. (V2.40) A retraction is tame if it preserves $d_C$ and, at smooth points of $O$, $T_xO$ has an
    sc-complement inside $E_x$ (for every presentation of $C$).
41. (V2.41) For a tame $r$ at a smooth $x \in O$, $(1 - Dr(x))E \subseteq E_x$ is an sc-complement
    of $T_xO$; only the second tameness condition is used.
42. (V2.42) For a tame retract, $\dim(T_xO/T^R_xO) = d_O(x)$ at smooth points, where $T^R_xO$ is an
    sc-subspace and $C_xO$ a partial quadrant in $T_xO$, and $d_O = d_C$ everywhere; a tame chart of
    any M-polyfold realizes $d_X$.
43. (V2.43) A tame M-polyfold has an equivalent atlas of charts with tame models.
44. (V2.44) A face is the closure of a component of $\{d_X = 1\}$; face-structured means every $x$
    lies in exactly $d_X(x)$ faces.
45. (V2.45) Near a point of a tame retract, the retraction maps points of a coordinate hyperplane
    $f_i$ into the closure of a single component of $\{d_C = 1\} \cap O \cap f_i$.
46. (V2.46) The faces of a tame retract are the components of the sets $f_i \cap O$, distinct for
    distinct $i$ through a common point.
47. (V2.47) A tame retract is face-structured.
48. (V2.48) In a tame M-polyfold each point has a tame chart realizing $d_X$ whose model is
    face-structured, and lies in at most $d_X(x)$ global faces.
49. (V2.49) A face of a tame retract is the retract of $r \circ P_i$ on a neighbourhood of the face,
    hence a sub-M-polyfold, and with its induced structure it is tame with $d_F = d_O - 1$.
50. (V2.50) Every point of a tame M-polyfold lies in exactly $d_X(x)$ local faces, each tame with
    $d_F(x) = d_X(x) - 1$; the intersection of $\#\sigma$ of them is, near $x$, a tame sub-M-polyfold
    $A$ with $d_A = d_X - \#\sigma$.
51. (V2.51) In a tame M-polyfold the interior $F \cap \{d_X = 1\}$ of a face is a component $G$
    and a sub-M-polyfold; if $X$ is face-structured, every face is a tame sub-M-polyfold with
    $d_X = d_F + 1$.
52. (V2.52) $X \times Y$ is an M-polyfold with the product charts, $d_{X\times Y} \le d_X + d_Y$, with
    equality and $X \times Y$ tame when $X$ and $Y$ are tame.
53. (V2.53) For a local sc-diffeomorphism $f$ and an sc-smooth $g$ the fibred product is a
    sub-M-polyfold of $X \times Y$ with $d = d_Y$, tame if $Y$ is; if both are local
    sc-diffeomorphisms, $d = d_X = d_Y$, and $2d = d_{X\times Y}$ for tame $X$, $Y$.
54. (V2.54) A strong bundle map preserves the double filtration, is linear in the fibre and
    sc-smooth on $[0]$ and $[1]$; a strong bundle retraction is an idempotent one, covering an
    sc-smooth retraction.
55. (V2.55) A local strong bundle retract $K = R(U \triangleleft F)$ and its part $K[1]$ are
    sc-smooth retracts, with sc-smooth projections to $O$.
56. (V2.56) Sections of $K$ are sc-smooth into $K[0]$, sc⁺ into $K[1]$; sc⁺ implies sc-smooth.
57. (V2.57) A strong bundle chart is a fibrewise linear homeomorphism $P^{-1}(V) \to K$ over a chart
    of $X$; compatibility means sc-smooth transitions on $[0]$ and $[1]$; equivalence of strong
    bundle atlases is an equivalence relation, and charts restrict to open subsets of the base.
58. (V2.58) A strong bundle is $P : Y \to X$ with a strong bundle atlas; $Y[0]$ and $Y[1]$ are
    M-polyfolds, $P[0]$ and $P[1]$ are sc-smooth, and one chart decides whether $y \in Y_{m,k}$; the
    charts compatible with the atlas depend only on its class.
59. (V2.59) Sections of a strong bundle are sc-smooth into $Y[0]$, sc⁺ in the charts $\Phi[1]$, which
    is the same as sc-smooth into $Y[1]$.
60. (V2.60) The pull-back $f^*Y$ is the fibre product of $P$ and $f$.
61. (V2.61) The pull-back of a strong bundle along an sc-smooth map is a strong bundle, with the
    pulled-back charts and retractions $R_g$.
62. (V2.62) A paracompact Hausdorff locally metrizable space is metrizable (Smirnov), proved in a
    pull-back form.
63. (V2.63) Sc^k-smoothness restricts to smaller domains and is a local property.
64. (V2.64) $Y[1]$ is Hausdorff and paracompact (metrizable), and the inclusion $Y[1] \to Y[0]$ is
    sc-smooth.
