**V9.1 (Definition 9.1.2 (2017: 9.1), branched ep⁺-subgroupoids; `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.IsFunctorial`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.supp`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.orbitSupp`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.LocalBranchingStructure`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.IsBranchedEpSubgroupoid`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.isBranchedEpSubgroupoid_zero`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.localBranchingStructure_indicator`, `Polyfold.EpGroupoid.isBranchedEpSubgroupoid_indicator`, `Polyfold.rnPolyfold_isSmoothSubmanifold_singleton`).** A branched ep⁺-subgroupoid is a functor $\Theta \ge 0$ whose support lies in the smooth set, whose orbit support $|\mathrm{supp}(\Theta)|$ is paracompact, and which at every $x \in \mathrm{supp}(\Theta)$ has a local branching structure: an open $U(x)$, finitely many smooth submanifolds $M_i \subseteq U(x)$ through $x$ with proper inclusions, weights $\sigma_i > 0$, and $\Theta(y) = \sum_{y \in M_i} \sigma_i$ on $U(x)$. The zero functor and the indicator of a smooth point of $\mathbb R^n$ are examples. *Book:* Definition 9.1.2, p. 402 (2017: Definition 9.1, p. 292). *Hidden hypotheses:* none. *Deviation (restated):* $\Theta$ takes values in $\mathbb Q$ with $\Theta \ge 0$ a field; "the topology induced on $|\mathrm{supp}(\Theta)|$ is paracompact" is a `ParacompactSpace` instance on the subtype; "the inclusion $M_i \to U(x)$ is proper" is `IsProperMap` of the inclusion. The 2017 text's "(ii) and (iii)" in (4) reads "(2) and (3)" in 2021, which we follow (Findings, item 1). By Remark 9.1.4 the paracompactness in (1) holds when $|\mathrm{supp}(\Theta)|$ is component compact (`Polyfold.EpGroupoid.isBranchedEpSubgroupoid_of_isComponentCompact`, V9.63; point G.94 settled).

It is locally constant on every M-polyfold: in a chart it is the rank of $Dt$ for the chart sc⁺-retraction $t$ of the submanifold, locally constant along the retract; so a labelled local branching structure exists at every point of $\mathrm{supp}(\Theta)$. $\Theta$ is $n$-dimensional if at every point of its support some local branching structure has only $n$-dimensional $M_i$; the zero functor is $n$-dimensional for every $n$. At a point of $\mathrm{supp}(\Theta)$ the sheets of any two structures have the same tangent spaces and dimensions; hence the footnote: an $n$-dimensional $\Theta$ with nonempty support is not $m$-dimensional for $m \ne n$. $\Theta$ is locally equidimensional if at every point of its support some local branching structure has sheets of one dimension, equivalently every local branching structure, restricted near the point; the dimension is then locally constant on the support. ### Sign-off items

1. (V9.1) A branched ep⁺-subgroupoid is a nonnegative functor, supported in the smooth set with paracompact orbit support, locally a weighted sum of indicators of properly embedded smooth submanifolds through the point.
2. (V9.2) The dimension of a submanifold at a point is the dimension of its tangent space in a chart, independent of the chart and locally constant; $\Theta$ is $n$-dimensional if it has $n$-dimensional local branching structures.
3. (V9.3) On $\mathbb R^n$, finite weighted sums of indicators of closed submanifolds and indicators of locally closed submanifolds are branched ep⁺-subgroupoids.
4. (V9.4) The graphs of $\beta_1$, $\beta_2$ and those of $0$, $\beta_1 + \beta_2$ are two local branching structures of one $\Theta$ on $\mathbb R^2$ with no common sheet near the origin.
5. (V9.5) $|\mathrm{supp}(\Theta)|$ is locally closed in $|X|$.
6. (V9.6) $\Theta$ is closed or compact if $|\mathrm{supp}(\Theta)|$ is closed or compact in $|X|$.
7. (V9.7) At every point of the support there are symmetric local branching structures inside every neighbourhood.
8. (V9.8) $\Theta$ is of orbifold type if its values are $0$ and $1$, of manifold type if moreover there is at most one morphism between two points of its support.
9. (V9.9) The dimensional decomposition $\Theta = \sum_k \Theta_k$ exists and is unique among local decompositions; the uniqueness among all decompositions, as stated, does not hold as we read it.
10. (V9.10) The dimensional decompositions of two local branching structures agree near the point.
11. (V9.11) $\mathrm{Gr}(X)$ is the category of formal rational sums of tangent sets at smooth points, with the push-forward by morphisms.
12. (V9.12) $T_yM$ is the set of tangent vectors fixed by the tangent of the retraction; in a chart it is a linear subspace of the dimension of $M$.
13. (V9.13) If the tangent sums do not depend on the structure, $T_\Theta$ is a section functor of $\mathrm{Gr}(X)$ with the text's (1) and (2).
14. (V9.14) The tangent sums do not depend on the structure.
15. (V9.15) A point is good if near it all sheets through it coincide, equivalently if $\Theta$ is constant on the support near it.
16. (V9.16) Being a good point does not depend on the local branching structure.
17. (V9.17) Good points are good for every structure, and near a good point $\Theta$ is a multiple of the indicator of one submanifold.
18. (V9.18) $s(\varphi)$ is good iff $t(\varphi)$ is good.
19. (V9.19) The good and bad sets are saturated subsets of the support.
20. (V9.20) The good set is open and dense in the support, the bad set closed and nowhere dense.
21. (V9.21) Every sheet of an $n$-dimensional structure at $x$ contains good points converging to $x$.
22. (V9.22) Every finite-dimensional submanifold is locally a graph over a cone of its tangent space, and each class of sheets with one tangent space at $x$ has finite slices near $x$ whose $\Theta$-values sum to the weights of the class.
